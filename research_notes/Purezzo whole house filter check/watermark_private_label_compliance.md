## 1. WaterMark scope: is a whole-house filter "WaterMark required", which specification applies, what a certificate covers

### Takeaway
Yes. A whole-house (point-of-entry, POE) drinking-water filter like the PWF-203BC (sediment + carbon block) is on the WaterMark **Schedule of Products** under "Water filters and water treatment appliances". It must be certified to **AS 3497 (2021) Drinking water treatment systems – Design and performance requirements**, and lead-free requirements apply. Wetted materials must also meet AS/NZS 4020. The old AS/NZS 3497:1998 and the ATS 5200 numbering are no longer the listed specification for this product type; the residual "WMTS-103" spec covers only non-drinking-water treatment.

### Cited Findings

Specification: "AS 3497 Drinking water treatment systems – Design and performance requirements", Year 2021, Lead Free Applies: "Yes". ### Inferences
- (Inference) The PWF-203BC has a 20" pleated sediment stage and a CTO (chlorine/taste/odour) carbon block, plumbed in at the point of entry, so it sits squarely in the "Water filters and water treatment appliances" POE line. It needs WaterMark certification to **AS 3497:2021** (plus AS/NZS 4020 for wetted materials). It is not on the excluded list.
- (Inference) The brief mentions "ATS 5200 series" and "AS/NZS 3497 (1998)". Those are outdated for this product type. The current Schedule names AS 3497 (2021) only. A supplier certificate that cites AS/NZS 3497:1998 or ATS 5200.xxx for this product should be checked for currency: a certificate issued before the 2021 spec was listed may lapse at renewal, and the 12-month rule for adopting amended specifications applies (see Q2).
- (Inference) "Lead Free Applies: Yes" matters for this unit. The three pressure gauges, bypass and 1-inch port fittings are commonly brass. Any copper-alloy wetted part must meet the NCC lead-free limits. From 1 May 2026, new certifications must be lead-free. From 1 May 2028, non-lead-free certified stock is no longer authorised for installation. Stock bought in late 2026 under an old, non-lead-free certificate would have a short legal installation window, so ask Purezzo for lead-free conformity evidence.

## 2. Private labelling: can the buyer's logo ride on Purezzo's certificate, who must be the licensee, and what it costs and takes to get the buyer's brand covered

### Takeaway
Under the WaterMark rules the WaterMark is applied by an **Approved User (licensee)** to products listed on *its* certificate. The marking must show "the Approved User's name, brand or trademark", the WaterMark, the licence number and the spec number. A buyer-branded unit is therefore compliant only if (a) the buyer's brand/model is listed on the certificate (as a brand name under the existing licensee's WMCC, or on the buyer's own WMCC), and (b) the cover markings match the licence. A unit carrying the buyer's logo plus a "WaterMark" logo whose certificate does not list that brand/model is not an authorised product. Importers and distributors can be licensees in their own right. IAPMO requires the manufacturer's written authorisation, accepts current traceable existing test reports, and quotes "a few months to 12 months plus". Official fees are modest next to testing and audit costs.

### Cited Findings
**Who can be the licensee**
- Definition: "Approved User — A manufacturer, assembler, distributor, retailer or importer of a product, or an agent thereof, who entered into an Approved User Agreement with and has been granted a WaterMark Licence by an Approved Certifier for use of the WaterMark in relation to a certified product. An Approved User may also be referred to as a Licensee." — [WaterMark Manual 2016.1, s1.3](https://abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/Manual-for-WaterMark-certification-scheme.pdf)

- Approved User obligations (s7.8.1): ensure the product "meets, and continues to meet, the requirements on which the certification is based… including: a) compliance with the Scheme Rules and this Manual; b) compliance with the conditions of use of the WaterMark and requirements for an annual declaration; and c) payment of all fees by the due dates." — [WaterMark Manual 2016.1](https://abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/Manual-for-WaterMark-certification-scheme.pdf)

### Inferences
- (Inference) The supplier's "(water mark)" note does **not** by itself make a buyer-logo unit legal. Section 9.6.3 requires the brand on the product to be the Approved User's (licensee's) name, brand or trademark. A buyer logo can be lawful in two ways:
  - **Route A, brand endorsement on Purezzo's licence (or that of whoever holds it).** The licensee asks its CAB to endorse the buyer's brand name and a model name on its WMCC schedule (the EBOS schedule shows the "Brand Name" and "Date Endorsed" columns). The cover then carries the buyer's brand, WaterMark, licence number and AS 3497. Cost is an endorsement fee plus perhaps a marking review. It is likely faster than a new certification, because no new type tests are needed if the product is identical, but no CAB publishes a turnaround. The buyer then depends on Purezzo keeping the licence alive (annual surveillance and fees) and stays at Purezzo's mercy if the licence lapses or is suspended.
  - **Route B, the buyer's own licence.** The buyer becomes the Approved User. This needs written manufacturer authorisation (IAPMO 5.7), Purezzo's factory audited, type tests accepted (existing reports if current, traceable and from a recognised lab) and a marking proposal accepted. Expect months (IAPMO: "a few months to 12 months plus"). It gives control and portability (a transfer counts as a new application, so a buyer-held licence is the only one the buyer truly owns). The buyer takes on annual fees, the annual declaration, records, surveillance and recall responsibility.
- (Inference) Whichever route is used, the Approved User must "demonstrate effective control over… branding". So the CAB will expect the logo artwork, Scope of Use and installation instructions to go through a marking proposal before the 64 units are made. Making the units before the brand is endorsed risks a non-compliant batch.
- (Inference) The WaterMark rules don't strictly bar an overseas licensee (the Rules contemplate foreign-made goods, the form asks for an ABN only for Australian companies, and the agreement accepts an "ABN/ARBN"). The buyer should still confirm with the CAB who the Australian point of contact is for recalls and surveillance samples.

## 3. Verifying Purezzo's WaterMark claim (database fields, red flags, searches for "Purezzo" and "Water Analytics")

### Takeaway

## 4. Plumbing law by state: can a licensed plumber install a non-WaterMark POE filter, penalties, and who bears them

### Takeaway
No. Every state and territory applies the PCA by law, and a POE drinking-water filter is a Schedule-of-Products item, so installing an uncertified one is non-compliant plumbing work everywhere. This is verified explicitly for QLD (statute) and VIC (regulator statement); it is inferred for the others from the national scheme. The **plumber** is directly liable: offences, licence discipline, rectification. **Queensland goes further**: its non-conforming building product (NCBP) law puts importers and suppliers in the "chain of responsibility", with fines up to 1,000 penalty units (A$172,700 at the 1 July 2026 value) for breaching the duty. Elsewhere, selling an uncertified product was historically not an offence in itself (industry submission, 2007). A false WaterMark representation still exposes the seller under the ACL. No state keeps its own filter register; the national WaterMark database is the register. WELS does not cover filters. I found no filter-specific backflow rule.

## 5. Consumer law, product liability, insurance, warranty norms and recalls

### Takeaway
By putting its own logo on the unit, the buyer becomes a "manufacturer" under ACL s7. The importer limb applies too (the Chinese maker has no Australian place of business). The buyer therefore carries non-excludable consumer-guarantee liability (claims against manufacturers within 3 years) and safety-defect liability, including damage to other property (ss138–141), plus mandatory injury reporting within 2 days. The classic loss is escape of water from a burst housing or fitting. As a benchmark, Suncorp put the average burst flexi-hose claim above A$27,500 (2025). Online P&PL cover for small importers/wholesalers starts around A$250–450/yr for A$5–20m. Plumbers' public liability is about A$546–1,930/yr (median ~A$1,070). An importer-installer of mains-pressure products should expect underwriting questions. The category warranty norm is **10 years** (Puretec whole-house WH/EM/FilterWall: 1 yr parts & labour + 9 yrs parts; consumables excluded). No Australian recall of whole-house filter housings was found; the official recall search was blocked (403).

## 7. Checklist and realistic path from quote to legally installable branded stock

### Takeaway
(Inference, built from the findings above.) Fastest legal path:
1. Verify licence 080071 (or whatever Purezzo cites) in the live database. Confirm holder, CAB, AS 3497, lead-free status and whether PWF-203BC itself is listed.
2. Have the licensee endorse the buyer's brand and model, with an accepted marking proposal, **before** the factory applies the buyer's logo.
3. Then produce and ship.

If the 20" three-gauge cabinet / pleated-cartridge build is not the certified family, extra CAB testing or endorsement is needed first. A buyer-owned licence is the slow, controllable route: months, with IAPMO quoting "a few months to 12 months plus". Official ABCB charges are small (A$525 per licence per year). Real costs are CAB application, audit and endorsement fees plus lab tests, and none are published, so get written quotes.

### Cited Findings
- Steps and preconditions: accredited type testing, factory QMS audit at each location, accepted marking proposal, Scope of Use, licence issue, annual surveillance. — [WaterMark Manual 2016.1, s8.5, 9.3, 9.6](https://abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/Manual-for-WaterMark-certification-scheme.pdf)
- Non-manufacturer applicants need the manufacturer's written authorisation. Existing reports are accepted if current, traceable and from a recognised lab. Endorsement fees apply for adding models or changing a licence. — [IAPMO POL-100](https://iapmooceania.org/media/a5dfckvx/pol-100-wmk-governance-rules.pdf)
- Time: "a few months to 12 months plus" for type testing. — [IAPMO Oceania](https://iapmooceania.org/certification-services/watermark-product-certification)
- ABCB licence fee A$525 per licence (pass-through). — [IAPMO POL-100 s4.3(d)](https://iapmooceania.org/media/a5dfckvx/pol-100-wmk-governance-rules.pdf); [NoD 2025/1.0 (indexed)](https://www.nccb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2025/2025-1.0%20NoD%20-%20Amended%20fees%20and%20charges_0.pdf)

**C. Realistic sequence (rough order; durations only where sourced)**
1. Days 0–2: the buyer manually checks the WaterMark DB (080071, PWF-203BC, Purezzo, AQUAPRIMUS, Water Analytics). Hold the deposit until this is done.
2. Purezzo confirms the licence, scope and lead-free status, then lodges the brand/model endorsement and marking proposal with its CAB. Duration not published. Likely shorter than a new certification, because no new type tests are needed if the product is identical (inference).
3. The buyer's brand/model appears on the WaterMark DB. Only then should the factory produce with the logo.
4. Production, sea freight, and customs/biosecurity clearance: ChAFTA CoO, packing declaration, BMSB random-inspection exposure through 30 Apr 2027.
5. Install with licensed plumbers to AS/NZS 3500 and local backflow rules; issue compliance certificates where required. Supply Scope of Use, instructions, warranty and (in QLD) "required information".
6. Fallback (Route B, own licence): "a few months to 12 months plus" (IAPMO), plus CAB and lab costs on quote.
7. Calendar flag: products not certified lead-free lose installation authorisation on 1 May 2028.

**D. Do-not-do list**
- Don't let the factory mark the buyer's logo plus "WaterMark" before the database lists the buyer's brand.
- Don't accept a certificate in another company's name without a CAB-endorsed brand listing.
- Don't buy a "family" variant that isn't on the schedule.
- Don't sell in QLD on the strength of an unverified claim (NCBP duties, 1,000 PU).
