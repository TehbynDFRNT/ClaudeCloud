## Key Question 1: Which SKUs sell, and what are the retail floors (price to beat)?

### Takeaway
Mainstream floors: 48-inch steel dog crate about A$105 at Kmart; wooden coops and hutches A$137-306 at Bunnings; 160-200 L compost tumblers A$108-190 on Amazon AU and A$150 at Bunnings (180 L); 150-170 cm cat trees A$71-109 at Kmart; small steel raised beds only A$28-59 at Bunnings; cheapest polycarbonate walk-in found at A$1,320 (Bunnings, special order), while PE-cover walk-ins sit at A$49.99 (Aldi) and A$214.92 (Bunnings). ### Inferences
- The 48-inch steel crate is capped by Kmart's A$104.95. An in-band price of A$100 leaves almost no headroom. Pricing 10-20% under the Amazon AU snapshot figure (A$135.65, unverified) would give A$108-122, which sits above Kmart, so it is only defensible if the SKU is clearly better (for example, a heavier 20-gauge double-door spec).
- Standard raised beds are cheap at retail (A$28-59 for small sizes at Bunnings). An in-band galvanised bed has to be a large, tall or kit SKU, and no floor for that size was found.
- The lowest mainstream polycarbonate walk-in is A$1,320, which leaves a wide gap to the A$100-400 band. PE-cover walk-ins already sell at A$49.99 and A$214.92.
- Standard 150-170 cm cat trees are A$71-109 at Kmart, so an in-band price tops out near A$95-100. A premium 180 cm, five-tier tree (Petbarn A$339) leaves more room.
- 160-200 L compost tumblers sit at about A$108-190 on Amazon AU and at A$149.99 on Bunnings (180 L), so an in-band Marketplace price has to undercut those.
- Standard wooden coops and hutches have floors of A$136.90-189.95, which points to an in-band price of roughly A$120-150.

### Gaps
- No Amazon AU product page could be opened; all Amazon prices are undated search snapshots, and delivered prices are unknown.
- Not checked: Temu AU, Kogan, eBay AU importer listings, OzBargain, Aldi Special Buys (apart from the greenhouse), Dropshipzone (i.Pet), Facebook Marketplace and Gumtree asking prices.
- No Kmart or Aldi coop, no PE-cover greenhouse floor beyond the two listed, and no floors at all for strollers, orthopedic beds, playpens, outdoor kennels, steel sheds, storage boxes or garden carts.
- Sell-through and demand were not measured; that is covered by the broad Marketplace researcher.

## Key Question 2: Which named Chinese factories make them, at what FOB, MOQ and CBM?

### Takeaway
Named listings exist for 42-48-inch wire crates, galvanised and aluzinc raised beds, wooden coops and aluminium or polycarbonate hobby greenhouses. The most concrete FOB anchors are Laizhou Haotai's galvanised beds (US$25 per set at 200-499 sets, US$22.45 at 1,000+; size tier not captured) and Changzhou Growell's 6 x 8 ft polycarbonate walk-in (US$90-200 per piece, MOQ 50). Wooden coops list at US$50-150 with MOQ 10-100, which does not fit under Bunnings floors once freight is added. Dog-crate FOB is unresolved: the one listing that shows US$2-3 for a 122 cm crate is implausible and is not used. No factory data was retrieved for cat trees, compost tumblers, strollers, beds, playpens, kennels, sheds, storage boxes, carts, PE-cover walk-ins or metal coops.

## Key Question 3: Biosecurity and compliance for timber housing and wood packaging (DAFF / BICON)

### Takeaway
Timber items are routed by BICON according to dimensions. Per the BICON search snapshot, "Timber and timber products" covers pieces with at least one measurement of 200 mm or less, and items over 200 mm in every dimension go to a separate "logs, log cabins and oversize timber" case. BICON returned HTTP 403, so this is unverified. Wood packaging (crates, pallets, dunnage) must meet the ISPM 15 treatment and marking rules (DAFF snippet; fetch pending).
