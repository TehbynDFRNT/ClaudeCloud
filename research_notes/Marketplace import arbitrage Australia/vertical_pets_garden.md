## Key Question 1: Which SKUs sell, and what are the retail floors (price to beat)?

### Takeaway
Mainstream floors: 48-inch steel dog crate about A$105 at Kmart; wooden coops and hutches A$137-306 at Bunnings; 160-200 L compost tumblers A$108-190 on Amazon AU and A$149.99 at Bunnings (180 L); 150-170 cm cat trees A$71-109 at Kmart; small steel raised beds A$28-59 at Bunnings; the cheapest polycarbonate walk-in found is A$1,320 at Bunnings (special order); PE-cover walk-ins sit at A$49.99 (Aldi) and A$214.92 (Bunnings). Mainstream price caps are the binding constraint for crates, cat trees and coops. Marketplace sell-through was not measured (outside this brief).

### Inferences
- The 48-inch steel crate is capped by Kmart's A$104.95 snapshot. An in-band price of A$100 leaves almost no headroom. Pricing 10-20% under the Amazon AU snapshot figure (A$135.65, unverified) gives A$108-122, which sits above Kmart. That is only defensible if the SKU is clearly better, for example a heavier 20-gauge double-door spec.
- The retail-to-factory spread for this category is wide: Kmart's A$104.95 is about US$73, while the one Hebei wire-crate listing opened this session quotes US$4.86-6.17 FOB (for sizes up to 91 cm; see Key Question 2). Margin is therefore set by the retail cap and competition more than by factory cost.
- Standard raised beds are cheap at retail (A$28-59 for small sizes). An in-band galvanised bed would have to be a large, tall or kit SKU; no floor for that size was found.
- The lowest mainstream polycarbonate walk-in is A$1,320, which leaves a wide gap to the A$100-400 band. PE-cover walk-ins already sell at A$49.99 and A$214.92.
- Standard 150-170 cm cat trees are A$71-109 at Kmart, so an in-band price tops out near A$95-100. A premium 180 cm five-tier tree (Petbarn A$339) leaves more room.
- 160-200 L compost tumblers sit at about A$108-190 on Amazon AU and at A$149.99 on Bunnings (180 L), so an in-band Marketplace price has to undercut those.
- Standard wooden coops and hutches have floors of A$136.90-189.95, which points to an in-band price of roughly A$120-150, or A$180 at the top of the coop range.

### Gaps
- No Amazon AU product page could be opened (HTTP 500 on the product page; HTTP 503 on search). All Amazon prices are undated search snapshots; delivered prices are unknown.

## Key Question 3: Biosecurity and compliance for timber housing and wood packaging (DAFF / BICON)

### Takeaway
Biosecurity text is snippet-only. The DAFF timber, timber-packaging and ISPM 15 pages returned HTTP 503 on every attempt (five requests in total), and the BICON case page returned HTTP 403. From the search snapshot, BICON routes timber by dimension: "Timber and timber products" covers pieces with at least one measurement of 200 mm or less, and items over 200 mm in every dimension go to a separate "logs, log cabins and oversize timber" case. A timber coop of about 1.5 m is therefore likely to be in the second case, though this is unverified. Wood packaging must meet ISPM 15 treatment and marking (snippet). Treatment certificate costs, inspection fees and delay times were not established. For pet beds with natural fillings, no commercial-import rule was found; DAFF personal-effects guidance flags animal beds, animal hair and plant-based fillings, so BICON should be checked per filling.
