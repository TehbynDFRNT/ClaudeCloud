## Q1. Electrical compliance: EESS levels, Responsible Supplier, certificate costs, certificate re-use, ACMA radio/EMC/cyber, GEMS, SGG refrigerant licences

### Takeaway
Almost every mains-powered product in this vertical is EESS **Level 3** (Australian/NZ certificate of conformity + per-model, per-importer database registration + RCM): air fryers, espresso machines, stand mixers, vacuum cleaners (incl. a robot's mains-powered self-empty base), room heaters, electric blankets, fans, R290 portable ACs, mains massagers and **every battery charger/power supply** — so cordless products (stick vacs, robot vacs, mowers, e-mobility) still carry a Level 3 charger. A factory's existing certificate can only be used with the certificate holder's permission and each importer must still register the model itself; generic "no-cert" stock needs new Australian certification (regulator fee alone A$641–1,280 per model, plus lab testing — no published lab prices found). The genuinely light-touch items are **portable evaporative coolers (Level 1)** and (inferred) **electric pressure washers (not on the Level 2/3 list)**. Portable ACs add GEMS MEPS registration and refrigerant rules (R410A banned since 1 Jul 2024; R32 needs an A$3,000 SGG equipment licence; R290 avoids the licence but becomes Level 3).

### Cited Findings
**Fees and obligations**
- EESS fees "from 1 July 2026": Responsible Supplier registration **A$239.74/yr**; Level 2 or 3 equipment registration **A$89.85 (1 yr), A$179.70 (2 yr), A$449.25 (5 yr)**; Level 3 registration cannot outlast the current Certificate of Conformity; GST-free — [EESS Registration Fees](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-fees/) (earlier 2025-26 figures were A$231.91 and A$86.91–434.56 — [EESS via search summary](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-fees/))
- In-scope = rated >50 V AC or >120 V ripple-free DC (and <1000 V AC/1500 V DC), household/personal use. **Level 3**: electrically safe, RCM-marked, "a valid Australian or New Zealand issued certificate of conformity", registered on the national EESS database and linked to the registered responsible supplier. **Level 2**: registration + compliance folder producible within 10 days. **Level 1**: RCM + English documentary evidence + linked to a registered responsible supplier — [EESS in-scope equipment definitions & risk levels v4.2 (aligns to AS/NZS 4417.2:2020)](https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/EESS-Inscope-Equipment-Definitions-and-Risk-Levels-v4.2-.pdf)
- NSW Fair Trading (a certifier for declared/Level 3 articles) fees, "July 2025 to June 2026" (page last updated 1 Jul 2026): application for model approval **Category 1 A$641; Category 2 A$1,280**; extend A$641; renew A$641; modify A$481; notice of change A$157 (+A$46 per extra approval); Recognised External Approval Scheme initial application A$4,952 + A$214/hr assessment — [NSW electricity safety fees](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/selling-goods-and-services/electricity-safety-fees)

**Risk level by product** (read from the 3/2/1 column of the EESS v4.2 document — [EESS v4.2](https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/EESS-Inscope-Equipment-Definitions-and-Risk-Levels-v4.2-.pdf))
- **Level 3**: Air conditioner incorporating flammable refrigerant (ISO 817 class 2/3, e.g. R290; AS/NZS 60335.2.40); Blanket (>0.6 m², incl. controller; AS/NZS 60335.2.17); Cooking appliance—portable type (griller/roaster/oven AS/NZS 60335.2.9; frying pan/deep fryer/wok 60335.2.13) → air fryers; Fan (incl. fans that blow a water mist); Kitchen machine (food preparation by mechanical means; AS/NZS 60335.2.14) → stand mixers; Lawn care appliance (mower 60335.2.77, robotic mower 60335.2.107); Liquid heating appliance (portable, ≤10 L, hot beverage/cooking; 60335.2.15) → espresso machines; Massage appliance (household, portable, self-contained); Power supply or charger (household type for charging batteries or supplying separate equipment; battery charger AS/NZS 60335.2.29); Room heater (60335.2.30) → oil-column/panel heaters; Tool—portable type (hand-held motor tools, excludes lawn & garden machinery); Vacuum cleaner (household, portable; 60335.2.2)
- **Level 1 exception**: "Portable evaporative coolers are level 1 as they are not fixed devices" (only fixed evaporative coolers are Level 3)
- **Level 2**: Air conditioner incorporating non-flammable or low-flammability refrigerant (ISO 817 class 1 or 2L → R410A, R32); Heating pad—flexible; Floor polisher; Projector
- WA Building & Energy's Level 3 extract of AS/NZS 4417.2 Table B.4 (62 items) names the same items and does **not** name pressure washers — [WA Building and Energy](https://www.wa.gov.au/sites/default/files/announcements/electrical-appliances-and-equipment-importing-selling-or-hiring)

**Certificate re-use / transfer**
- A certificate holder is not automatically a responsible supplier; equipment must still be registered by a Responsible Supplier and RCM-marked; only certificates uploaded to the EESS Certification Database can be used; certificate holders can lock certificates with a password; "It is an expectation that Responsible Suppliers have a working relationship with certificate holders" — [EESS Certification (General)](https://eess.gov.au/safety-requirements/certification-general) (via search summary)
- The Level 3 equipment declaration states "The Responsible Supplier has permission to use this certificate for this registration from the certificate holder" and that it "will ensure the certificate remains current for the life of this registration" — [EESS Responsible Supplier Declarations](https://www.eess.gov.au/responsible-supplier/responsible-supplier-declarations/) (via search summary)
- Real example: Phihong (power-supply maker) issues letters authorising Australian responsible supplier World Tech Trading Pty Ltd to use Phihong's Certificate of Conformity for EESS registration; letters carry expiry dates and commit the maker to keep the product unchanged, bear recall/replacement costs and name AU importers on request — [Phihong letter RCMPOE60S](https://www.phihong.com/wp-content/uploads/RCMPOE60S-1BT-EESS-A1.pdf); [Phihong letter RCMAA10S](https://www.phihong.com/wp-content/uploads/RCMAA10S-050A-EESS-A1.pdf)
- "Imported Level 3 products must be registered by each importer (must be in Australia/NZ), even if another importer has registered the same product"; overseas entities cannot be Responsible Suppliers — [Energy Safe Victoria FAQ](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/industry-guidance/electrical/frequently-asked-questions/electrical-equipment-safety-and-efficiency) (via search summary)

**ACMA radio/EMC and cyber (robot vacuums, smart appliances)**
- ACMA duties: arrange testing, register as responsible supplier on the national database, keep compliance records, label before supply; ACMA-only registration has no fee; ACMA "levels" do not correspond to EESS levels — [ACMA "Know what you must do"](https://www.acma.gov.au/know-what-you-must-do); [EESS "ACMA requirements – use of RCM"](https://www.eess.gov.au/rcm/acma-requirements-use-of-rcm/) (both via sibling notes au_landed_cost_compliance.md)
- Wi-Fi/Bluetooth radio standard is AS/NZS 4268 (adopted by the Radiocommunications (Short Range Devices) Standard 2014); EMC evidence (e.g. CISPR 32/EN 55032) and an ACMA Supplier's Declaration of Conformity kept on file — [Raspberry Pi 5 Australian SDoC](https://pip.raspberrypi.com/categories/894-approvals/documents/RP-005012-CF/rpi-5-Australia_SDoC_LoA.pdf); [SolaX ACMA form C02](https://at.solaxpower.com/uploads/file/c02-suppliers-declaration-of-conformity-jan-2018-en.pdf)
- Cyber Security (Security Standards for Smart Devices) Rules 2025 apply from 4 March 2026 to consumer connectable products (statement of compliance; devices made before 4 Mar 2026 exempt) — [Rules F2025L00276](https://legislation.gov.au/F2025L00276/asmade/2025-03-04/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html); [Home Affairs](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/about-us/our-portfolios/cyber-security/security-standards-for-smart-devices) (via sibling notes)

**GEMS (portable air conditioners) and refrigerant (SGG) rules**
- GEMS Determination 2019 for air conditioners introduced the SEER-based Zoned Energy Rating Label and "applied MEPS to single duct portable air conditioners for the first time" — [UL summary](https://www.ul.com/news/new-greenhouse-and-energy-minimum-standard-gems-determination-2019-air-conditioners). GEMS products must be registered before sale; air conditioners are fee band 3 = **A$670 per registration** (renewal same) — [GEMS registration payment help](https://reg.energyrating.gov.au/application/help/payment/) (undated); same band in [2019 fees instrument](https://legislation.gov.au/F2019L01065/asmade/2019-08-15/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html). Four AC registrations were cancelled in Feb 2024 and ACs are a primary 2024-25 compliance-monitoring focus — [GEMS 2024-25 compliance program](https://www.energyrating.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-07/2024-25_gems_compliance_monitoring_program.pdf)
- From 1 July 2024 import/manufacture of small (≤2.6 kg charge) AC equipment using HFCs with GWP >750 is prohibited, **explicitly including portable air conditioners**, even if imported uncharged; multi-head units added 1 July 2025; stock imported before 1 Jul 2024 may still be sold — [National EPA](https://www.nationalepa.gov.au/node/264) (via search summary; page returned 503 on fetch); [FTA/ABF notice 2024/24](https://ftalliance.com.au/newsdetails/32286). R32 (GWP 675) and R290 (GWP <5) remain importable — [WTO import-licensing summary](https://importlicensing.wto.org/content/ozone-depleting-substances-and-synthetic-greenhouse-gases-1)
- Equipment pre-charged with HFCs (incl. air conditioners) needs an equipment licence; application fee **A$3,000**, non-refundable (2025-26) — [ABLIS pre-charged equipment licence](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/ag/pre-charged-equipment-licence/251). Exemption thresholds conflict (≤5 pieces and <10 kg per consignment per ABLIS vs ≤25 kg SGG per calendar year per WTO summary); licences valid 2 years per UNEP — [WTO](https://importlicensing.wto.org/content/ozone-depleting-substances-and-synthetic-greenhouse-gases-1); [UNEP presentation](https://ozone.unep.org/system/files/documents/Sessions%204%20and%205-1-%20Australia%27s%20licensing%20system.pdf)

- **Implication for buying generic units:** a Marketplace importer cannot legally rely on a factory's RCM/SAA certificate unless the holder authorises it in writing (and the certificate is uploaded/unlocked on the EESS database); even then the importer must register the model in its own name every year. A factory that "has SAA" may hold a certificate issued to (or controlled by) a *different* AU importer — that certificate is only usable if its holder authorises you. Buying "generic" no-cert stock means paying for Australian certification per model, which on a 100–500-unit first order adds A$10–50/unit (see Q6).
- Scenario B costs are **my estimates** anchored only on the published NSW regulator fee (A$641–1,280) plus an assumed lab/review cost; real quotes could be lower if the factory supplies a full IECEE CB test report with AU national differences, or higher if retesting is needed.

## Q2. Lithium batteries and e-mobility: DG shipping, NSW and other state rules, ACCC action, e-bike/e-scooter rules, realism for a small importer

### Takeaway
E-bikes and e-scooters are not realistic for a small Marketplace importer: NSW has required separate certification + approval marks for each vehicle model, its battery and its charger since 1 Feb 2026 (marks must also appear in online listings; penalties to A$825,000), Victoria is consulting on similar rules, Queensland is tightening rider rules (16+ with licence from 31 Aug 2026), and the ACCC is developing a national standard (2026-27 priority). On top, packs >100 Wh are full Class 9 dangerous goods. Legal 250 W pedelecs also sit far above the A$100–400 band (Amazon AU e-bikes ~A$1,490+). Smaller lithium products (robot/stick vacs, massage guns, mowers) carry a DG paperwork/surcharge burden but are importable.

### Cited Findings
- NSW (page last updated 18 Feb 2026): covered products are e-micromobility vehicles declared electrical articles under the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2017 — certain e-bikes, e-scooters, e-skateboards, self-balancing scooters, plus their Li-ion batteries and chargers. Accepted standards: e-bikes ≤500 W: AS 15194:2016, EN 15194:2017+A1:2023 or UL 2849:2022 (>500 W: UL 2849); e-bike batteries EN 50604-1, IEC 62133-2:2017 or UL 2271:2023 (UL 2271:2018 only until 1 Feb 2027); e-scooters/e-skateboards/self-balancing: AS/NZS 60335.2.114:2023, EN 17128:2020, UL 2272:2024 (UL 2272:2016 until 1 Feb 2027); chargers AS/NZS 61558 or AS/NZS 60335.2.29. Battery, charger and vehicle **each** need a Certificate of Approval and safety approval mark on the product; changing battery or charger creates a new model; testing by NATA or recognised international lab (e.g. CNAS); fines up to A$825,000; Information Standard breaches up to A$5,500 each — [NSW: selling or renting out e-bikes and other e-micromobility products](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/safety-home/electrical-safety/lithium-ion-battery-safety/new-standards-for-lithium-ion-batteries-e-micromobility-devices)
- "The safety approval mark must be included on every website where an e-micromobility vehicle is listed"; the page does not say whether one supplier can use another's certificate — [NSW supplying e-micromobility products](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/selling-goods-and-services/supplying-e-micromobility-products)
- Dates conflict across sources: earlier NSW material said mandatory testing/certification from Aug 2025 and labelling from Feb 2026 — [NSW ministerial release](https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/nation-leading-safety-and-information-standards-for-lithium-ion-battery-products-now-effect) (via search summary); a Statement of Regulatory Intent says certification commencement was moved to 1 Feb 2026 — [NSW SRI](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/fair-trading/how-we-regulate/statements-of-regulatory-intent/statement-of-regulatory-intent-e-micromobility-vehicles) (via sibling notes)
- Victoria: Energy Safe Victoria consulted (Aug–Oct 2025) on declaring e-bikes, e-scooters, e-skateboards and their batteries "controlled electrical equipment" (certification before supply); submissions published Jan 2026; no final rule found — [ESV consultation](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/media-centre/news/public-consultation-open-proposal-strengthen-e-transport-device-safety-requirements) (via sibling notes); [ESV submission (Bicycle Network)](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-01/ESV-battery-safety-submission-Bicycle-Network.pdf)
- Queensland (page updated 26 Aug 2026): PMDs (e-scooters) max 25 km/h device speed, 12 km/h on footpaths, max 60 kg; "New age and licensing rules apply from 31 August 2026" — riders must be 16+ and hold a driver licence (learner OK); no registration/insurance needed — [Qld personal mobility devices](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/safety/rules/wheeled-devices/personal-mobility-devices). Qld government accepted (or in principle) all 28 recommendations of the e-mobility inquiry, incl. national mandatory Li-ion battery standards and aligning with EN 15194 — [Bicycle Network, 23 Mar 2026](https://bicyclenetwork.com.au/newsroom/2026/03/23/queensland-inquiry-recommends-sweeping-e-mobility-reform/) (via search summary)
- Federal/ACCC: e-micromobility is one of the ACCC's four 2026-27 product-safety priorities (develop nationally consistent standards for e-bikes first, covering power limits and Li-ion battery safety); 2026-27 Budget: A$6.6m over three years for ACCC and Treasury — [Maddocks](https://www.maddocks.com.au/insights/e-bikes-e-scooters-and-e-commerce-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-acccs-2026-27-product-safety-priorities); [Clayton Utz](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2026/june/accc-launches-202627-product-safety-priorities) (via search summaries). On 6 Aug 2026 Assistant Minister Andrew Leigh said the ACCC is tasked with a national e-bike standard informed by EN 15194 — [Treasury transcript](https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/andrew-leigh-2025/transcripts/doorstop-interview-federation-mall-canberra) (via sibling notes)
- Infrastructure ministers agreed "the Commonwealth will reinstate the EN-15194 standard", applying to "the importation, sale and road use of e-bikes across Australia" (article 3 Dec 2025) — [Bicycle Network](https://bicyclenetwork.com.au/newsroom/2025/12/03/feds-return-to-euro-e-bike-standard/)
- E-bike rules per a retailer blog (9 Feb 2026, not an official source): max continuous rated power 250 W; motor cuts out at 25 km/h; in NSW throttles that move the bike without pedalling above 6 km/h are illegal; NSW transitioning from 500 W to 250 W; WA riders must be 16+; converted e-bikes banned from Sydney Trains/NSW TrainLink/Metro from 1 Nov 2025 — [Reid Cycles](https://www.reidcycles.com.au/blogs/news/ebike-laws-in-australia-february-2026-update)
- Li-ion batteries are Class 9 DG (UN3480 alone; UN3481 packed with/contained in equipment); sea DG needs an Australian "MO41" declaration prepared by a qualified person — [BWSC 2027 Freight Manual](https://assets.worldsolarchallenge.org/app/uploads/2026/06/10154922/2027_BWSC_Freight_Manual_V2.0-Published-10062026.pdf) (via sibling notes). FedEx: IDG surcharge applies to Section I lithium shipments; Section II (small batteries in equipment) none — [FedEx AU lithium guide](https://www.fedex.com/en-au/shipping-guide/pack/lithium-batteries/ion-batteries/contained-in-equipment/power-rating-greater-than-100wh/qty-less-than-equal-to-5kg.html)
- Factory DG documentation exists for small items: Shenzhen Pentasmart and ZM Electronics massage-gun pages list UN38.3 and MSDS — [Pentasmart](https://pentasmart.en.made-in-china.com/product/NEGUAYpVVchD/China-Handheld-Two-Heads-Fascial-Massage-Gun-Portable-Mini-Deep-Muscle-Massager.html); [ZM Electronics](https://zmesz2022.en.made-in-china.com/product/gxdRPQoChUWv/China-Electric-Percussion-Relaxing-Muscle-CE-Certification-Body-Massage-Gun.html)
- Price reality: Amazon AU e-bike best-sellers are A$1,490–2,299 for complete bikes (e.g. NCM Paris 250 W 36 V 15 Ah A$1,499) — [Amazon AU best sellers: Electric Bicycles](https://www.amazon.com.au/gp/bestsellers/sporting-goods/5044902051); e-scooters: iScooter 350 W 25 km/h A$399.99, Lenoxx 350 W A$418.95, a 130 W 16 km/h unit A$199.95, Razor E90 kids A$299.99 — [Amazon AU best sellers: Scooters](https://www.amazon.com.au/gp/bestsellers/sporting-goods/5044429051)

### Inferences
- **Realism:** for a 100–200-unit e-scooter order, three NSW certifications (vehicle/battery/charger) at ≈A$18.8k (my estimate) add ≈A$94/unit — enough to flip a 19.5% margin to −8.6% (Q6). Selling only outside NSW avoids NSW certification but not ACL/deemed-manufacturer fire liability, DG freight, or listing visibility into NSW; and rider-legality varies by state (Qld legal with age/licence limits; NSW status not verified). Score: not viable for a beginner.
- **E-bikes fail the brief on price alone:** no legal 250 W pedelec in Australia retails near A$400; the cheapest EN 15194-claiming FOB offers (US$280–339) land at ≈A$640+ before any certification.
- **DG cost model used in Q6 (assumption):** A$750 per shipment for small Li-ion in equipment (robot/stick vacs, massage guns) and A$1,500 per shipment for >100 Wh packs (mowers, e-scooters, e-bikes). Treat as placeholders until forwarder quotes.

## Q4. Warranty, returns and defect risk under the ACL; product-liability insurance loading for electricals

### Takeaway
The importer of these goods is the ACL "deemed manufacturer" and the seller of record, so it must itself remedy failures (refund/replace/repair) and carries injury/fire liability; electrical heat/battery products (heaters, blankets, air fryers, chargers, e-mobility) are the riskiest categories in the whole Marketplace brief. No electrical-specific defect-rate or premium data was found; I model 4–10% warranty allowances by product and A$1,500/yr (A$3,000 for e-mobility) liability cover as labelled assumptions.

### Cited Findings
- An onshore importer of goods made offshore can be the "deemed manufacturer"; consumers can claim against foreign manufacturer and AU importer concurrently — [LegalVision](https://legalvision.com.au/legal-considerations-of-importers-under-australian-consumer-law); [HWL Ebsworth](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/the-australian-consumer-law-suppliers-and-manufacturers-who-is-ultimately-liable-to-consumers-for-defective-goods/) (via sibling notes)
- Sellers must provide the remedy and "can't refuse to help... or tell them to contact the manufacturer" — [ACCC parallel imports](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/buying-parallel-imports) (via sibling notes; ACCC pages returned 403 to me)
- Importer is deemed manufacturer when the maker has no AU place of business, "which can raise insurance quotes" — [Finder](https://www.finder.com.au/business-insurance-for-importers); public liability averages ≈A$39/month for low-risk small businesses — [Upcover](https://www.upcover.com/blog/public-liability-insurance-for-small-business) (via sibling notes)
- Quality signal for cheap electricals: CHOICE rated Kmart's A$169 Anko robot vacuum 36% (lowest in its test) — [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/home-and-living/laundry-and-cleaning/vacuum-cleaners/articles/should-you-buy-kmarts-anko-robot-vacuum)
- NSW penalties up to A$825,000 for non-compliant e-micromobility products — [NSW](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/safety-home/electrical-safety/lithium-ion-battery-safety/new-standards-for-lithium-ion-batteries-e-micromobility-devices)
- GEMS Regulator cancels non-compliant AC registrations (4 in Feb 2024) — [GEMS 2024-25 compliance program](https://www.energyrating.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-07/2024-25_gems_compliance_monitoring_program.pdf)

### Inferences
- Warranty/defect allowances used in Q6 (assumptions): heaters/coolers 4%; air fryer, mixer, blanket 5%; espresso, portable AC, pressure washer, mower, massagers 6%; robot/stick vacuums and massage chair 8% (motors, batteries, firmware, app support); e-bikes/e-scooters 10%.
- Insurance: A$1,500/yr for an electrical-goods importer (≈3× the cited low-risk public-liability average; assumption) and A$3,000/yr for e-mobility; some insurers may decline uncertified or lithium-heavy product lines.
- Warranty friction is worse on Marketplace than via retail: the seller must personally handle failures for whatever period the ACL's acceptable-quality/durability test implies (which can run beyond any stated warranty) with no spare-parts channel; robot vacuums and espresso machines generate after-sales queries (app pairing, descaling, pump/seal failures).

### Gaps
- No category defect/return rates for small appliances, and no importer-specific premiums or "electrical loading" percentages were found; ACL statute and recall data (productsafety.gov.au, 403) not read.

## Q6. Per-SKU unit economics, compliance blockers, verdict scores and ranking

### Takeaway
**Compliance cost and risk do kill the margin for a small importer in this vertical.** With an existing, authorised Australian certificate (Scenario A) only five lines clear ~15% gross margin at 10–20% under Amazon AU — **electric pressure washer (17–30%), cordless stick vacuum at A$89 with a 900-unit MOQ (22%), dual-basket air fryer at container volume (20%), espresso at A$109 (15%), and e-scooter outside NSW (19–21%)** — and once Australian certification has to be bought per model (Scenario B) every mains-powered Level 3 appliance drops to ≤9%, while robot vacuums, stand mixers, portable ACs, mowers, heaters, blankets and all massagers are loss-making even before certification. Best (still modest) pick: the **electric pressure washer** (Level 1, A$13–18k cash, ≈A$21–32 GP/unit across scenarios) — contingent on verifying that Bunnings/Aldi don't sell comparable units near A$99.

- **Bottom line for the report:** for a small importer the vertical is mostly a no-go: (1) Amazon AU/Kmart floors already sit at ≈1.2–1.7× FOB, (2) Level 3 certification per model adds A$10–50/unit on first orders, (3) compact appliances lose Marketplace's bulky-goods/pickup advantage, and (4) heat/battery products carry outsized ACL and fire liability. If pursued at all: a Level 1, mid-bulk product (pressure washer; possibly evaporative cooler in dry-climate cities) with a factory that can hand over test reports, ordered as a single 20GP.
