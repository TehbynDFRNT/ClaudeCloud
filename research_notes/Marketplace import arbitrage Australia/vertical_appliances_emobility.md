# Vertical deep-dive: Home appliances, electronics and e-mobility (import from China, sell new on Facebook Marketplace AU at A$100-400)

_Status: work in progress (incremental save). All prices observed 2026-10-09 unless stated._

## Q1. Electrical compliance (EESS levels, Responsible Supplier, certificates, certificate re-use, ACMA, GEMS, refrigerant licences)

### Takeaway
(draft) Almost every mains-powered product in this vertical is EESS **Level 3** (certificate of conformity + per-model registration), and battery products whose body is extra-low-voltage still drag in a Level 3 charger/power supply. Portable evaporative coolers and (probably) pressure washers are the main exceptions.

### Cited Findings
- EESS fees "from 1 July 2026": Responsible Supplier registration **$239.74 per year**; Level 2 or 3 equipment registration **$89.85 (1 yr), $179.70 (2 yr), $449.25 (5 yr)**; Level 3 registration cannot outlast the current Certificate of Conformity; fees GST-free — [EESS Registration Fees](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-fees/)
- In-scope equipment = rated >50 V AC RMS or >120 V ripple-free DC (and <1000 V AC/1500 V DC), designed or marketed for household/personal use. Level 3 must be electrically safe, RCM-marked, hold a **valid Australian or New Zealand issued certificate of conformity**, and be registered on the national EESS database linked to the registered responsible supplier; Level 2 = registration + compliance folder producible within 10 days; Level 1 = RCM + documentary evidence in English + linked to a registered responsible supplier — [EESS In-scope equipment definitions & risk levels v4.2 (aligns to AS/NZS 4417.2:2020)](https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/EESS-Inscope-Equipment-Definitions-and-Risk-Levels-v4.2-.pdf)
- Risk levels read from the same EESS document (column position of the "X" against the 3/2/1 header):
  - **Level 3**: Air conditioner incorporating flammable refrigerant (ISO 817 class 2 or 3, e.g. R290; AS/NZS 60335.2.40); Blanket (AS/NZS 60335.2.17, incl. associated controller); Cooking appliance—portable type (household, cooks/warms food, portable: griller/roaster/oven AS/NZS 60335.2.9; frying pan/deep fryer/wok 60335.2.13); Fan (incl. fans that blow a water mist); Kitchen machine (food preparation by mechanical means, AS/NZS 60335.2.14); Lawn care appliance (mower AS/NZS 60335.2.77, trimmer, robotic mower 60335.2.107); Liquid heating appliance (portable, ≤10 L, heats liquid for hot beverage/cooking, AS/NZS 60335.2.15); Massage appliance (household, portable, self-contained); Power supply or charger (output ≤50 V AC/120 V DC, household type for charging batteries or supplying separate equipment; battery charger AS/NZS 60335.2.29); Room heater (AS/NZS 60335.2.30); Tool—portable type (hand-held motor tools, excludes lawn & garden machinery); Vacuum cleaner (household, portable, AS/NZS 60335.2.2) — [EESS definitions v4.2](https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/EESS-Inscope-Equipment-Definitions-and-Risk-Levels-v4.2-.pdf)
  - **Evaporative cooler is Level 3 only when fixed**: the document's comment says "Portable evaporative coolers are level 1 as they are not fixed devices" — [EESS definitions v4.2](https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/EESS-Inscope-Equipment-Definitions-and-Risk-Levels-v4.2-.pdf)
  - **Level 2**: Air conditioner incorporating non-flammable or low-flammability refrigerant (class 1 or 2L, i.e. R410A, R32); Heating pad—flexible; Floor polisher; Projector — [EESS definitions v4.2](https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/EESS-Inscope-Equipment-Definitions-and-Risk-Levels-v4.2-.pdf)
- WA Building & Energy reproduces the Level 3 extract of AS/NZS 4417.2 Table B.4 (62 items) including "Cooking appliance – portable type", "Kitchen machine", "Liquid heating appliance", "Room heater", "Blanket", "Vacuum cleaner", "Lawn care appliance", "Power supply or charger", "Fan", "Evaporative cooler", "Air conditioner incorporating flammable refrigerant" and "Message [sic] appliance"; pressure washers are not named — [WA Building and Energy](https://www.wa.gov.au/sites/default/files/announcements/electrical-appliances-and-equipment-importing-selling-or-hiring)
- Certificate re-use: a certificate holder is not automatically a responsible supplier; equipment must still be registered by a Responsible Supplier and RCM-marked; only certificates uploaded to the EESS Certification Database can be used to register Level 2/3 equipment; certificate holders can lock certificates with a password; "It is an expectation that Responsible Suppliers have a working relationship with certificate holders" — [EESS Certification (General)](https://eess.gov.au/safety-requirements/certification-general) (via search summary)
- In the Level 3 equipment declaration the responsible supplier declares "The Responsible Supplier has permission to use this certificate for this registration from the certificate holder" and that it "will ensure the certificate remains current for the life of this registration" — [EESS Responsible Supplier Declarations](https://www.eess.gov.au/responsible-supplier/responsible-supplier-declarations/) (via search summary)
- Real-world example: Phihong (power supply maker) publishes letters authorising Australian responsible supplier World Tech Trading Pty Ltd to use Phihong's Certificate of Conformity for EESS registration; letters state the manufacturer will keep the product unchanged, bear recall/replacement costs and supply names of AU importers on request; letters carry expiry dates — [Phihong letter RCMPOE60S](https://www.phihong.com/wp-content/uploads/RCMPOE60S-1BT-EESS-A1.pdf); [Phihong letter RCMAA10S](https://www.phihong.com/wp-content/uploads/RCMAA10S-050A-EESS-A1.pdf)
- Each Australian importer must register imported Level 3 products even if another importer has registered the same product; overseas entities cannot be Responsible Suppliers — [Energy Safe Victoria FAQ](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/industry-guidance/electrical/frequently-asked-questions/electrical-equipment-safety-and-efficiency) (via search summary)
- Small air-conditioner HFC ban: from 1 July 2024 import/manufacture of small (≤2.6 kg charge) air-conditioning equipment using HFCs with GWP >750 is prohibited — explicitly including **portable air conditioners** — and from 1 July 2025 multi-head units too; R32 (GWP 675) and R290 (GWP <5) remain importable — [National EPA](https://www.nationalepa.gov.au/node/264); [ABF/FTA notice 2024/24](https://ftalliance.com.au/newsdetails/32286); [WTO import licensing summary](https://importlicensing.wto.org/content/ozone-depleting-substances-and-synthetic-greenhouse-gases-1)
- Pre-charged HFC equipment (incl. air conditioners) needs an Ozone Protection/SGG **equipment licence**; application fee **A$3,000**, non-refundable (2025-26) — [ABLIS Pre-Charged Equipment Licence](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/ag/pre-charged-equipment-licence/251). Exemption thresholds conflict: ABLIS says no licence for ≤5 pieces totalling <10 kg per consignment; WTO summary says importers of ≤25 kg SGG-in-equipment per calendar year don't need a licence; a UNEP 2025 presentation says HFC equipment licences last 2 years — [WTO](https://importlicensing.wto.org/content/ozone-depleting-substances-and-synthetic-greenhouse-gases-1); [UNEP presentation](https://ozone.unep.org/system/files/documents/Sessions%204%20and%205-1-%20Australia%27s%20licensing%20system.pdf)
- GEMS: the 2019 Air Conditioner Determination applied **MEPS to single-duct portable air conditioners for the first time** and introduced the Zoned Energy Rating Label (SEER) — [UL summary of GEMS Determination 2019](https://www.ul.com/news/new-greenhouse-and-energy-minimum-standard-gems-determination-2019-air-conditioners). GEMS products must be registered before sale; air conditioners sit in fee band 3 = **$670 per registration** (undated help page; same band in 2019/2020 fee instruments) — [GEMS registration payment help](https://reg.energyrating.gov.au/application/help/payment/); [GEMS Registration Fees Instrument 2019](https://legislation.gov.au/F2019L01065/asmade/2019-08-15/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html). GEMS Regulator cancelled four air-conditioner registrations in Feb 2024 and made air conditioners a primary compliance-monitoring focus for 2024-25 — [GEMS 2024-25 compliance monitoring program](https://www.energyrating.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-07/2024-25_gems_compliance_monitoring_program.pdf)
- ACMA: Wi-Fi/Bluetooth devices need EMC and radio compliance evidence (e.g. AS/NZS 4268 radio standard adopted by the Radiocommunications (Short Range Devices) Standard 2014; EMC standard e.g. CISPR 32/14-1) and a supplier's Declaration of Conformity (ACMA form) kept on file — [Raspberry Pi 5 Australian SDoC](https://pip.raspberrypi.com/categories/894-approvals/documents/RP-005012-CF/rpi-5-Australia_SDoC_LoA.pdf); [SolaX ACMA C02 form](https://at.solaxpower.com/uploads/file/c02-suppliers-declaration-of-conformity-jan-2018-en.pdf)
