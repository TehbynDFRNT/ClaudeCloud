# Landed Cost Structure and Compliance: Importing Bulk Consumer Goods from China into Australia for Resale (2025–2026)

*Research date: 9 October 2026. FX used throughout: AUD 1 = USD 0.6928 (1 Oct 2026), so USD 1 = AUD 1.4434. Some government sites (productsafety.gov.au, ATO, Meta help pages) blocked automated fetching, and the shared web-search budget ran out near the end, so several items are listed under Gaps. ## 1. Sea freight, destination charges, local delivery and air freight (Shenzhen/Ningbo/Shanghai → Sydney/Melbourne/Brisbane/Perth)

### Takeaway
In mid/late 2026, indicative China→east-coast ocean rates were about USD 1,500–2,640 per 20ft and USD 2,900–3,600+ per 40ft. LCL was quoted at roughly USD 60–280/CBM. Carriers pushed about USD 1,000/TEU of increases (GRIs) in September 2026, and port-to-port transit is about 23–31 days, with worst cases near 30–47 days. Australian destination charges often match or exceed the ocean freight. These include the carrier's destination terminal handling charge (DHC, about AUD 585–625 per 20ft), terminal "landside"/infrastructure levies (about AUD 225–235 per box plus booking and side-loader fees), LCL depot charges, the customs broker and cartage. LCL and a 20ft container cost about the same at roughly 14–15 CBM. Air freight (about USD 3.80–9.50/kg chargeable) only works for small, light, high-value items.

## 2. Australian import duties and taxes: general rate vs ChAFTA, GST, IPC, broker fees, ABN/GST registration, input tax credits

### Takeaway
Duty is charged on the customs value (FOB-based) in AUD:
- **General rate:** commonly 5%. About 457 "nuisance" tariffs were abolished from 1 July 2024, including fridges, dishwashers, washing machines, toasters, electric blankets, clothing and hand tools.
- **ChAFTA rate:** 0% for most Chinese goods, but only if a valid Certificate of Origin is held at the time of import.

GST is 10% of (customs value + duty + international transport and insurance).

For consignments over AUD 1,000, a Full Import Declaration costs:
- the ABF Import Processing Charge (IPC): AUD 50 electronic if under AUD 10,000, or AUD 152 if AUD 10,000 or more;
- DAFF's biosecurity charge: AUD 71 for sea, from 1 July 2026.

GST-registered importers recover import GST as an input tax credit (and some can defer it to their BAS), so for them GST is a cash-flow item rather than a cost.

### Cited Findings
- **ABF IPC table** (page last updated 13/08/2026), for import declarations (N10) and warehouse declarations (N20):

  | Consignment value (sea/air/post) | Electronic | Documentary |
  |---|---|---|
  | ≤ AUD 1,000 | $0 | — |
  | > AUD 1,000 and < AUD 10,000 | $50 | $90 |
  | ≥ AUD 10,000 | $152 | $192 |

  Ex-warehouse declarations (N30) cost $23 electronic or $63 documentary. — [ABF Import Processing Charges](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/importing/cost-of-importing-goods/charges/import-processing-charge)
- **Biosecurity cost-recovery charge on Full Import Declarations** (collected by ABF for DAFF), for consignments with customs value over AUD 1,000:
  - From 1 July 2026: sea AUD 71.00 (previously 68.00), air AUD 48.00 (previously 46.00).
  - Set by ACN 2026/23, dated 29 June 2026. — [ACN 2026/23](https://www.abf.gov.au/help-and-support-subsite/CustomsNotices/2026-23.pdf)
- **GST on imports:**
  - GST is "10% of the value of the taxable importation".
  - Value of the taxable importation = customs value + any duty payable + "the amount paid or payable to transport the goods to Australia and to insure the goods for that transport (T&I)" + WET if applicable.
  - GST is paid at the same time and in the same manner as duty.
  - "There is provision for certain importers to defer GST on imports" (deferral to the BAS).
  - Page last updated 1/07/2026. — [ABF GST and other taxes](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/importing/cost-of-importing-goods/gst-and-other-taxes)
- Customs value must be expressed in AUD under ABF exchange-rate rules. — [ABF cost of importing goods](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/importing/cost-of-importing-goods)
- ExFreight on duty, thresholds and GST credits:
  - Duty is "commonly 5% on many goods", depending on tariff classification and ChAFTA preference.
  - Goods ≤ AUD 1,000 clear on Self-Assessed Clearance and are generally free of duty and GST at the border. At or above AUD 1,000, a full import declaration is needed.
  - Documents: commercial invoice, packing list, B/L or AWB, and a valid Certificate of Origin for ChAFTA.
  - GST-registered businesses can claim the 10% back as an input tax credit. — [ExFreight](https://www.exfreight.com/shipping-from-china-to-australia/)
- **Nuisance tariff removal, 1 July 2024:**
  - 457 tariffs were removed ([Explanatory Statement F2024L00659](https://legislation.gov.au/F2024L00659/asmade/2024-06-07/es/original/pdf)); Treasury describes it as "around 500" ([Treasury](https://treasury.gov.au/consultation/c2024-506306)).
  - Treasury examples: toothbrushes, hand tools, fridges, dishwashers, clothing, menstrual products.
  - Further examples of 5% duties removed: washing machines, fishing reels, ballpoint pens, toasters, electric blankets, bamboo chopsticks. This covers about 14% of tariff lines and about AUD 8.5bn of annual trade. — [Austaxpolicy](https://www.austaxpolicy.com/news/about-500-nuisance-tariffs-to-be-abolished-from-july/)
- **ChAFTA tariff elimination:** Australian tariffs were zero on 82% of China's exports from day one, "rising to 100 per cent tariff elimination on 1 January 2019". A different DFAT page mentions 98% by 1 Jan 2029; the context of that figure is unclear. — [DFAT guide to using ChAFTA](https://www.dfat.gov.au/trade/agreements/in-force/chafta/doing-business-with-china/guide-to-using-chafta-to-export-or-import) (via search summary)
- **ChAFTA origin evidence:**
  - A Certificate of Origin is issued by an authorised body in the exporting country. It covers a single shipment and up to 20 unique goods, and is valid for up to one year. — [DFAT ChAFTA certificates of origin](https://www.dfat.gov.au/trade/agreements/in-force/chafta/doing-business-with-china/chafta-certificates-of-origin)
  - A Declaration of Origin can replace the certificate only where the goods are covered by a current Origin Advance Ruling.
  - ABF Customs Notice 2026/24 warns against declarations based on expired rulings. Without a valid ruling, "a valid Certificate of Origin must be held at the time of importation". — [ACN 2026/24](https://www.abf.gov.au/help-and-support-subsite/CustomsNotices/2026-24.pdf) (via search summary)
- Without a Certificate of Origin or a valid declaration, the ChAFTA preference cannot be claimed. The source also mentions infringement notices of 75% of underpaid duty; that is from a 2020 article and was not re-verified. — [FTA Alliance](https://www.ftalliance.com.au/newsdetails/18904)
- **Broker fees:** there is no official tariff. One anecdotal LCL invoice shows "customs and agency fees" of AUD 368 ex GST. — [Flying Solo forum](https://www.flyingsolo.com.au/members/searates-australia/forums/replies/)
- **GST registration threshold:** registration is required once GST turnover (gross sales, not profit) reaches AUD 75,000 over a rolling 12 months, looking back or forward. Below that it is voluntary. — [ausbusinessregister (secondary)](https://ausbusinessregister.com.au/selling-into-australia-gst-guide/)
- **ATO data matching:** the ATO's online selling data-matching program targets incorrect income reporting and "failure to comply with ABN and GST registration obligations, such as when online sellers transition from hobby status to being 'in business'". — [ATO online selling data-matching program](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/commitments-and-reporting/in-detail/privacy-and-information-gathering/how-we-use-data-matching/online-selling-data-matching-program-protocol/online-selling-data-matching-program-overview)

### Inferences
- **FOB basis for duty:** because the ABF adds transport and insurance separately to customs value in the GST formula, duty is charged on the FOB-equivalent customs value, not on freight. This is an inference from the formula, consistent with standard broker practice.
- **Government cost for a GST-registered importer with a ChAFTA certificate:** only IPC plus the biosecurity charge, AUD 121 per entry under AUD 10k or AUD 223 at AUD 10k and above. Without a certificate, add 5% of the FOB value in AUD where the general rate is 5%. Check first whether the line was among the abolished nuisance tariffs (already Free).
- **Make origin evidence a purchase-order term:** require the supplier to obtain a ChAFTA Certificate of Origin (from CCPIT or China Customs) before shipment and forward it with the documents. If it arrives after clearance, the preference is generally lost under the ABF's 2026/24 position.
- **Not GST-registered:** if turnover is under AUD 75k and you stay unregistered, import GST (≈10–11% of FOB+freight) plus GST on local invoices (broker, depot, cartage) becomes a real cost of ≈ AUD 13/unit in the worked example. Voluntary registration is worth evaluating for regular importers.
- **Crossing AUD 10,000:** consignments of AUD 10,000 or more pay IPC of AUD 152 instead of AUD 50. This is immaterial at scale. Staying at or below AUD 1,000 avoids both charges but is only realistic for samples.

## 3. Electrical compliance (EESS/RCM, risk levels, certification) and ACMA radio/EMC plus smart-device cyber rules

### Takeaway
Any mains-powered household product (above 50 V AC) is "in-scope" under the Electrical Equipment Safety System (EESS) and must carry the Regulatory Compliance Mark (RCM). The supplier must be a registered Responsible Supplier (AUD 231.91 a year).

Equipment is graded Level 1, 2 or 3 by risk:
- **Level 3** needs a Certificate of Conformity from a recognised certifier and national database registration (AUD 86.91 for 1 year up to 434.56 for 5 years). Level 3 includes many likely Marketplace products: portable cooking appliances (air fryers), kettles, toasters, fans, room heaters, vacuum cleaners, hair-care and massage appliances, extension sockets, portable lights, decorative lighting, power tools, and power supplies/chargers.
- **Level 2** also requires database registration.

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth products also need ACMA compliance: test evidence, a Supplier's Declaration of Conformity, responsible-supplier registration and the RCM. From 4 March 2026, internet-connectable consumer devices also need a cyber-security statement of compliance.

### Cited Findings
- **In-scope definition:** equipment rated above 50 V AC RMS or 120 V ripple-free DC, and below 1000 V AC / 1500 V DC, that is designed or marketed for household, personal or similar use. Marketing it as commercial or industrial does not remove it from scope.
  - Level 1: low or unknown risk (any in-scope item not in Level 2 or 3).
  - Level 2: medium risk.
  - Level 3: high risk, as defined in AS/NZS 4417.2. — [EESS risk-level definition](https://www.eess.gov.au/equipment/risk-level-definition/)
- **Level 3 obligations:** the item must be linked to a registered Responsible Supplier, registered on the National Database, hold a valid Certificate of Conformity from a recognised certifier, and be marked with the RCM. All Level 1/2/3 items must show brand or trade name, model number and the RCM. Level 3 "may mean the product must be tested by an accredited laboratory". — [WA Building & Energy factsheet](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-09/electrical_appliances_and_equipment_selling_hiring_or_advertising_factsheet.pdf)
- **Level 3 list** (extract of AS/NZS 4417.2:2020 Appendix B, Table B.4) — [WA factsheet](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-09/electrical_appliances_and_equipment_selling_hiring_or_advertising_factsheet.pdf):
  - **Kitchen and laundry:** bread toaster; cooking appliance – portable type; dishwashing machine; kitchen machine; liquid heating appliance; microwave oven; range; range hood; refrigerating appliance; washing machine; clothes dryer.
  - **Heating, cooling and water:** air conditioner with flammable refrigerant; evaporative cooler; fan; room heater; immersion heater; water heater; electric blanket; over blanket/duvet/wrap; electric hot water bottle.
  - **Personal care:** beauty care lamp; hair care appliance; iron; massage appliance; razor/hair clipper.
  - **Lighting:** decorative lighting outfit; LED lamp (self-ballasted); luminaire – portable type; nightlight; inspection headlamp; lamp holders.
  - **Plugs, cords and power:** cord extension socket; plug; socket-outlet; outlet device; supply flexible cord; power supply or charger; residual current device.
  - **Tools, garden and other:** tool – portable type; glue gun; soldering equipment; lawn care appliance; hedge clipper; arc welding machine; vacuum cleaner; insect electrocutor; fence energizer; submersible pump; swimming pool/spa equipment.
- EESS guidance says a mobile phone charger is Level 3 under "Power supply or charger". — [EESS in-scope definitions v4.1](https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/EESS-Inscope-Equipment-Definitions-and-Risk-Levels-v4.1.pdf) (via search summary)
- A retail air-fryer listing in Australia shows EESS equipment number E2023101338 and certificate number SAA-230561-EA. — [Woolworths listing](https://www.woolworths.com.au/shop/productdetails/1117735899/healthy-choice-3l-black-digital-air-fryer-w-200c-non-stick-removable-basket) (via search summary)
- **WA penalty:** selling, hiring or advertising appliances without compliance marks and an Australian approval carries a maximum of AUD 2,500 for individuals and AUD 10,000 for corporations. — [WA factsheet](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-09/electrical_appliances_and_equipment_selling_hiring_or_advertising_factsheet.pdf)
- **EESS fees from 1 July 2025** (GST-free) — [EESS registration fees](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-fees/) (via search summary):
  - Responsible Supplier: AUD 231.91 per year.
  - Level 2/3 equipment registration: AUD 86.91 (1 year), 173.83 (2 years), 434.56 (5 years).
  - Level 3 registration cannot run longer than the Certificate of Conformity. — [EESS Level 3](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-in-scope-electrical-equipment/level-3/)
  - An older Queensland page shows AUD 224.30 and 84.06 at the 2023 indexation. — [WorkSafe Qld](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/safety-and-prevention/hazards/electricity/electrical-products-and-equipment/selling-electrical-equipment/equipment-certification-and-registration/fees-for-electrical-appliances)
- **Certificates of Conformity** are issued by government regulators or recognised external certification schemes, based on test reports from approved testing entities. No price is published, so costs depend on the certifier and lab. — [EESS certification (general)](https://eess.gov.au/safety-requirements/certification-general) (via search summary)
- **AS/NZS 3820** (essential safety requirements): eBay's submission to Energy Safe Victoria notes that declaring a product controlled equipment "would require compliance with AS/NZS 3820:2009 before supply". — [ESV summary of submissions, Jan 2026](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/Summary-of-submissions-Electrical-safety-requirements-for-lithium-ion-battery-powered-e-transport-devices-%28January-2026%29.pdf)
- **ACMA supplier duties:** arrange any required testing, register as a responsible supplier on the national database, obtain and keep compliance records, and label products before supply. — [ACMA "Know what you must do"](https://www.acma.gov.au/know-what-you-must-do)
- **Using the RCM for ACMA purposes:**
  - The supplier must be registered on the national (EESS) database before applying the RCM.
  - ACMA-only registration has no fee and does not let you enter product details.
  - Agents are not permitted.
  - ACMA's "levels" do not correspond to EESS levels. — [EESS "ACMA Requirements – Use of RCM"](https://www.eess.gov.au/rcm/acma-requirements-use-of-rcm/); [2013 labelling amendment notice](https://legislation.gov.au/F2013L00085/asmade/2013-01-23/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)
- **Supplier's Declaration of Conformity (ACMA form C02):** made under the Radiocommunications Equipment (General) Rules 2021 and the Radiocommunications Labelling (EMC) Notice 2017. It is kept on file rather than lodged, produced on request, and supported by test reports or certification. — [Espressif SDoC example](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/Suppliers%20declaration%20of%20conformity-%20ESP32-PICO-V3-ZERO%20RCM%20SDOC.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Cyber Security (Security Standards for Smart Devices) Rules 2025:**
  - Registered 4 March 2025; core obligations apply from 4 March 2026 to consumer "relevant connectable products".
  - Requirements: no universal default passwords, a vulnerability reporting mechanism, and a defined minimum security-update period.
  - Suppliers must supply the product with a statement of compliance: device type, batch identifier, support period and a declaration. Manufacturer and supplier keep copies for 5 years.
  - Home Affairs guidance: devices manufactured before 4 March 2026 need not comply.
  - Exclusions include desktops, laptops, tablets and smartphones.
  - Enforcement is by compliance, stop and recall notices. — [Rules F2025L00276](https://legislation.gov.au/F2025L00276/asmade/2025-03-04/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html); [Home Affairs](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/about-us/our-portfolios/cyber-security/security-standards-for-smart-devices); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/cyber-security-standards-for-consumer-smart-devices)

### Inferences
- **Level 3 product without an Australian certificate:** for air fryers, kettles, heaters, fans, vacuums, hair tools, massagers, power boards, LED or fairy lights, chargers and power tools, the main deal-killer is a factory that can't supply a current Australian Certificate of Conformity plus test reports, ideally with the factory or its AU agent already holding the registration. Without that, each model needs Australian testing and certification before even a 100-unit order can be sold legally.
- **Minimum fixed admin, even with an existing certificate:** Responsible Supplier AUD 231.91 plus a 1-year Level 3 registration AUD 86.91 = AUD 318.82, about AUD 3.19/unit on 100 units, before any testing or certification fees.
- **Robot vacuums need several clearances:**
  - The robot runs on a battery (under 50 V), so it is likely outside EESS voltage scope itself. Its mains charger or dock falls under "power supply or charger" (Level 3).
  - Wi-Fi or Bluetooth means ACMA radio and EMC evidence, an SDoC, Responsible Supplier registration and the RCM.
  - App or cloud connectivity means a cyber statement of compliance for units made on or after 4 March 2026.
  - The lithium battery triggers dangerous-goods shipping (see section 4).
- **Portable power stations:** they charge from mains and have 230 V inverter outlets, so they plausibly fall in scope. They also carry large lithium packs (dangerous goods). Classification needs confirmation from a certifier, and they are high risk for a novice.
- **Marking and listings:** the RCM must be on the product and the registration is publicly searchable. Showing the RCM in Marketplace photos and keeping certificate numbers on file is low-cost evidence. WA penalties apply to "advertising" as well as selling.

### Gaps
- **Level 2 list not retrieved:** the current official "EESS In-scope Equipment Definitions and Risk Levels v4.3" list was not retrieved, so the Level 2 list is unknown.
- **No testing or certificate costs:** no published typical cost for Australian testing plus a Certificate of Conformity (e.g. AS/NZS 60335-2-x for an air fryer or heater) was found. Quotes are needed from certifiers and labs.
- **Power stations:** EESS classification of portable power stations is not confirmed.
- **Responsible Supplier eligibility:** whether a Responsible Supplier must be an Australian or NZ legal entity, and whether a Chinese factory's existing certificate can be used under the importer's registration, was not confirmed from an official source.
- **ACMA detail:** ACMA compliance levels for Wi-Fi/Bluetooth short-range devices and RF-exposure (EME) obligations were not retrieved.

## 4. Lithium batteries: dangerous-goods shipping, NSW/VIC/federal e-micromobility rules, e-bike standards, and whether e-bikes/scooters are realistic

### Takeaway
Lithium-ion batteries are Class 9 dangerous goods (UN3480 for batteries alone, UN3481 when packed with or inside equipment). They need dangerous-goods paperwork (for sea freight, an Australian "MO41" dangerous-goods declaration), carry surcharges, and have restricted LCL acceptance.

Since 1 February 2026, NSW requires separate certification and an approval mark for each e-bike, e-scooter or other e-micromobility vehicle, its battery and its charger. The mark must also appear in online listings, and corporate penalties reach AUD 825,000. Victoria has proposed similar rules, and on 6 August 2026 the federal government tasked the ACCC with a national e-bike standard.

For a small Marketplace importer, e-bikes and e-scooters are not realistic.

### Cited Findings
- **Classification and paperwork:** lithium-ion batteries are Class 9 dangerous goods, UN3480 (alone) and UN3481 (packed with or contained in equipment). Dangerous goods need a DG Declaration for air and an "MO41" for sea freight, prepared by a qualified person. — [BWSC 2027 Freight Manual (June 2026)](https://assets.worldsolarchallenge.org/app/uploads/2026/06/10154922/2027_BWSC_Freight_Manual_V2.0-Published-10062026.pdf)
- **Air rules (FedEx):**
  - Packing instructions 966 (packed with equipment) and 967 (contained in equipment) have Section I (needs a Shipper's Declaration) and Section II (compliance statement on the waybill).
  - Li-ion cells and batteries must be offered at a state of charge of 30% or less unless approved, then cargo aircraft only.
  - A dangerous-goods (IDG) surcharge applies to Section I shipments. — [FedEx AU lithium guide](https://www.fedex.com/en-au/shipping-guide/pack/lithium-batteries/ion-batteries/contained-in-equipment/power-rating-greater-than-100wh/qty-less-than-equal-to-5kg.html) (via search summary)
- **Generic surcharge:** dangerous-goods surcharges run from about $40 to several hundred dollars per package depending on class, carrier and service. This is not Australia-specific. — [Shipware](https://shipware.com/glossary-of-shipping-terms/dangerous-goods/)
- **Dedicated DG LCL services:** consolidation services for Class 8/9 cargo, covering batteries, e-bikes, e-scooters and power tools, exist on China–Europe. This shows such cargo needs specialist consolidation. — [Logistics Business (cargo-partner)](https://www.logisticsbusiness.com/transport-distribution/haulage-freight-forwarding/lcl-service-for-dangerous-goods/)
- **NSW scope and dates:**
  - Applies to e-micromobility vehicles declared electrical articles under the Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) Act 2017: certain e-bikes, e-scooters, e-skateboards and self-balancing scooters, plus their Li-ion batteries and chargers.
  - From 1 Feb 2026, products must be certified and marked. UL 2271:2018 and UL 2272:2016 are accepted only until 1 Feb 2027. — [NSW supplying e-micromobility products](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/selling-goods-and-services/supplying-e-micromobility-products)
  - The safety standards themselves applied from 1 Feb 2025. Testing and certification commencement was moved to 1 Feb 2026. — [NSW Statement of Regulatory Intent](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/fair-trading/how-we-regulate/statements-of-regulatory-intent/statement-of-regulatory-intent-e-micromobility-vehicles) (via search summary)
- **NSW accepted standards** — [NSW page](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/selling-goods-and-services/supplying-e-micromobility-products):

  | Product | Accepted standards |
  |---|---|
  | E-bikes ≤500 W | AS 15194:2016, EN 15194:2017+A1:2023 or UL 2849:2022 |
  | E-bikes >500 W | UL 2849:2022 |
  | E-bike batteries | EN 50604-1:2016+A1:2021, IEC 62133-2:2017 or UL 2271:2023 |
  | E-scooters, e-skateboards, self-balancing scooters (and their batteries) | AS/NZS 60335.2.114:2023, EN 17128:2020 or UL 2272:2024 (batteries also UL 2271) |
  | Chargers | AS/NZS 61558 or AS/NZS 60335.2.29 |

- **NSW certification and marking:**
  - Battery, charger and vehicle each need their own Certificate of Approval and safety approval mark, on the product itself.
  - Online listings must also show the mark.
  - Testing must be by a NATA lab or a recognised international lab (e.g. CNAS), with colour photos of the interior and exterior.
  - Changing the battery or charger creates a new model.
- **NSW penalties:** up to AUD 825,000, plus AUD 5,500 per Information Standard breach. — [NSW page](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/selling-goods-and-services/supplying-e-micromobility-products)
- **Victoria:**
  - Energy Safe Victoria proposed declaring e-bikes, e-scooters, e-skateboards and their Li-ion batteries "controlled electrical equipment", which would mean certification before supply. Consultation closed 13 Oct 2025. — [ESV consultation](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/media-centre/news/public-consultation-open-proposal-strengthen-e-transport-device-safety-requirements)
  - The January 2026 summary records 208 survey responses and 22 submissions with mixed views. No final decision or commencement date was found. — [ESV summary Jan 2026](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/Summary-of-submissions-Electrical-safety-requirements-for-lithium-ion-battery-powered-e-transport-devices-%28January-2026%29.pdf)
- **Federal:** on 6 Aug 2026, Assistant Minister Andrew Leigh announced the government is tasking the ACCC to develop a national e-bike safety standard, informed by EN 15194, then other e-micromobility. — [Treasury transcript](https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/andrew-leigh-2025/transcripts/doorstop-interview-federation-mall-canberra); [Bicycle Network, 13 Aug 2026](https://bicyclenetwork.com.au/newsroom/2026/08/13/accc-to-develop-e-bike-safety-standard/)
- **Federal import reference:** the federal government reportedly reinstated EN 15194 as the reference for importing, selling and road use (secondary blog). — [Reid Cycles, Feb 2026](https://www.reidcycles.com.au/blogs/news/ebike-laws-in-australia-february-2026-update)
  - A Queensland parliamentary submission says Commonwealth import rules still permit EN 15194:2017-compliant devices. — [Qld Parliament submission](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/com/SDIWC-1AF9/TOLMEUPOCA-AB90/submissions/00000108.pdf)
  - NSW reportedly plans to cap road-legal e-bikes at 250 W, down from 500 W (secondary). — [Reid Cycles](https://www.reidcycles.com.au/blogs/news/ebike-laws-in-australia-february-2026-update)
- **Self-balancing scooters (hoverboards):** a separate national mandatory standard applies, the Consumer Goods (Self-balancing Scooters) Safety Standard 2018. — [Compliance Gate list](https://www.compliancegate.com/?p=30478)

### Inferences
- **E-bikes, e-scooters and hoverboards are not viable for a small Marketplace importer:**
  - three certifications and marks per model in NSW (vehicle, battery, charger);
  - the mark must be shown in listings;
  - Victoria is moving toward certification and a national ACCC standard is coming, so rules are moving targets;
  - DG shipping with MO41 paperwork and limited LCL acceptance;
  - high fire and product-liability exposure, since the importer is the deemed manufacturer (section 7).
- **Other battery products also carry a DG premium:** robot vacuums, power banks, power stations, cordless tools and battery ride-on toys are not banned, but expect dangerous-goods documentation (UN38.3 test summary, SDS and DG declaration — UN38.3 is from general knowledge, not verified this session), forwarder surcharges, and some consolidators refusing LCL. Confirm carrier acceptance and the surcharge before placing the order.
- **Air:** Section II shipments (small batteries inside equipment) are the least burdensome. Large packs (>100 Wh, e.g. power stations) and standalone batteries (UN3480) are the most restricted.

### Gaps
- **UN3171 and UN38.3:** UN3171 (battery-powered vehicles, e.g. e-bikes in sea freight) and the UN38.3 test-summary requirement were not verified with a source this session.
- **No surcharge figures:** no published China→Australia DG surcharge amounts for LCL or FCL were found.
- **Other states:** Victoria's final decision is unknown. Queensland, WA and SA lithium/e-mobility rules were not researched.
- **ACCC timeline:** the timeline for the national ACCC e-bike standard is unknown.

## 5. ACCC mandatory safety/information standards and bans relevant to likely products; gas appliance certification

### Takeaway
Many plausible Marketplace categories fall under mandatory federal standards, with ACL penalties for businesses of up to the greater of AUD 50m, 3× the benefit, or 30% of adjusted turnover. These include:
- toys for 0–36 months, toys with magnets, projectile and aquatic toys, and lead in toys;
- products containing button or coin batteries;
- bunk beds, household and folding cots, the 2024 infant-sleep standard, prams and baby walkers;
- the toppling-furniture information standard (warnings, including in online listings);
- pedal bicycles and bicycle helmets;
- portable pools, quad bikes, mini motorbikes and self-balancing scooters;
- treadmills, exercise cycles and vehicle jacks/stands/ramps.

Gas BBQs, outdoor cookers and camping stoves must be certified under state gas-safety rules (Gas Technical Regulators Committee scheme, AS/NZS 5263 series). Uncertified gas appliances are unlawful to sell.

  - **Batteries:** button/coin batteries, and products containing them (Safety and Information Standards 2020).
  - **Children's products:**
    - infant sleep products (Safety Standard 2024); infant products (Information Standard 2024);

  - **Furniture:** toppling furniture (Information Standard 2024); bean bags (2014); bunk beds (CPN 1 of 2003).

ACL penalties for businesses are the greatest of AUD 50m, 3× the benefit, or 30% of adjusted turnover; individuals face up to AUD 2.5m. — [MK Lawyers, 13 Nov 2025](https://mk.com.au/accc-set-to-enforce-mandatory-toppling-furniture-safety-standards-what-furniture-suppliers-need-to-know/) (via search summary)
- **Button and coin batteries:**
  - Covered products include cameras, calculators, toys, games, novelty items, musical greeting cards, storage containers, remote controls, measuring scales and thermometers.

- **Cots and infant sleep:**
  - Two new mandatory standards (infant sleep products, and inclined non-sleep products) were introduced 18 July 2024.
  - Folding cots had until 19 January 2026 to comply. The standard references AS/NZS 2195:2010 and BS EN 716-1:2017. — [Product Safety Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/node/517) (via search summary)

  - Each state and territory requires gas appliances to be certified against Australian Standards. Cancelled certification has national effect. — [Product Safety Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/node/1106)

- **Butane "lunchbox" camping cookers:**
  - Testing for the gas technical regulators found failing over-pressure shut-off devices, so certification was cancelled for multiple brands. Up to 31 models were withdrawn, and the regulators' view is that they cannot lawfully be sold.
  - WA banned six models outright.
  - These warnings date from around 2015. — [WorkSafe NT](https://worksafe.nt.gov.au/forms-and-resources/safety-alerts/safety-warning-on-portable-butane-gas-cookers); [NSW warning](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/about-us/product-safety-news/news/nsw-public-warning-on-portable-butane-%E2%80%98lunchbox%E2%80%99-cookers); [WA warning](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/about-us/product-safety-news/news/wa-public-warning-on-portable-butane-lunchbox-cookers)

### Inferences
- **Highest-risk (likely deal-killers without an existing Australian test report to the named standard):** children's and nursery products (cots, folding cots, infant sleep products, prams, walkers), toys for under-3s and magnet toys, bunk beds, quad bikes and mini motorbikes, hoverboards, gas appliances, and bicycles and helmets. ## 6. Biosecurity (DAFF): BMSB seasonal measures, timber/wood packaging, wooden/rattan/bamboo goods, fees and delays

### Takeaway
China is not a brown marmorated stink bug (BMSB) "target risk" country, so China-origin goods shipped directly do not need mandatory BMSB treatment.
- For the 2026-27 season (1 Sep 2026 – 30 Apr 2027), China remains an "emerging risk" country, which means random onshore inspections. Airfreight from China and the USA gets random verification inspections.
- The practical biosecurity hurdles for China imports are:
  - timber packaging (ISPM-15) and packing declarations;
  - wooden furniture and other timber articles, which may need treatment certificates;
  - bamboo, rattan, cane and plant-fibre goods, which need a treatment and/or phytosanitary certificate or face inspection;
  - container cleanliness.
- Costs:
  - AUD 71 per sea entry, charged on the Full Import Declaration;
  - inspection fees, indexed up about 3.8% from 1 July 2026;
  - storage and demurrage while goods are held.

### Cited Findings
- **2026-27 BMSB season:**
  - Runs 1 September – 30 April inclusive.
  - "Japan, Republic of Korea, United Kingdom and China are currently still considered emerging risk countries".
  - "Airfreight from USA and China will be subject to random verification inspections".
  - Alternative clearance pathways, including the BMSB Safeguarding Arrangement Scheme, cease from the 2026-27 season. — [DAFF 2026-27 BMSB industry presentation](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-27-bmsb-industry-presentation.pdf) (via search summary; the PDF itself returned errors)
- **Other 2026-27 changes:** the Rolled Goods Policy was removed; breakbulk must be shipped or sealed within 120 hours of treatment; ethyl formate is now approved onshore. — [KLN BMSB 2026/27 guide](https://info.oceania.kln.com/knowledgehub/guide-to-2026/27-stink-bug-season-bmsb) (via search summary)
- **2025-26 season:**
  - 41 target risk countries. The UK, China, Japan and Korea were emerging risk countries only, meaning random onshore inspection.
  - China, Japan and Korea were subject to heightened surveillance of roll-on/roll-off (Ro-Ro) vessels. — [BIMCO](https://www.bimco.org/news-insights/bimco-news/2025/09/25-bmsb/); [DAFF 2025-26 presentation](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2025-26-bmsb-industry-presentation.pdf) (via search summary)
  - One forwarder guide placed China inside the target list, which conflicts with DAFF. — [icecargo](https://icecargo.com.au/the-ultimate-stink-bug-guide-2025-26-bmsb-season-australia/)
- **Furniture paperwork:** in addition to the invoice, packing list and packing declaration, furniture may need:
  - a fumigation or pest-control certificate covering timber goods and wooden packing;
  - a newly-manufactured plywood declaration;
  - evidence of legal logging;
  - a storage declaration if goods were not treated in the export container.
  - Descriptions must be specific (e.g. "wooden furniture", not just "furniture"). — [icecargo furniture fact sheet](https://icecargo.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Importing-Furniture-into-Australia-Fact-Sheet.pdf); [icecargo guide](https://icecargo.com.au/import-furniture-australia/)
- **Plant materials:** goods made of or containing bamboo, cane, rattan, plant fibre or other plant material need a treatment and/or phytosanitary certificate. — [icecargo fact sheet](https://icecargo.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Importing-Furniture-into-Australia-Fact-Sheet.pdf)
  - DAFF has a separate BICON case for bamboo products (fencing, poles, handicrafts, chopsticks, baskets, skewers). — [DAFF timber types](https://agriculture.gov.au/import/goods/timber/types)
- **Customs system question for wooden furniture:** for tariff headings 9401.61 and 9403.60, the Integrated Cargo System asks whether goods contain wood without documents proving acceptable treatment or manufacture under BICON. — [DAFF industry advice 131-2023](https://agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/industry-advice/2023/131-2023) (via search summary)
- **Consequences of non-compliance:** goods may be seized, re-exported or destroyed. Treatments include fumigation and heat treatment. Articles must be free of bark, insects and soil. — [icecargo guide](https://icecargo.com.au/import-furniture-australia/)
- **DAFF fees from 1 July 2026:**
  - Most biosecurity charges rose by 3.8% (indexation). New prices are in the 2026-27 Cost Recovery Implementation Statement (CRIS).
  - Diagnostic charges are being restructured from 1 Nov 2026. — [KLN DAFF fee advisory](https://info.oceania.kln.com/customer-advisory/daff-biosecurity-and-imported-food-regulatory-fee-changes-effective-1-july-2026); [FTA IFN 11-2026](https://ftalliance.com.au/newsdetails/40746)
  - Historical reference: diagnostic fees were AUD 30 per 15 minutes in 2018. — [DAFF IAN 79-2018](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/industry-advice/2018/79-2018)
- **FID biosecurity charge:** AUD 71 (sea) from 1 July 2026. — [ACN 2026/23](https://www.abf.gov.au/help-and-support-subsite/CustomsNotices/2026-23.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Don't budget for BMSB fumigation on China direct shipments.** Do allow a contingency for a random inspection (fees plus several days of storage) on BMSB "target high-risk" goods during Sep–Apr.
- **Avoid transhipment via target-risk countries.** Shipping via a BMSB target-risk country could trigger treatment requirements; check routing with the forwarder.
- **Wooden, bamboo or rattan goods are the main biosecurity cost driver.** Require from the factory:
  - ISPM-15 marked pallets and crates, or plastic or cardboard packaging;
  - treatment certificates from a DAFF-recognised provider for timber, bamboo or rattan articles;
  - a correct packing declaration.
  Without these, expect inspection or onshore fumigation charges, delays, or re-export.
- **Inspection cost order of magnitude:** a household-removals destination quote listed quarantine inspection fees of about AUD 730 / 1,030 / 1,090 for 20ft / 40ft / 40HC (seen via search summary only; [moverspoe](https://data.moverspoe.com/AQS/AQS_OffersFiles/238048/RWQuote.htm)). This is weak evidence, but a full inspection plus storage can plausibly add hundreds of AUD per container.

## 7. Australian Consumer Law obligations, product liability insurance, ABN/business registration and GST for a Marketplace seller of new goods

### Takeaway
Someone regularly buying stock to resell new goods on Marketplace is very likely "in business". That means:
- they need an ABN, and an ASIC business name if trading under any name other than their own;
- they must register for GST once GST turnover reaches AUD 75,000;
- the ATO data-matches online sellers.

The importer of goods made by a foreign manufacturer with no Australian place of business is the "deemed manufacturer" under the Australian Consumer Law (ACL). It carries both the seller's consumer-guarantee obligations (it must provide remedies itself, not redirect buyers) and manufacturer-level liability for safety defects. That makes product liability insurance essential. Entry-level combined public and products liability policies start at a few hundred dollars a year, but importers of riskier products pay more.

### Cited Findings
- **Deemed manufacturer:**
  - An onshore importer of goods made offshore can be directly liable as the "deemed manufacturer".
  - In the *Leeks* case, the court held that consumers can claim against the foreign manufacturer and the Australian importers concurrently.
  - Manufacturers, including deemed manufacturers, are liable for certain consumer-guarantee breaches. — [LegalVision](https://legalvision.com.au/legal-considerations-of-importers-under-australian-consumer-law); [HWL Ebsworth](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/the-australian-consumer-law-suppliers-and-manufacturers-who-is-ultimately-liable-to-consumers-for-defective-goods/)
  - When the manufacturer has no place of business in Australia, the importer is deemed to be the manufacturer, which can raise insurance quotes. — [Finder](https://www.finder.com.au/business-insurance-for-importers)
- **Seller's obligations:** consumer guarantees apply. The seller "is responsible for providing a solution to the consumer" and "can't refuse to help... or tell them to contact the manufacturer". A manufacturer's warranty may not apply in Australia. — [ACCC parallel imports](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/buying-parallel-imports)
- **ACCC enforcement:** in February 2025 the ACCC reviewed more than 2,000 Australian online retailers and flagged language implying that consumer rights are limited or that the manufacturer's warranty is the only remedy. — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/australia/acccs-sweeping-investigation-exposes-dubious-e-commerce-returns)
- **Marketplace sellers:** "if you're selling regularly or advertising new products, the law will likely treat you as a business". Such sellers generally need an ABN and a legal structure, and should register a business name with ASIC if not trading under their personal name. — [Sprintlaw](https://www.sprintlaw.com.au/articles/selling-on-facebook-marketplace-australian-legal-compliance-guide/)
- **Data matching:** the ATO data-matches online sellers for ABN/GST non-compliance, including those moving from "hobby" to "in business". — [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/commitments-and-reporting/in-detail/privacy-and-information-gathering/how-we-use-data-matching/online-selling-data-matching-program-protocol/online-selling-data-matching-program-overview)
- **GST:** registration is required at AUD 75,000 GST turnover (gross) over a rolling 12 months. — [ausbusinessregister (secondary)](https://ausbusinessregister.com.au/selling-into-australia-gst-guide/)
- **Insurance benchmarks:**
  - Public liability averages about AUD 39/month for low-risk businesses. Product cover is often combined with public liability. — [Upcover](https://www.upcover.com/blog/public-liability-insurance-for-small-business)
  - Quotes depend on product type, sales volume and limit; brokers can quote importers. — [Finder](https://www.finder.com.au/business-insurance-for-importers)

### Inferences
- **Treat consumer guarantees as a cost line.** Budget a returns and warranty allowance (assumption: 2–5% of revenue for simple goods, more for electricals). Never say "no refunds" or "manufacturer warranty only" in listings; that is a misleading-representation risk.
- **Being the deemed manufacturer is the main reason to avoid batteries, nursery goods and gas appliances.** One fire or injury claim can exceed years of margin, and many policies exclude or price up uncertified products.
- **Keep records per model:** test reports, certificates, supplier details and batch/lot numbers. This supports recalls, which are mandatory if a product is unsafe, and insurance claims.
- **Insurance on a 100-unit/year operation:** an entry-level combined policy (order of AUD 400–600/yr, inferred from the AUD 39/month benchmark) adds about AUD 4–6/unit. It falls quickly with volume.

### Gaps
- **Statute not read:** ACL s 7 and Part 3-5 were not read directly, nor the mandatory "warranty against defects" wording (Competition and Consumer Regulations).
- **No importer premiums:** no reliable importer-specific premium data was found. A search snippet cited a ProRisk combined public/products policy "from $200 plus taxes" with a $20m limit, but the source page wasn't confirmed and it may not suit importers.
- **ATO not read directly:** ATO "Are you in business?" guidance was not fetched (403).

## 9. Worked example: 100 units at USD 60 FOB, 0.15 CBM and 25 kg per unit, LCL to Sydney

### Takeaway
For 100 units (15 CBM, 2.5 t, so 15 W/M) at USD 6,000 FOB (AUD 8,660.51 at 0.6928), the mid-case ex-GST landed cost is **about AUD 130 per unit with a ChAFTA certificate (0% duty), or AUD 134 at 5% duty**. That is about 1.5× the FOB price in AUD (AUD 86.61). The plausible range is AUD 113–158 per unit, about 1.3–1.8× FOB.

At 15 CBM the shipment sits right at the LCL/20ft break-even:
- A 20ft for the same 100 units costs about AUD 130–160 per unit in 2026 conditions.
- Filling the 20ft with about 180 units (27 CBM) drops it to about AUD 117–122 per unit.
- Air freight is unviable: AUD 137–343 per unit for freight alone.

If you are not GST-registered, add about AUD 13 per unit of unrecoverable GST.

### Cited Findings (inputs)
- FX: AUD/USD 0.6928 on 1 Oct 2026. — [exchange-rates.org](https://www.exchange-rates.org/exchange-rate-history/aud-usd-2026-10-01)
- LCL ocean rates: USD 60–280/cbm (June 2026); LCL stable through the Q3 2026 peak. — [ExFreight](https://www.exfreight.com/shipping-from-china-to-australia/); [KLN](https://info.oceania.kln.com/customer-advisory/fcl-shipping-rates-from-china-to-australia-september-2026-outlook)
- FCL 20ft: USD 1,500–1,800 (June 2026), plus about USD 1,000/TEU of GRIs announced for September 2026. — [ExFreight](https://www.exfreight.com/shipping-from-china-to-australia/); [KLN](https://info.oceania.kln.com/customer-advisory/fcl-shipping-rates-from-china-to-australia-september-2026-outlook)
- Fixed LCL destination items: delivery order AUD 80, THC AUD 70, re-engineering AUD 45, cargo reporting AUD 45, security AUD 70 (= AUD 310). Customs and agency fee AUD 368. — [Flying Solo forum](https://www.flyingsolo.com.au/members/searates-australia/forums/replies/)
- Government charges: ABF IPC AUD 50 for customs value of AUD 1,000–10,000 (AUD 152 at AUD 10,000 or more). DAFF biosecurity charge on the Full Import Declaration AUD 71 for sea. — [ABF IPC](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/importing/cost-of-importing-goods/charges/import-processing-charge); [ACN 2026/23](https://www.abf.gov.au/help-and-support-subsite/CustomsNotices/2026-23.pdf)
- GST = 10% × (customs value + duty + transport and insurance). — [ABF GST](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/importing/cost-of-importing-goods/gst-and-other-taxes)
- 20ft Sydney local costs:
  - Maersk DHC AUD 600 (from 1 Oct 2026). — [Maersk](https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2026/09/01/maersk-terminal-handling-service-australia)
  - Patrick Sydney landside charge AUD 224.90 + vehicle booking AUD 50.45 (2026). — [Patrick notice](https://www.transport.wa.gov.au:443/getmedia/eff2ef31-5dce-4c0c-83e6-d16a67c52cbd/Patrick_Terminals_landside_charges_2025_notice_of_intention.pdf)

- Air freight: USD 3.80–9.50/kg chargeable; volumetric divisor 6,000. — [ExFreight](https://www.exfreight.com/shipping-from-china-to-australia/)

## 10. Per-category compliance checklist: what can kill or raise the cost of an import

### Takeaway
Before ordering, run each product through seven gates:
1. Tariff and ChAFTA certificate of origin
2. EESS electrical level
3. ACMA radio/EMC and cyber
4. Batteries (dangerous goods and button-battery rules)
5. ACCC mandatory standard
6. Gas certification
7. Biosecurity (wood, bamboo, rattan, packaging)

Items that fail gates 2, 4, 5 or 6 without existing Australian test reports or certificates are usually deal-killers at 100-unit scale. Furniture and timber goods mainly add paperwork and treatment cost, not prohibitions.

### Cited Findings
(Each row restates findings cited in sections 2–8; sources there.)

| Category (examples) | Main regulatory hurdles | Kill / cost risk for a 100-unit importer |
|---|---|---|
| **Mains kitchen and home appliances** (air fryers, kettles, toasters, fans, heaters, vacuums, hair tools, massagers) | EESS Level 3 (Certificate of Conformity, database registration, Responsible Supplier, RCM); ACMA EMC; ACL deemed manufacturer | **High** unless the factory has a current Australian certificate and test reports. Fees ≥ AUD 319 a year plus testing. |
| **Power boards, chargers, LED and decorative lights, portable lamps** | Level 3 ("cord extension socket", "power supply or charger", "decorative lighting outfit", "luminaire – portable") | **High** without an Australian certificate. Cheap chargers are a frequent non-compliance item. |
| **Smart or Wi-Fi/BT devices** (robot vacuums, cameras, smart plugs) | ACMA radio/EMC + SDoC + RCM; charger at Level 3; cyber statement of compliance (made on or after 4 Mar 2026); Li-ion dangerous goods (UN3481) | **High.** Multiple regimes plus DG freight surcharge. |
| **Portable power stations, power banks** | Li-ion DG (UN3480/UN3481; large Wh), EESS status unclear, high fire/ACL exposure | **Very high** |
| **E-bikes, e-scooters, hoverboards** | NSW certification of vehicle, battery and charger plus marks (from 1 Feb 2026), including in listings; Victoria proposal; national ACCC standard coming; hoverboard mandatory standard (2018); DG shipping | **Prohibitive** for a small importer |
| **Toys (0–36 months, magnets, projectile, aquatic), kids' ride-ons** | ACCC mandatory toy standards; lead in toys; button-battery standard; DG if Li-ion | **High.** Needs test reports to the AU-referenced standards. |
| **Products with button or coin cells** (remotes, scales, LED candles, thermometers) | Secure compartment, child-resistant battery packaging, warnings, compliance testing (since 22 Jun 2022) | **Medium**: packaging and testing cost, often missed |
| **Nursery** (cots, folding cots, infant sleep products, prams, walkers, bath aids) | Multiple mandatory standards, including the 2024 infant-sleep standard (folding cots by 19 Jan 2026) | **Very high.** Avoid. |
| **Furniture** (dressers, wardrobes, bookcases, TV units; bunk beds; bean bags) | Toppling-furniture warnings online and on product (from 4 May 2025); bunk-bed and bean-bag standards; timber biosecurity (treatment certificates, ISPM-15, ICS wood question for 9401.61/9403.60) | **Medium.** Paperwork, treatment and inspection risk; bunk beds high. |
| **Bamboo, rattan, cane, wicker goods** | Treatment and/or phytosanitary certificate per BICON; inspection | **Medium–High.** Re-export or destruction if non-compliant. |
| **Bicycles, helmets** | Pedal bicycle mandatory standard (CPN 6 of 2004); helmet standard 2024 | **High** without test reports |
| **Gas BBQs, pizza ovens, patio heaters, camping stoves** | State gas certification (GTRC scheme, AS/NZS 5263.x; 2023 core-standard recertification) plus approval mark | **Prohibitive** if uncertified |
| **Portable pools; trampolines** | Portable pool mandatory standard (2013); trampolines: no mandatory standard found (unverified) | **Low–Medium** |
| **Fitness and auto** (treadmills, exercise cycles, trolley jacks, stands, ramps, recovery straps) | Mandatory standards | **Medium–High** |
| **Quad bikes, mini motorbikes** | Mandatory standards (2019) | **Prohibitive** |
| **Clothing and textiles** | Care-labelling information standard (2023); children's nightwear standard; many clothing tariffs abolished 2024 | **Low** |
| **Cosmetics** | Cosmetics information standard (ingredient labelling) | **Medium.** Other chemical-import rules not researched. |

### Inferences
- **Supplier vetting:** before deposit, ask for the Australian certificate numbers (EESS, gas, NSW e-micromobility). Verify them on the public databases and get written permission to be listed as the Australian Responsible Supplier or an authorised supplier. Also obtain test reports to the exact Australian standards named in the mandatory standard, and confirm the ChAFTA certificate of origin and ISPM-15 packaging in the purchase order.
- **Best fit for a first import:** non-electrical, non-battery, non-children's, non-gas, non-timber or treated-timber goods with low CBM per dollar. Where duty is 5%, a certificate of origin removes it; for many lines, the 2024 tariff abolition has already made duty Free.

### Gaps
- **AICIS registration for cosmetics importers:** registration with the Australian Industrial Chemicals Introduction Scheme (AICIS) was not researched.
- **Food-contact materials:** requirements for kitchenware were not researched.
- **Energy rating:** GEMS energy-efficiency registration for appliances such as fridges, air conditioners and TVs was not researched. It could add cost for some appliance categories.
- **Trampolines and bicycle standard references:** not confirmed (see section 5).
