# Fast-selling NEW product categories on Facebook Marketplace Australia (AUD $100–$400), 2025–2026

*Researched 9 Oct 2026. Method and limits: Meta publishes no Marketplace sales data, and Marketplace search needs a login. Price evidence therefore comes from (a) Gumtree AU listings, used as the closest classifieds proxy, and (b) Facebook Marketplace item and category pages that search engines have indexed. **Every price below is an ASKING price, not a sold price.** Reddit could not be reached at all. Whirlpool thread pages (403), the Amazon AU Best Sellers page (503), the Australia Post trends page (403) and Google Trends could not be fetched, so evidence from them is either missing or comes from search-result summaries only. The shared web-search budget ran out before freestanding boxing bags, kayaks, gaming chairs, heaters and electric blankets, cat trees and pressure washers could be price-checked.*

---

## 1. Which categories do Australian Marketplace sellers and flippers report as selling fastest (days-to-sell), especially brand-new-in-box items from small importers or drop-shippers?

### Takeaway
There is no hard days-to-sell data for Australian Marketplace. The only Australian time-to-sale figure found is an unsourced 2026 guide's claim that correctly priced furniture sells in 24–48 hours. Australian seller guidance consistently names the same fast movers: furniture, kids' gear, gym equipment, outdoor/garden items and small appliances. Nothing found measures how fast brand-new-in-box items sell compared with used ones.

### Cited Findings
- An Australian seller guide (Roopon, published 19 May 2026, "Roopon Team", **no data sources cited**) says: "Priced right, furniture sells within 24–48 hours". This is the only time-to-sell figure in the article. — [Roopon](https://www.roopon.com/blog/how-to-sell-on-facebook-marketplace-australia)
- The same guide lists the strongest Australian categories as:
  - furniture: sofas, dining tables, bed frames, wardrobes, desks
  - children's items: prams, high chairs, cots
  - electronics
  - gym equipment: dumbbells, barbells, resistance bands, home gym setups
  - outdoor and garden: lawn mowers, outdoor furniture, garden tools, BBQs
  - small appliances: stand mixers, air fryers, coffee machines

  — [Roopon](https://www.roopon.com/blog/how-to-sell-on-facebook-marketplace-australia)
- Roopon also says gym equipment sells "extremely well, especially in January and after winter". It adds that "fast fashion rarely moves", that buyers usually offer 10–20% below the asking price, and that an RRP anchor such as "RRP $350, selling for $180" helps frame value. — [Roopon](https://www.roopon.com/blog/how-to-sell-on-facebook-marketplace-australia)
- **US data, promotional, no methodology:** Swoopa, a flipping-tool vendor (byline 30 Oct 2025), says air conditioners and portable fans "sell almost instantly" and baby products and toys "sell within days". It gives no measured sell-through or days-to-sell figures. — [Swoopa](https://getswoopa.com/top-selling-items-facebook-marketplace-june-2025/)
- **Gumtree AU, older and second-hand-focused (c.2022 "Trading in the Circular Economy" report):** the items that "sold the quickest and also made the most money" were:
  - clothing, shoes and accessories: 17%
  - games and toys: 10%
  - electronic goods: 10%
  - home décor and furniture: 9%

  — [Savings.com.au](https://www.savings.com.au/news/gumtree-pre-loved-items-worth-small-fortune); [EcoVoice](https://www.ecovoice.com.au/going-full-circle-new-research-from-gumtree-reveals-the-value-of-the-circular-economy-now-stands-at-62-billion/) (EcoVoice adds books at 8%)
- **Gumtree AU most-searched items, October 2020 (COVID era, old):** boat, mountain bike, ride-on mower, caravan, surfboard, kayak, fridge, dining table, Lego, outdoor furniture. Caravan searches were +68% and boat searches +17%; the article is inconsistent about whether these compare with Sept 2020 or with 2019. — [B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/caravan-searches-spike-in-latest-gumtree-items-trends/)
- Canstar's estimated resale prices for the same Oct 2020 list:
  - mountain bike $300
  - surfboard $250
  - kayak $400
  - fridge $250
  - outdoor furniture $300

  — [Canstar](https://www.canstar.com.au/budgeting/10-items-in-demand-gumtree/)
- **Anecdotes (Whirlpool forum, older posts; exact thread page not verified):**
  - buyers report "brand new" Marketplace and Gumtree listings that don't hold up, such as a "new" PS4 with no receipt and a "brand new" gas bottle about 12 years out of date
  - one poster reports an iPhone 7 Plus selling on Gumtree within 37 minutes

  — [Whirlpool Gumtree/Marketplace General Discussion](https://forums.whirlpool.net.au/archive/2634319); [p.13](https://forums.whirlpool.net.au/archive/95xv8k73-7); [p.10](https://forums.whirlpool.net.au/archive/95xv8k73-4)
- The WA Small Business Development Corporation says listing on Marketplace is for individuals (not Business Pages) and is not well suited to building an eCommerce brand or scaling a business. — [Small Business WA](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/blog/expert-tips-instagram-and-facebook-sales)
- Sprintlaw says that if you sell regularly or advertise new products, the law will likely treat you as a business. Australian Consumer Law guarantees then apply: acceptable quality, matching the description, and no misleading origin or warranty claims. — [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/selling-on-facebook-marketplace-australian-legal-compliance-guide/)
- CPA Australia (via Nest Egg, **June 2021**) said the ATO can request data from platforms such as Facebook Marketplace. Profit-making reselling is taxable. — [Nest Egg](https://www.nestegg.com.au/earn-money/tax-refund/facebook-marketplace-flippers-are-safe-from-the-ato-for-now)

### Inferences
- How fast an item sells appears to depend on its category and price, not on whether it is new. The categories named as fast in Australia are mostly bulky household, fitness, outdoor and kids' goods, which suits a new-in-box importer of bulky generic goods.
- New-in-box items are probably sold the same way flippers describe: undercut retail and anchor on the RRP. Price discipline matters more than the "new" label, because buyers expect to offer 10–20% below asking.
- Buyers distrust "brand new" claims they cannot verify. That suggests receipts, an Australian warranty statement and inspection at pickup are selling points.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/AusFinance, r/flipping, r/australia) was blocked, and Whirlpool thread pages returned 403. No first-hand Australian days-to-sell reports for new-in-box goods could be collected.
- No news.com.au, ABC, SMH or Guardian AU story was found profiling an Australian Marketplace business that sells new imported stock.
- A TikTok "things to sell on Facebook Marketplace from Temu" discovery page exists, but it was not analysed. No Australian YouTube or TikTok reseller data was gathered.

### Inferences
- In this band, small and easily shipped generic items such as gadgets, small kitchen appliances and accessories compete directly with Temu (about 5m Australian shoppers), Amazon AU and Kmart. Amazon's Prime Day best-seller mix shows its strength is small branded goods.
- Marketplace's relative edge is therefore likely to be bulky or heavy goods, where delivery cost, delivery time and damage risk weaken online rivals (see Section 5).
- Home & Garden being a top-three online category ($11.4bn) shows large household demand for outdoor, furniture and garden goods. It does not show which channel wins the bulky lines.
- Temu's local-seller program could, over time, bring locally warehoused bulky goods onto Temu. This is a competitive threat to monitor, though no evidence was found that it has happened at scale.

- Kogan and Catch best-seller data was not researched.
- No 2025 Gumtree AU trends or circular-economy report was found; the latest Australian Gumtree category data is about 2022.

## 3. Seasonality in Australia

### Takeaway
Hard ABS and ARA data confirms three peaks for household goods: **November** (Black Friday / Cyber Monday, the largest), **June** (EOFY and mid-year sales, which boost furniture) and **Boxing Day**. Outdoor and water categories are weather-sensitive: BCF's fishing, marine and watersports sales fell in the 2025 southern drought, while 4WD/touring held up in WA, QLD and NT. Evidence for winter heating demand is anecdotal only.

### Cited Findings
- **Black Friday–Cyber Monday 2025** (28 Nov–1 Dec): the ARA and Roy Morgan forecast a record $6.8bn, +4% on 2024.
  - About 6 million Australians were expected to take part, spending $804 on average.
  - The main reasons to take part were low prices (42%), strong deals (38%) and saving on Christmas gifts (25%).

  — [ARA media release, 7 Nov 2025](https://www.retail.org.au/media/record-black-friday-weekend-bonanza-tipped-to-hit-6-8-billion-as-shoppers-chase-value)
- **November 2025 actuals (ARA summary of ABS data):**
  - Retail turnover was +7.0% YoY at $39.1bn.
  - **Household goods was the largest gain at +10.1% YoY.**
  - Nearly one in three shoppers began Christmas buying earlier than in 2024.

  — [ARA](https://www.retail.org.au/media/early-christmas-shopping-drives-strong-november-retail-growth); [Inside Retail](https://insideretail.com.au/business/november-has-become-critical-latest-retail-sales-data-revealed-202601). Caveat: the ABS has been transitioning from Retail Trade to a household spending indicator, so the exact series behind these figures was not verified.
- **ABS, December 2024 (released 3 Feb 2025):**
  - "Cyber Monday drove more spending on household goods as consumers took advantage of discounts on big ticket items."
  - Furniture, homewares, electronics and electrical items were boosted in early December.
  - Household goods were +1.6% in December after +1.3% in November.

  — [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/retail-spending-steady-december)
- **ABS, June 2025 (released 31 Jul 2025):**
  - Household goods retailing was +2.3% in June.
  - "Mid-year sales events increased spending on discretionary items like furniture", and EOFY sales boosted department stores and clothing.

  — [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/retail-sales-surge-june)
- **Boxing Day 2025 (ARA/Roy Morgan forecast):**
  - Household goods $476m (+4.4%), clothing $216m (+1.9%), department stores $123m (+5.1%).
  - The post-Christmas week (25–31 Dec) was forecast at $3.832bn (+4.4%).
  - The ARA expects "value-driven behaviour to remain a feature of consumer spending into 2026".

  — [Roy Morgan/ARA](https://www.roymorgan.com/findings/10076-ara-roy-morgan-media-release-boxing-day-peak-season-2025); [ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-12-24/boxing-day-sales-australia-2025/106171474)
- **A conflicting, more cautious view:** Deloitte Access Economics (Nov 2025) expected more muted end-of-year spending because an inflation spike had reduced purchasing power. — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone1/au/en/docs/services/economics/deloitte-access-economics-retail-forecasts-nov-2025-executive-summary.pdf)
- **Outdoor and camping (Super Retail Group, which owns BCF):**
  - FY25 (to June 2025): BCF sales hit a record, +7.9% to $951m; Rebel was +4.8% to $1.4bn. — [Inside Retail](https://insideretail.com.au/business/four-key-takeaways-from-super-retail-groups-fy25-results-202508)
  - H1 FY26 (to Dec 2025): BCF like-for-like sales fell 1.6%. Drought in SA and VIC "heavily impacted Fishing, Marine and Watersports", while 4WD & Touring kept momentum in WA, QLD and NT. — [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/super-retail-groups-profit-to-dip-after-bcf-hit-by-the-big-dry-in-victoria-and-south-australia.html)
  - Later in FY26: BCF's second-half-to-date like-for-like sales fell 3.3%, which the company blamed on fuel prices after the Middle East conflict, felt most in regional areas. — [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/super-retail-group-sales-momentum-stalls-as-fuel-prices-and-rising-rates-bite.html)
- **Spring and summer:** Gumtree's October 2020 most-searched list was led by boats, mountain bikes, ride-on mowers, caravans, surfboards, kayaks and outdoor furniture (2020 data). — [B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/caravan-searches-spike-in-latest-gumtree-items-trends/)
- **Gym:** gym equipment sells best "in January and after winter" (unsourced guide claim). — [Roopon](https://www.roopon.com/blog/how-to-sell-on-facebook-marketplace-australia)
- **Cooling:**
  - CHOICE says portable evaporative coolers cost under 10c per hour to run, against about $0.35–$0.65 per hour for portable air conditioners, but they "don't work well in humid conditions". — [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/home-and-living/cooling/portable-air-conditioners/articles/portable-evaporative-coolers-vs-portable-air-conditioners)
  - In the US, ACs and portable fans "sell almost instantly" on Marketplace (promotional source). — [Swoopa](https://getswoopa.com/top-selling-items-facebook-marketplace-june-2025/)
- **Winter heating:** the only "demand surge" claim found for heated throws is a seller's own marketing ("sells out faster than expected… especially during winter"). — [AdoreEarth](https://adorearth.com/blogs/news/best-electric-blanket-australia-2025-the-complete-buyers-guide). No 2025 heater or electric blanket sales data was found.
- **Christmas, kids' gifts:**
  - Vuly promoted trampolines for Black Friday 2025 from $399 (Flare). — [Vuly](https://www.vulyplay.com/en-AU/black-friday-sales-trampoline)
  - A ride-on toy retailer advertises ride-on cars "from as low as $120" with 2–9 working-day delivery. — [Ride On Toys Kids](https://rideontoyskids.com.au/)

### Inferences
Practical calendar for a new-in-box Marketplace importer (inferred from the above):

| Months | Demand focus |
|---|---|
| Jan | Gym equipment (New Year) |
| Sep–Dec | Outdoor furniture, gazebos, pools, SUPs, evaporative coolers |
| Late Oct–24 Dec | Christmas lines: kids' ride-ons, trampolines, basketball hoops, bikes |
| Nov | Black Friday deal-seeking across household goods (the biggest spike) |
| Jun | EOFY: furniture and big-ticket items |
| May–Aug | Heaters and electric blankets (mostly under $100 and dominated by Kmart, Aldi and Harvey Norman, so a poor fit for the band) |

- Christmas stock probably needs to be landed and listed by about October to catch early buyers, given ARA's "one in three start earlier" finding.
- Southern drought and 2026 fuel-price pressure are real downside risks for water-sports and regional touring products such as SUPs, kayaks and 12V fridges. QLD, WA and NT looked more resilient for 4WD and touring.
- Evaporative coolers sell best in dry climates (inland and southern Australia), which points to Perth, Adelaide, inland NSW/VIC and Canberra rather than humid Brisbane or Darwin.

### Gaps
- No Google Trends seasonal curves (not accessible).
- No Marketplace-specific seasonal data.
- No 2025 Australian sales data for heaters or electric blankets.
- No full-season ARA Christmas 2025 category forecast was found (only Black Friday and Boxing Day).

## 5. Bulky vs small items: does Marketplace's local pickup give a structural advantage?

### Takeaway
Yes, and the evidence is reasonably concrete. Australia Post caps a domestic parcel at **22kg, 105cm and 0.25m³**, and many candidate items (24kg dumbbells, racks, trampolines, lounge sets, mattresses, bed frames, fridges) exceed this. They therefore travel by freight at extra cost and take days. Online and drop-ship sellers quote 2–8+ business days or charge $40–$70+ for metro delivery. Small items compete head-on with Temu, with about 5m Australian shoppers.

### Cited Findings
- **Australia Post domestic limits** (Post Charges guide dated 1 Sep 2026):
  - Maximum 22kg, 105cm greatest length and 0.25m³.
  - Above 5kg, the charge uses the greater of actual or cubic weight (cubic factor 250).

  — [Australia Post Post Charges guide (PDF)](https://auspost.com.au/content/dam/auspost_corp/media/documents/post-guides/post-charges-guide-ms11.pdf)
- A packaging blog says oversized parcels that enter the network may incur a $100 "Over Maximum Limits Fee". This could not be verified against Australia Post. — [EB Packaging](https://ebpackaging.com.au/blogs/news/australia-post-packaging-requirements-a-quick-guide)
- **Delivery friction seen in listings:**
  - Dumbbell seller: $40 delivery in metro Melbourne. — [Gumtree](https://www.gumtree.com.au/s-gym-fitness/adjustable+dumbbells+24kg/k0c18565)
  - Mattress seller: $50 Penrith, $70 Sydney metro. — [Gumtree](https://www.gumtree.com.au/s-beds/brand+new+queen+size+mattress/k0c20074)
  - Gardeon, Everfit and Rigo listings: "delivery only, 2–8 business days". — [Gumtree](https://www.gumtree.com.au/s-garden/4+piece+wicker+outdoor+lounge+setting/k0c18398)
  - Everfit bench on FB: about 4–10 days delivery. — [FB](https://www.facebook.com/marketplace/item/120416254389476/)
  - Ride-on retailer: 2–9 working days. — [Ride On Toys Kids](https://rideontoyskids.com.au/)
- Roopon advises giving dimensions for furniture, stating whether delivery is available "at what cost", and taking cash or a confirmed bank transfer on collection for large items. — [Roopon](https://www.roopon.com/blog/how-to-sell-on-facebook-marketplace-australia)
- **Trust environment:**
  - ABC (Mar 2024): Marketplace "has a reputation for being overrun with scammers… the problem may be getting worse", and the platform accounts for most online buying scams. — [ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2024-03-01/facebook-marketplace-has-become-the-home-of-scammers/103521536)
  - US Senate testimony (Sep 2025) by a former Meta researcher estimated that about 0.5% of Marketplace users report scams or counterfeit goods each month, about 5m reports a month globally. — [US Senate Judiciary testimony (PDF)](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/6ccd5abd-cbb9-5107-d48d-b99e821eb244/2025-09-09%20-%20Testimony%20-%20Sattizahn1.pdf)
- **Meta policy:** Marketplace prohibits non-physical items (services, subscriptions, digital products, rentals). — [Meta Offers Policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/358970040838616)
- No Meta rule explicitly banning dropshipping or requiring sellers to hold stock was found. Third-party guides say nothing in the Commerce Policies prohibits dropshipping. Some claim shipped Marketplace orders are US-centric and that elsewhere Marketplace is local pickup only. **These guide claims conflict and were not verified for Australia.** — [SaleHoo](https://www.salehoo.com/learn/facebook-marketplace-dropshipping); [AutoDS](https://www.autods.com/blog/dropshipping-tips-strategies/how-to-dropship-on-facebook-marketplace/)
- Temu had about 5m Australian shoppers in 2025, and Amazon AU's best sellers are small branded items. — [Roy Morgan](https://www.roymorgan.com/findings/10158-amazon-temu-and-shein-growth-story-continues-march-2026); [About Amazon AU](https://www.aboutamazon.com.au/news/retail/amazon-prime-day-2025-delivers-record-sales-and-savings-in-australia)

### Inferences
- **A local importer that holds stock has three edges on Marketplace:**
  1. Same-day pickup against 2–10 day drop-ship or online delivery.
  2. Inspect-before-pay, which matters given Marketplace's scam reputation and buyer scepticism about "brand new" claims.
  3. No freight cost to the buyer, which for bulky goods (above 22kg or 105cm) often adds $40–$70+ in metro areas and more regionally.
- Drop-shippers listing Dropshipzone stock on Marketplace usually cannot offer pickup ("No Local Pickup – Delivery Only"). Their many listings therefore compete on price but not on immediacy.
- The advantage is strongest for heavy, awkward or fragile goods: dumbbells, racks, plates, trampolines, outdoor lounges, mattresses, bed frames, 12V fridges, rowers and treadmills. It is weakest for compact goods such as massage guns, air fryers and small heaters, where Temu, Amazon and Kmart are cheaper and convenient.
- If Meta does not offer integrated shipping in Australia (unverified), Marketplace is structurally a local-pickup channel. That favours bulky goods and penalises sellers without local stock.

### Gaps
- Whether Facebook Marketplace shipping or checkout is available in Australia in 2025–26 could not be confirmed from Meta sources.
- No Australian freight-courier rate data for bulky goods (for example 30–50kg consignments to metro or regional addresses) was gathered.
- No data on Temu's or Amazon AU's delivery times or costs for bulky goods in Australia.

**Shortlist for an importer (inference).** These categories combine bulky or heavy goods, adequate demand evidence and less-than-extreme visible saturation:
- **Heavy home-gym gear:** 2×24kg adjustable dumbbells, adjustable racks, benches and spin bikes, with January the key window.
- **Q4 kids' outdoor:** trampolines and basketball hoops, for the Black Friday to Christmas window.
- **Generic dual-zone 12V fridges:** a cautious pick, priced against the $430–$599 branded anchors and aimed at the QLD, WA and NT touring markets.
- **Evaporative coolers:** a cautious, seasonal pick, in dry-climate metros only.

The most saturated categories (mattresses, ride-ons, wicker lounge sets, egg chairs, gas-lift beds, iSUPs) have proven demand but are crowded with warehouse sellers and Dropshipzone listings. An entrant would need a clear angle there: same-day pickup, bundles, or a better spec at the same price.

### Gaps
- **No row has measured days-to-sell or sold prices.** The ranking is a reasoned judgement from proxies.
- Candidates **not price-checked** because the search budget ran out:
  - freestanding boxing bags
  - sit-on-top kayaks
  - gaming and office chairs
  - cat trees and dog kennels
  - oil heaters and electric blankets
  - pressure washers
  - sun loungers and daybeds
  - kids' bikes and balance bikes
  - cubby houses and swing sets
  - air hockey and foosball tables
  - small power stations under 500Wh

  Several of these, notably kayaks and freestanding boxing bags, are bulky and could rank well.
- Compliance risks were not researched in this workstream: mandatory safety standards for toys, trampolines and ride-ons, electrical safety (RCM) for coolers and walking pads, pool-fencing laws, and dangerous-goods freight for lithium. They could change the ranking.
- Google Trends AU seasonality and the Amazon AU Best Sellers and Movers & Shakers rankings could not be checked from this environment. The report writer should treat item-level demand claims as unverified by those proxies.
