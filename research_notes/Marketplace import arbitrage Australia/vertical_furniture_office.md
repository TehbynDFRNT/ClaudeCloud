# Home & Office Furniture: buying China-direct and selling brand-new on Facebook Marketplace (A$100–$400), costed SKU by SKU

**How the data was collected (2026-10-09).**
- **Amazon.com.au**: search pages blocked automated requests (HTTP 503), but product pages loaded. Prices come from each page's "New (n) from $X & FREE Delivery / + $Y Delivery" all-offers line where it showed, and otherwise from the "n offer(s) from $X" price in each product's related-item carousel (the page's own buy-box price was hidden). Carousel prices do not show delivery or seller, and may include non-new offers. Rows marked "verified new + delivery" come from the all-offers line.
- **Other retailers and suppliers fetched directly**: IKEA AU category pages, Fantastic Furniture category pages, Amart (amartfurniture.com.au) category pages, Made-in-China listing and product pages, and OzBargain tag and store pages.
- **Blocked (HTTP 403, or content not rendered without JS)**: Kmart, Kogan, eBay AU, Temple & Webster, Gumtree, Reddit, productsafety.gov.au, accc.gov.au, Officeworks (JS-only), Temu (JS-only) and Big W (timeout).
- **Unreachable (HTTP 503 / empty reply)**: agriculture.gov.au (BICON/DAFF) and industry.gov.au (Anti-Dumping Commission).
- **WebSearch**: the session-wide quota ran out mid-task, so some floors rely on earlier search-engine snippets. These are flagged.

## Key question 1 — Marketplace is flooded with USED furniture/office chairs: which NEW items still sell fast at a premium over used, and at what prices?

### Takeaway
I could not reach Marketplace, Gumtree or Reddit, so there is no direct evidence on asking prices or sell-through. Indirect evidence (Amazon AU "bought in past month" badges, review volumes, and bulky-delivery fees) points to the following:
- **Strongest new-only demand:** queen mattresses-in-a-box, gaming chairs and cheap flat-pack storage. These are the items where hygiene concerns or assembled-state fragility make used items less substitutable.
- **Price ceilings:** brand-new sellers must undercut the delivered prices of Amazon AU importer brands. Those floors are about A$75 (headrest mesh chair), A$100 (gaming chair), A$170 (single-motor desk), ~A$350 (dual-motor desk), A$187 (queen pocket-spring mattress), A$240 delivered (gas-lift queen) and A$82 (5-tier bookshelf).

### Inferences
- **New items that should still command a premium over used.** None of this is measured on Marketplace itself.
  - Mattresses: hygiene and bed-bug aversion. They are also roll-packed, so a 30×30×160 cm box fits a car.
  - Upholstered and gas-lift beds: bed-bug aversion, and they are hard to disassemble.
  - Flat-pack particleboard case goods (tallboys, TV units, shoe cabinets, bookshelves): these lose integrity when disassembled and moved, so used units sell poorly.
  - Gaming chairs bought as gifts.
- **Weakest premium over used:** office/mesh chairs and desks. Used corporate chairs, refurbished premium brands and WFH-era surplus compete directly. Recliners and sofas also have abundant used supply, but new-in-box sells to buyers who won't take a used upholstered item.
- **Prices that a brand-new Marketplace listing must stay under:**

  | Item | Amazon floor (A$) | Implied Marketplace target (A$) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Mesh chair with headrest | 75 | ≈60–67 |
  | Gaming chair | 100 | ≈85–90 |
  | Single-motor desk | 170 | ≈149 |
  | Dual-motor desk | ~348 | ≈299 |
  | Queen pocket-spring mattress | 187 | ≈165 |
  | 6-drawer tallboy | 157–180 | ≈139 |
  | Gas-lift queen bed | 240 delivered | ≈199 |
  | Recliner | 315 | ≈279 |
  | 5-tier bookshelf | 82 | ≈69 |

  Several targets fall **below the A$100 band floor** (mesh chair, gaming chair, L-desk, basic TV unit, shoe cabinet, bookshelf). So within the band you must trade up to premium specs.

### Gaps
- Facebook Marketplace (login wall), Gumtree (HTTP 403) and Reddit (HTTP 403) were not accessible. There are no observed "brand new" asking prices, days-to-sell, or ratios of used to new listings for any SKU. This is the single biggest evidence gap. The other Marketplace-demand researcher's notes should be used to validate it.
- "Bought in past month" badges only appear on some ASINs, so they cannot be compared across all SKUs.

## Key question 2 — Who sets the floor in Australia (Kmart, IKEA, Fantastic, Amart, Officeworks, Temple & Webster, Artiss/Dropshipzone, Koala/Ecosa/Emma, Amazon AU sellers, Temu)?

### Takeaway
Across almost every SKU, the lowest delivered price comes from **Amazon AU importer brands**: Artiss, Oikiture, Giantex, ALFORDSON, PORIYA, Zinus and Giselle.
- **IKEA** is the floor in a few places: bookcases (LAIVA A$36, GERSBY A$70), TV benches (LACK 160 cm A$99) and smaller shoe cabinets (BISSA A$70). It sits at parity on gaming chairs (HUVUDSPELARE A$100). On basic chairs, Amazon's Artiss mid-back mesh chair at A$58.34 undercuts IKEA FLINTAN at A$79.
- **Fantastic Furniture** is the floor for foam mattresses (A$159) and basic office chairs (A$79).
- **ALDI Special Buys** set occasional deal floors: pocket-spring queen in a box A$239; electric desk A$189.
- **Kmart, Temu, Kogan, eBay, Temple & Webster and Officeworks** could not be priced (blocked).

### Inferences
- **The binding constraint is the Amazon AU importer brands, not big-box retail.** They import by the container load, have Prime/FBA or free-delivery logistics, and price basic SKUs at levels that leave a small importer no margin after A$148/m³ of freight and port costs (see Q6).
- **IKEA is the hard floor for open shelving and low TV benches.** It also matches Amazon on gaming chairs.
- **ALDI is a recurring disruptor** for desks and mattresses: A$189 and A$239 deals.
- **Mattress DTC brands (Koala, Ecosa, Emma) are not the floor.** Their queen mattresses sit far above A$187 (Koala Plus double A$987 deal, 23/12/2025 — [OzBargain node 941445](https://www.ozbargain.com.au/node/941445)). The floor is set by Giselle, Zinus and FF house brands.

### Gaps
- Kmart (403), Kogan (403), eBay AU (403), Temple & Webster (403), Officeworks (JS-only) and Temu AU (JS-only) prices were not observed. Kmart floors are especially important for the "not above the Kmart floor" rule and remain unverified.
- Some Amazon floors come from carousel "offers from" prices (delivery and seller not shown). Others came from an earlier search-engine snippet: Oikiture dual-motor 140×70 at A$348.42, observed date unknown.

## Key question 3 — Chinese clusters and named factories; FOB prices at realistic MOQs (incl. mixed-SKU containers)

### Takeaway
Supply sits in clear clusters:
- **Anji, Zhejiang:** mesh, gaming and office chairs; manual and power recliners.
- **Ningbo/Zhejiang:** standing-desk frames (Prorials, Xinyi, Weisetech, Lumi Legend, Jiecang).
- **Foshan, Guangdong:** office chairs, panel furniture, mattresses, compressed sofas.
- **Bazhou/Langfang, Hebei:** stools, chairs, shoe cabinets, metal beds.
- **Tianjin:** gaming chairs and stools.
- **Shandong (Qingdao/Shouguang):** gas-lift beds, TV units.

### Inferences
- **Mixed-SKU 40HQs are feasible from a single cluster** (Kingnod and Jikeyuan state they accept mixed models). A multi-cluster mix (Ningbo desks + Anji recliners + Shandong TV units + Guangdong shelves) would need either a consolidator, or separate LCL/FCL from Ningbo and Shenzhen.
- **Price ladders show the main FOB lever is volume.** Moving from 100 to 500+ units typically cuts 5–13%: Boke US$33→29, Yolanda US$28→24, Sweetnight US$59→39.
- **Quoted ranges are not firm prices.** Listing prices are pre-RFQ and spec-dependent; no RFQs were sent, per the brief.

### Gaps
- No Loctek (Ningbo) listing surfaced on Made-in-China in this pass. Jiecang listings were found, but not their OEM pricing for frames alone.
- Desktop (top-only) FOB prices were not found. A US$17 per 140×70 particleboard top is an **assumption**.
- ImportYeti, Alibaba, 1688 and GlobalSources were not used (WebSearch quota exhausted). Business-licence scope was not visible on product pages; badges and self-claims are the only manufacturer-vs-trader evidence.

## Key question 4 — Bulkiness: CBM per unit, units per 40HQ, effect on landed cost; Marketplace pickup advantage vs online delivery fees

### Takeaway
Freight plus destination costs work out to about **A$148 per m³** for a full 40HQ (A$104 ocean + A$44 port, broker and cartage). That makes cube the deciding cost for beds and sofas: gas-lift queen ~A$71/unit, 2-seat sofa bed ~A$65, recliner ~A$49. Desk frames (A$4–8) and roll-packed mattresses (A$21) barely feel it. Marketplace pickup saves the buyer A$32–102 of Amazon/Artiss delivery only on the bulkiest goods. Those are also the goods where China-direct landed cost is already uncompetitive, with full-size recliners as the main exception.

- **Assumed cost per m³: A$148.1** = (US$4,900 ÷ 0.6929 = A$7,072 ocean + A$3,000 destination, labelled estimate) ÷ 68 m³ usable.
- **Freight and destination as a share of FOB:**

  - mattress 20%
  - premium TV unit 41%
  - mesh/gaming chairs 31–57%

## Key question 5 — Compliance: toppling furniture, EESS/RCM for electric desks, mattress rules, formaldehyde, biosecurity (BICON), anti-dumping, duty

### Takeaway
- **Toppling-furniture information standard (in force 4 May 2025).** It covers tallboys, chests, bookcases and display cabinets ≥686 mm, plus TV/entertainment units of any height. It needs permanent labels, point-of-sale warnings in-store *and online* (applies to Marketplace listings) and anchoring instructions. The ACCC found 90% of businesses non-compliant and has signalled tougher enforcement. Cost is low (≈A$1–2/unit + listing copy), but non-compliance risk is high.
- **Electric desks (and power recliners, LED or power-outlet TV units).** Treat these as in-scope electrical equipment under EESS: Responsible Supplier A$239.74/yr, equipment A$89.85/yr (Level 2 or 3), RCM, and a compliance folder or certificate. Test cost is the real barrier (estimated A$2–6k per model, unverified).
- **Duty.** Under ABF Schedule 3 the general rate is 5% for chairs, desks and case goods. **Mattresses and sofa-beds ("seats convertible into beds") are Free.** ChAFTA preference needs a Certificate of Origin.
- **Not verified in this pass:** BICON specifics, ADC measures, mattress flammability rules and formaldehyde rules (government sites unreachable).

### Cited Findings
**Toppling furniture:**
- Effective 4 May 2025. Covers "chests of drawers, wardrobes, bookcases, hall tables, display cabinets, buffets and sideboards with a height of 686mm or more, and entertainment units of any height". It requires warnings "online and instore at the point of sale… on the products themselves and in any accompanying instructions" — [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/babies-and-kids/children-and-safety/avoiding-common-dangers/articles/new-safety-laws-about-toppling-furniture).
- Requirements: "attaching permanent warning labels to furniture; displaying point of sale warnings both in-store and online; and providing safety information in instructions" — [Furntech](https://www.furntech.org.au/toppling-furniture-new-requirements/).
- Sweep: "more than 3000 furniture products at more than 160 businesses and found 90% had failed to comply" — [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/babies-and-kids/children-and-safety/avoiding-common-dangers/articles/new-safety-laws-about-toppling-furniture).
- Common failures: "warnings must be permanent on the product"; "if a consumer can add the product to the cart without seeing the safety information, it's considered non-compliant"; manuals must include anchoring advice. Penalties "as high as $50 million or 30% of adjusted turnover" — [LFRA / Unwrap](https://www.unwraplfra.com.au/accc-sweep-finds-widespread-non-compliance-with-mandatory-toppling-furniture-safety-rules/).
- The standard was made 13 Apr 2024 and registered 3 May 2024, with a 12-month transition (search-engine summary of ACCC/Product Safety pages) — [ACCC media release](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/most-furniture-suppliers-fail-mandatory-toppling-furniture-safety-rules).
- Online marketplaces "aren't legally required to ensure the products sold by third parties… are safe and compliant" — [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/babies-and-kids/children-and-safety/avoiding-common-dangers/articles/new-safety-laws-about-toppling-furniture).

**EESS:**
- "All Level 2 in-scope electrical equipment offered for sale must be registered by a Responsible Supplier with the EESS and be marked with the Regulatory Compliance Mark (RCM)". **Per-SKU compliance blockers** (cost and lead-time figures are estimates unless sourced):

- **Tallboy, chest, bookcase, TV unit, shoe cabinet** (the last as a precaution if ≥686 mm).
  - Order factory-applied permanent warning labels, an anchor kit and an instruction insert. About A$1.20/unit plus ~A$300 one-off artwork (estimate). No lead-time impact if specified in the PO.
  - Every Marketplace listing must show the warning before the buyer commits.
- **Electric desks (single/dual), power recliners, TV units or L-desks with mains LED drivers or power outlets.**
  - EESS supplier and equipment registration (A$329.59 in year 1, sourced).
  - Test reports to the relevant AS/NZS standards for the compliance folder, or a certificate for Level 3. EMC/RCM evidence is also needed.
  - Estimated A$2,000–6,000 per model, 4–8 weeks, unless the factory's existing IEC reports can be leveraged.
  - Design-out options: USB-powered LEDs; no built-in power boards.
- **Mattresses.** No mandatory requirement was verified. Check flammability, care labelling and stewardship (see Gaps) before ordering, and request CertiPUR/OEKO-type foam certificates from the factory (some listings advertise CertiPUR-US).
- **Chairs and stools.** No mandatory Australian standard was identified in this pass. Budget about A$800 for an optional gas-lift/stability test (estimate) to manage product-liability risk under the ACL.
- **All SKUs.**
  - Claim ChAFTA 0% via Certificate of Origin (saves 5% of FOB on 9401.3x/9401.6x/9401.7x/9403 goods).
  - Use ISPM-15 or non-timber pallets.
  - Expect random BMSB-season inspections (China is an "emerging risk" country).

### Gaps
- productsafety.gov.au and accc.gov.au returned HTTP 403, so the ACCC supplier guide's exact label wording and minimum label size could not be read.
- AS/NZS 4417.2 (paywalled) not checked: it was not confirmed whether a desk control box with an internal power supply is Level 1, 2 or 3, or whether an external adapter makes the adapter Level 3.
- agriculture.gov.au (BICON) was unreachable (HTTP 503 / empty reply). Import conditions for solid-timber or bamboo furniture versus MDF/particleboard, and packaging-declaration rules, were **not verified**.
- industry.gov.au (Anti-Dumping Commission Dumping Commodity Register) was unreachable (HTTP 503). **No evidence found** of anti-dumping duties on finished furniture or mattresses, but this is **unverified**.
- No source was found on an Australian mandatory formaldehyde limit for furniture, a mandatory domestic-mattress flammability standard, or care-labelling coverage of mattresses/upholstery. All treated as unknown, not as "no requirement".

**Category-level conclusion.** For home and office furniture, the "undercut Amazon AU by 10–20%" rule mostly fails, because Amazon AU itself is occupied by China-direct importers (Artiss, Oikiture, Giantex, ALFORDSON, PORIYA). Margin only exists where:
- Amazon prices are anchored by higher-spec or branded goods (dual-motor desks, full-size recliners, designer TV units); or
- the item carries a hygiene premium over used and duty is Free (mattresses), with tight FOB negotiation.
