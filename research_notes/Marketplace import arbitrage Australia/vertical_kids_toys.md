## Q1. Can stock land before Christmas 2026, or should the plan target later windows?

### Takeaway
Only goods that are already built and already have compliance documents can realistically land in time.
- **Stock goods:** pay the deposit around 16 Oct, they leave the factory around 23–26 Oct, and land and clear in Sydney/Melbourne around 22 Nov–5 Dec. That leaves about 3–4.5 selling weeks before 24 Dec.
- **Made-to-order runs (25–45 days):** they leave the factory 10–30 Nov and land around 10 Dec 2026–9 Jan 2027. That misses most of the Christmas peak and collides with Boxing Day discounting.

Plan the first real buy for **Christmas 2027**: order July–August 2027 and land in mid-to-late October. Options before then:
- **Easter 2027:** an order is feasible only if placed by about mid-December 2026, because of the Chinese New Year shutdown around 6 Feb 2027.
- **Non-seasonal nursery lines** (high chairs, playpens, strollers) can launch any time.

  - Sea freight is "a minimum of 30–40 days for a door to door shipment from China to Australia".
  - Air freight is 8–10 days.

  — [Freightos](https://www.freightos.com/freight-resources/shipping-from-china-to-australia/)

### Inferences

**Timeline from today (Fri 9 Oct 2026).** Dates are estimates built from the Freightos 30–40 days door-to-door rule of thumb plus the production lead times in the brief.

| Path | Deposit and approval | Leaves factory | Lands and clears (Syd/Mel) | Christmas selling time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Factory stock (7–10 days to pack), existing EN71/ASTM reports | ~16 Oct | ~23–26 Oct | ~22 Nov–5 Dec | ~3–4.5 weeks |
| A2. Factory stock but needing new AU tests | ~16 Oct | ~23 Oct (held for tests) | Tests take ~3–5 weeks (estimate), so landing ~mid-Dec | ~1 week, which is not viable |
| B. Made-to-order, 25 days | ~16 Oct | ~10 Nov | ~10–20 Dec | ~0–2 weeks |
| B2. Made-to-order, 45 days | ~16 Oct | ~30 Nov | ~30 Dec–9 Jan 2027 | None; lands into Boxing Day clearance |

- **Black Friday (27 Nov 2026)** temporarily lowers the floor at Kmart, Amazon and the dropship brands just as Path A stock lands. Expect to price at or below the promotional floor for the first two weeks.
- **Recommended windows:**
  1. **Christmas 2027:** order July to early August 2027. Allow 30–45 days of production and ship before China's National Day "Golden Week" (1–7 Oct, a general-knowledge inference). Land mid-to-late October and list from early November.
  2. **Easter 2027 (28 Mar):** production must finish before the factory shutdown around Chinese New Year (6 Feb 2027). The shutdown typically runs about late Jan to mid/late Feb; that span is an inference, not sourced. So order by about 10–15 Dec 2026 and ship by about 20 Jan 2027.
  3. **Post-Christmas, January–February 2027:** weak for toys, since Boxing Day clearances depress floors. Better suited to nursery lines.
  4. **EOFY (June 2027):** furniture-type kids' items (tables, high chairs, cubbies). Order March–April 2027.
- **Christmas 2026 strategy if insisting:** limit it to 1–2 SKUs that are in factory stock, are not lithium, and carry current EN71/ASTM reports. Examples are a 12V ride-on with an SLA battery and a trampoline with existing AS 4989/EN 71 test data. Ship by LCL or a 20ft container by about 26 Oct.

### Gaps
- No live China–Australia lane freight rate or port-to-port transit time was obtained; the SCFI route values are not public. Container ship schedules (Shanghai/Ningbo to Sydney/Melbourne, typically about 2–3 weeks port-to-port) were not verified.
- No sourced Chinese New Year factory-closure length was found; the late Jan–late Feb closure is an inference.
- No Google Trends or Marketplace seasonality curves for ride-ons, trampolines or bikes were accessible.

## Q2. Who sets the price floor?

### Takeaway
There are two floor-setters. The specific prices behind this summary are in the Findings below.
- **Dropshipzone-style house brands** (Rigo ride-ons, Keezi kids furniture, sandpits, kitchens and climbers, Everfit trampolines) and similar wholesaler brands (Mazam, Kahuna). They are listed at near-identical prices across Toys"R"Us AU, Amazon AU, Bunnings Marketplace, Myer and Kmart Marketplace.
- **Kmart Group's Anko house brand** (prices observed at Target AU). It sets very low floors for nursery items, play kitchens, scooters, small swing sets and activity tables.

By category:
- **Ride-ons:** the effective floor for a 12V ride-on with remote is about $150–165, and Amazon AU 12V models with a remote list around $209–$319.
- **Trampolines:** the floor is Bestway/Everfit (8ft $233.99, 14ft $443.99–$486.99). Lifespan, Plum, Vuly and Playsafe are premium anchors at $399–$869.
- **Not observed:** Aldi had no kids' Special Buys from 7 to 21 Oct 2026. Big W, Kmart direct, Temu, Kogan and eBay could not be observed.

### Inferences
- **Dropship brands are the real "Amazon floor".** Rigo, Keezi and Everfit SKUs appear on both Amazon AU and Toys"R"Us at similar prices; this is inferred from the Rigo listings on both. Their prices sit only about 25–45% above a direct importer's landed-plus-GST cost. Undercutting them by 10–20% therefore squeezes margins to single digits on commodity SKUs: 12V ride-ons, kitchens, tables, Pikler sets.
- **Kmart/Anko wins wherever it competes:** high chairs from $45, playpens $99–145, play kitchens $75–149, tri scooters $20–39, 2-in-1 swing sets $79. Imports can only win where they are clearly better spec (for example a 3-in-1 high chair with tray at $119 against the Anko 2-in-1 at $149) or bigger or bulkier.
- **Bulk is the Marketplace edge.**
  - Bulky, heavy items carry high delivery costs and long dropship lead times: 14ft trampolines (about 56 kg), 2-seat UTVs (about 31 kg, 0.26 CBM), cubbies and swing sets. Local pickup with immediate availability is the differentiator there.
  - Marketplace already shows many "delivery only, 2–8 business days" dropship listings, so "in stock, pick up today" is the positioning.
- **Premium anchors leave room** to price below a brand with like-for-like specs (enclosure, ladder, galvanised frame) in trampolines ($399–$869), cubbies ($629+ Lifespan) and timber swing sets ($429–$499). The safety and liability expectations of those buyers are higher, though.

### Gaps
- Amazon AU cheapest-delivered prices, sellers and delivery charges could not be observed for any category except ride-ons, and those only via snippets.
- Kmart, Big W, Kogan, Temu and eBay floors could not be observed. Kmart's own Anko prices are assumed similar to Target's Anko prices, which is not verified.
- Aldi's late-October and November toy Special Buys were not yet published.
- No Dropshipzone wholesale (trade) prices were obtained; the site's search page is JavaScript-rendered.
- There are no Marketplace sold prices or time-to-sell data for kids' items.

## Q3. Chinese clusters and named factories (FOB at realistic MOQs)

### Takeaway
The factories found sit in recognisable clusters:
- **Pingxiang and Xingtai (Hebei):** bikes, balance bikes and 6–12V ride-ons at the lowest FOB. Hebei Boyi Toy and Pingxiang Songma both list addresses in Pingxiang County.
- **Jiaxing/Pinghu (Zhejiang) and Shenzhen-listed sellers:** 24V and 2-seat ride-ons.
- **Lishui, Yongkang and Jinhua (Zhejiang):** trampolines, scooters, hoverboards and e-scooters.
- **Ningbo and Wenzhou (Zhejiang), Heze (Shandong) and Xiamen (Fujian):** timber play (kitchens, cubbies, sandpits, climbers, tables).
- **Zhongshan (Guangdong), Ningbo and Tianjin:** nursery products.

Realistic MOQs run from 10–100 units for most lines, rising to 300–500 for the cheapest timber, nursery and pram suppliers. Several high-ranking listings are trading companies operating from office towers, so verify manufacturer status before paying a deposit.

## Q4. Compliance: what blocks each product, at what cost and lead time?

### Takeaway
- **Heaviest compliance burden:**
  - **Hoverboards and kids' e-scooters:** federal hoverboard standard, NSW certification of vehicle, battery and charger, and lithium dangerous-goods shipping.
  - **Prams/strollers:** mandatory AS/NZS 2088-based standard.
  - **Playpens marketed for sleep:** these count as folding cots under the Infant Sleep Products Safety Standard 2024, fully in force since about 19 Jan 2026.
  - **Pedal bikes with a wheelbase of 640 mm or more:** mandatory AS/NZS 1927:1998.
- **Moderate burden:**
  - **Electric ride-ons:** toy standards, plus the ≤36-month small-parts standard if marketed to toddlers. Add the button-battery standard if the remote or a sound module uses coin cells, EESS/RCM rules for the charger, and trade-mark risk on licensed lookalikes.
  - **Painted wooden toys** (play kitchens, Pikler sets): the lead and heavy-metals standard applies, tested to AS/NZS ISO 8124.3.
- **Lighter burden:**
  - **Trampolines:** AS 4989:2015 is current but voluntary; no Commonwealth mandatory instrument was found.
  - **Cubbies, swing sets, sandpits, high chairs and kids' tables:** no mandatory product standard found; liability and voluntary testing still apply.
- **Duty:** zero with a ChAFTA certificate of origin.

### Cited Findings
**Commonwealth mandatory standards in force** (Federal Register of Legislation, queried via API 9 Oct 2026):
- **Toys for children up to and including 36 months:**
  - Consumer Goods (Toys for Children up to and including 36 Months of Age) Safety Standard 2023, F2023L01185. It commences the day after registration and has an 18-month transitional period.
  - Compliance routes: AS/NZS ISO 8124.1:2023, or alternatives EN 71-1:2014+A1:2018, ISO 8124-1 or ASTM F963-17.
  - Electric toys: AS/NZS 62115:2018.
  - A children's toy is anything "manufactured, designed, labelled or marketed as a plaything for a child up to and including 36 months".

  — [F2023L01185](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2023L01185/latest/text)
- **Lead and certain elements in children's toys:**
  - Consumer Protection Notice No. 1 of 2009, F2009L00223, in force from 1 Jan 2010.
  - Test standard: AS/NZS ISO 8124.3:2003.
  - Excluded from scope: "sporting goods, camping goods, bicycles, home and public playground equipment, trampolines…".

  — [F2009L00223](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2009L00223/latest/text)
- **Button/coin batteries:**
  - Consumer Goods (Products Containing Button/Coin Batteries) Safety Standard 2020, F2020L01656, plus the Information Standard 2020, F2020L01657. There are also standards for the batteries themselves (F2020L01658 and F2020L01659).
  - Core requirement: a secure battery compartment resistant to opening by young children, batteries not released under foreseeable use or misuse, plus compliance testing and warnings.

  — [F2020L01656](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2020L01656/latest/text); [F2020L01657](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2020L01657/latest/text)
- **Projectile toys and magnets:** Consumer Goods (Projectile Toys) Safety Standard 2020 (F2020L00687) and Toys Containing Magnets 2020 (F2020L01074). Only relevant if accessories are bundled. — [F2020L00687](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2020L00687/latest/text)
- **Pedal bicycles:**
  - Trade Practices Act Consumer Protection Notice No. 6 of 2004, F2005B01053, still **in force**. It applies AS/NZS 1927:1998 *Pedal bicycles—Safety requirements* (with variations).
  - Exclusions include "bicycles having a wheel base of less than 640mm", bikes designed to be hinged or folded, power-assisted bicycles and second-hand bikes.

   - Against: injury liability (needs insurance and an AS 4989-tested model), sharply seasonal (Nov–Dec, plus summer), and Everfit/Bestway price cuts at Black Friday.
2. **24V 2-seat UTV (6):**
   - For: high ticket and bulky; the 24V 2-seat spec is above most $150–$320 12V floors.
   - Against: electronics defect and return risk; toy, electric-toy and charger compliance; trading-company supplier (Zhehua); avoid licensed badges unless a licence covers AU; Christmas-peaked.
3. **Timber cubby (6):**
   - For: bulky, low regulatory burden (no mandatory standard; excluded from the lead standard), year-round with spring and Christmas peaks.
   - Against: the AU low floor (Keezi, Kmart, Bunnings) was **not observed**, assembly and quality complaints, timber biosecurity, and MOQ 300 at the cheapest maker.
4. **3-in-1 high chair (5):**
   - For: no mandatory standard, non-seasonal, compact.
   - Against: competes with Anko at $45–149, fall/tip liability for infants, trader supplier at MOQ 50.
5. **Tractor (5):**
   - For: about 17% under Rigo's tractor at $179, with 29% steady-state GP.
   - Against: small niche and Christmas-peaked.
6. **12V ride-on (4):** the most saturated line (Mazam, Rigo, Kahuna, Honey Joy, Kmart Marketplace sellers). Margin only works at or above the cheapest floors.
7. **16" bike (4):** good modelled margin, but a mandatory standard, assembly liability and probable sub-$100 Kmart/Big W floors (unverified).
8. **Playpen (4):** works only with the cheapest supplier (Memizo, established 2023, unaudited). Must avoid sleep claims or it falls under the infant sleep standard.
9. **Stroller (4):** margin only at a 500-unit MOQ (about A$54k cash), under a mandatory standard and with high liability.
10. **Sandpit, balance bike and swing set (3):** sandpit is below the band and Keezi sits close to import cost; balance bike faces Globber and Kinderfeets anchors with a weak margin; swing set needs 300 units and A$96k at about 13%.
11. **Pikler set, kids table and play kitchen (2):** Keezi and Anko floors leave no margin.
12. **Lithium e-mobility (2):** certification cost, NSW approval regime, DG freight and fire liability. Viable only at 500+ units with existing UL 2272 or AS/NZS 60335.2.114 reports.
13. **Kick scooter (1):** below band, and Anko sits at $20–39.

### Gaps
- Amazon AU cheapest-delivered prices (price, seller, delivery cost) are missing for all non-ride-on SKUs. The "price to beat" uses the cheapest observed AU online listing instead.
- Freight, test and inspection costs are assumptions; see the sensitivity columns. If freight is at the high case (A$135/CBM), trampoline GP falls about A$12 and UTV GP about A$13. LCL for small first orders cuts GP by A$13–40 on bulky items.
- Per-size trampoline FOB and 14ft carton CBM are estimates. Nanjian lists 0.194 CBM and 56 kg; Telamon 0.532 CBM and 30 kg. The 0.25 CBM used could be 0.4; at 0.4 CBM, GP falls about A$18 (freight plus handling).
- Demand velocity (units per week on Marketplace) is unknown for every SKU, so payback-in-units cannot be converted into payback time.
- Product liability insurance premiums, warranty and returns handling, and Marketplace scam and no-show costs are not included.
