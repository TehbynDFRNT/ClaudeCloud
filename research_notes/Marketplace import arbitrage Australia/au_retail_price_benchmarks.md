# AU retail price benchmarks ("price to beat") for new goods resold on Facebook Marketplace at AUD $100–$400

Scope and method:
- All prices are AUD for new items, observed 2026-10-09 unless a date is given. Dated deals are sale prices, not everyday prices.
- Live retailer data came from:
  - Amazon.com.au Best Sellers pages. These show the lowest current offer ("N offers from $X") and rating counts. The ranking is marketplace-wide. "Offers from" excludes delivery and can include non-new offers.
  - Aldi Special Buys pages.
  - Public Shopify catalogues (products.json) of Advwin, Everfit, Gardeon, Artiss, Devanti (the last four are New Aim brands), Lifespan Fitness, Lifespan Kids, Sihoo and Desky.
  - Search pages of Super Retail Group stores (BCF, Supercheap Auto, Rebel). Prices came from embedded product JSON.
  - OzBargain tag RSS feeds, giving dated deal prices.
- Kmart, Kogan, eBay AU, Bunnings, Big W, Gumtree, camelcamelcamel and OzBargain HTML returned HTTP 403 or timed out.
  - Kmart prices below are search-engine snippets, marked "(snippet)". They were not live-verified.
- Temu renders prices client-side, so none could be read.
- Facebook Marketplace requires a login (https://www.facebook.com/marketplace/sydney/search/?query=adjustable%20dumbbells), so no Marketplace asking prices were collected.
- Amazon search results were served as "Deliver to United States", which hides AU-warehoused bulky stock. They were therefore not used. Changing the postcode triggered a bot-check page, which was not bypassed.
- The session-wide web-search budget ran out partway through. Catch.com.au no longer exists (see Q2).

## Q1. What is the realistic floor price on Amazon AU (new, delivered) for each item, and who are the dominant sellers?

### Takeaway
For most bulky "Marketplace-type" goods, Amazon AU's floor is set by Chinese-brand FBA sellers and AU importers, usually with free Prime delivery:
- Walking pads: about $180–$210.
- Magnetic rowers: about $255–$360.
- Power racks: about $270.
- 12V kids' ride-ons: about $140–$280.
- Large cat trees: about $90–$130.
- Gazebos: about $99–$180.
- 4-piece wicker sets: from about $330.

Prime Day and other sale events push these about 20–45% lower, for example a walking pad at $159 instead of about $199, and a spin bike at $260 instead of from $470. Branded categories (coffee machines, robot vacuums, air fryers, BBQs) have floors set by name brands on promotion, not generics.

- Largest likely convenience premium: trampolines, 4–7 piece outdoor sets, massage chairs, power racks and benches, treadmills and rowers, and big ride-ons (24V, 2-seat). These are heavy, need assembly, and face $45–$130+ delivery and 1–15 day transit from importers.
- A local seller could plausibly price at or slightly above the online item price and still beat the delivered total. This is inference only and should be validated with live Marketplace listings.
- Smallest premium: walking pads, office chairs, cat trees, standing desks and air fryers, where Amazon Prime offers free, fast delivery near $150–$200.

### Gaps
- No direct "brand new" Marketplace asking prices were collected for any product. This is the main missing input; a manual logged-in Marketplace scan is needed.
- No data was found on buyer willingness to pay for same-day pick-up in AU.

## Q4. Which items have expensive or slow delivery for bulky goods, giving local Marketplace sellers an edge?

### Takeaway
Delivery is charged separately and by postcode for almost every bulky importer item. Examples: Advwin (all states, postcode-based), New Aim brands (courier by weight; Allied Express over 22kg), Sihoo ($15–$50 flat by state), Desky ($39.95), and Kmart big-and-bulky (no free delivery). Quoted bulky charges run from about $45 for a gazebo to about $100–$130 for treadmills, large trampolines and massage recliners. Remote and regional transit can take up to 15 business days.

Artiss says "Delivery fees are charged based on your location, size and weight of your order" ([Artiss](https://www.artiss.com.au/policies/shipping-policy)).

## Q5. What are the trade prices of Australian dropship wholesalers (Dropshipzone etc.) whose brands flood Marketplace and eBay?

### Takeaway
Dropshipzone does not publish trade prices; the catalogue shows "Price: Sign up or Login to use this feature". Its parent New Aim owns the brands most visible on Amazon AU, Kmart Marketplace and eBay: Artiss, Everfit, Gardeon, i.Pet, Keezi, Rigo, Devanti, Weisshorn and Cefito. New Aim publishes retail list prices on its brand sites. These run well above those brands' typical marketplace prices, so marketplace prices (not brand-site prices) indicate what competing resellers can profitably sell at.

### Cited Findings
- New Aim's site lists its brands as Artiss, Devanti, Gardeon, Everfit, Giselle Bedding, Cefito, i.Pet, Keezi, Jingle Jollys, Green Fingers, Rigo and Weisshorn. It claims "1 in 2 Aussies own a New Aim product" and calls itself "one of Australia's leading dropshipping service providers" ([New Aim](https://www.newaim.com.au/)).
