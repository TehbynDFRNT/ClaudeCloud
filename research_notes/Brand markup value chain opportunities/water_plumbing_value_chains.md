## Q0. Cross-cutting barriers: which certifications gate each product, what they cost, and whether a private label can ride someone else's certificate

### Takeaway
Three separate national gates stack on these products, and each one has a "brand endorsement" route that lets a small operator ride a manufacturer's certificate instead of holding its own:
- **WaterMark** covers anything plumbed to drinking water. That includes UV units for drinking water, RO units, softeners and scale "conditioners", tapware, valves, backflow devices, hoses and water heaters.
- **EESS/RCM** covers anything that plugs in. Level 3 needs a third-party Certificate of Conformity: element water heaters, pool and spa equipment (pumps, chlorinators, pool UV), and submersible bore and tank pumps. Level 2 needs only self-declared registration and a compliance folder: surface pressure pumps, drinking-water UV and, from 12 Feb 2027, element-free heat-pump water heaters.
- **The Clean Energy Regulator register** must list a heat-pump or solar water heater before it creates STCs.

On top of these sit WELS (tapware, toilets, showers) and the lead-free brass rule. Lead-free bites hardest on tapware, valves and backflow devices, where brass is in contact with drinking water.

On cost, government fees are trivial: WaterMark A$525 per licence per year, EESS A$239.74 per supplier per year plus A$89.85–449.25 per registration, and WELS A$600 a year for up to 5 models. The real cost and delay are in the certifier (CAB) and lab work: months, with quotes not published. All three systems let a brand or trade name sit on an existing certificate or licence, so "private label riding" is a structural feature, not a loophole. Licence 080071 (PWF family, five brands) is the proof case.

### Cited Findings
- **WaterMark scope for treatment products (Schedule of Products 2026-2):**
  - "Water filters and water treatment appliances" covers POU/POE drinking-water systems "including … ultraviolet treatment systems, and reverse osmosis systems". The spec is AS 3497:2021, and lead-free applies.
  - "Water conditioner" ("in line or end of line water treatment devices for conditioning of water and prevention of scaling") is also AS 3497:2021.
  - UV "for non-drinking water purposes, i.e., bathing" and other non-drinking treatment fall under WMTS-103.
  - (Fetched by the earlier Purezzo round before the ABCB robots.txt bar was noticed; not re-fetched.) — [WaterMark Schedule of Products 2026-2 (PDF), as quoted in the Purezzo compliance notes](https://watermark.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2026/WaterMark%20Schedule%20of%20Products%202026-2_0.pdf)
- **Greywater:** the Schedule of Excluded Products 2026-2 lists "Domestic grey water treatment systems — PCA". These are regulated under the Plumbing Code and state approvals, not WaterMark. — [Schedule of Excluded Products 2026-2 (PDF), as quoted in the Purezzo notes](https://watermark.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2026/WaterMark%20Schedule%20of%20Excluded%20Products%202026-2.pdf)
- **Lead-free, national timetable:** the Schedule says that from 1 May 2026 only lead-free-conforming products may be certified and manufactured by Approved Users, and that "From 1 May 2028 only products WaterMark certified as conforming to the lead free requirements … will be authorised for use in plumbing installations". The 2026-2 revision moved that enforcement date from 1 May 2026 to 1 May 2028. — [Schedule 2026-2 as quoted in the Purezzo notes](https://watermark.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2026/WaterMark%20Schedule%20of%20Products%202026-2_0.pdf)
- **Lead-free in Victoria:** the regulator says that from 1 May 2026 "all plumbing products installed by plumbing practitioners that contain copper alloy that are in contact with drinking water must comply". The limit is a weighted-average lead content of no more than 0.25% (PCA 2025 cl. A5G4), and the trigger is the start of plumbing installation. Exempt: "non-potable fixtures (e.g. toilets, irrigation pumps, dishwashers) and firefighting equipment and showerheads". — [BPC Victoria (fetched 2026-10-10)](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/resource-hub/industry-updates/new-lead-free-plumbing-product-requirements)
- **Lead-free in Queensland (water utilities):** "from 1 May 2026 … non-lead-free products cannot be installed in drinking water systems", affecting "water meters, meter assemblies, valves, fittings and other brass/bronze components". — [qldwater eFlash #639](https://qldwater.com.au/e-flashes-1/eflash-639)
- **Conflict to flag:** Victoria and the utilities treat 1 May 2026 as the installation cut-off, while the national WaterMark schedule (2026-2) authorises installation of non-LF-certified stock until 1 May 2028. — sources above; see also [Outdoor Design guide, 12 Feb 2026](https://www.outdoordesign.com.au/news-info/your-guide-to-the-2026-lead-free-plumbing-requirement/11060), which says "only certified lead free products will be permitted after the deadline" of 1 May 2026.
- **EESS fees from 1 July 2026:** responsible-supplier registration **A$239.74 a year**. Equipment registration is **A$89.85 (1 yr) / A$179.70 (2 yrs) / A$449.25 (5 yrs)**, GST-free. — [EESS Registration Fees (fetched 2026-10-10)](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-fees/)
- **EESS Level 3 rules:**
  - Level 3 equipment needs "a valid and current Certificate of Conformity … issued by a recognised certifier", registration on the National Register by a registered Responsible Supplier, and RCM marking.
  - A renewal "can link to a different certificate so long as the models and Trade Names of the registered equipment are on the new certificate". Trade names are listed on certificates, which is the electrical equivalent of WaterMark brand endorsement.
  - [EESS Level 3 page](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-in-scope-electrical-equipment/level-3/)
- **Level 3 classes relevant here (ESV equipment classes):**
  - **Water heater:** unvented storage with an element, 4.5–680 L, or instantaneous with live parts in water.
  - **Swimming pool or spa equipment:** pool pumps (AS/NZS 60335.2.41), "UV radiation water treatment appliances" (AS/NZS 60335.2.109) and "Other" (AS/NZS 3136, i.e. chlorinators). It excludes products "exclusively promoted for commercial use" and heat pumps.
  - **Submersible pump:** includes deep-well pumps, excludes shower boost pumps.
  - **Liquid heating appliance:** portable, 10 L or less, for hot beverages.
  - **Refrigerating appliance:** household, "cools and stores food".
  - **Heat pump water heater without a heating element:** **Level 2** from 12/02/2027. HPWHs with an element move into the Level 3 "water heater" definition.
  - [Energy Safe Victoria – equipment classes (fetched 2026-10-10)](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/industry-guidance/electrical/appliances-equipment-and-manufacurers/equipment-classes); [UL Solutions compliance insights Feb 2026](https://au-nz.ul.com/news/compliance-insights-february-2026)
- **STC eligibility (Clean Energy Regulator):**
  - Only models on the register create STCs, and listing requires AS/NZS 2712:2007 certification from an accredited body. The register covers air-source heat pumps with 425 L or less of storage.
  - Model eligibility ends at certificate expiry or 31 Dec 2030, whichever is first.
  - Installed systems must match the listing exactly.
  - Installers need state licences.
  - (Search-result summaries of CER pages.) — [CER manufacturers and importers](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/renewable-energy-target-participants-and-industry/manufacturers-and-importers); [CER solar water heater accreditation](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters/solar-water-heater-accreditation)
- **WELS fees** are tiered by model count: tier 1 (1–5 models) **A$600 a year**, tier 2 (6–10) A$1,100, tier 3 (11–20) A$1,700. They are GST-exempt. (Search snippet; the waterrating.gov.au fetch failed at robots.txt, connection 000, so the year of the table is unconfirmed.) — [WELS fees for registration](https://waterrating.gov.au/node/15)
- **WaterMark cost and time (from the earlier round):** ABCB charges A$525 per licence per year. IAPMO quotes "a few months to 12 months plus". Importers and distributors can be licensees, and brand names can be endorsed onto an existing licensee's certificate. — [Purezzo compliance notes Q2/Q7, citing IAPMO Oceania](https://iapmooceania.org/certification-services/watermark-product-certification)
- **Proof that private labels ride one licence:** WaterMark licence **080071** (expiry 07/08/2029) carries the PWF family under AQUAPRIMUS, Water Filter Direct, AquaOzzy, Aquarius Water Filters and PureFlow. — [Purezzo factory-verification notes Q3](https://watermark.abcb.gov.au/product-search) (search-indexed register entries; the register itself is not fetchable by agents)

### Inferences
- (Inference) For a small operator, the cheapest barrier to "clear" is **brand endorsement on an existing WaterMark licence plus an existing EESS certificate (Level 3) or self-declared registration (Level 2)**. Holding its own licence makes sense only for a hero product, or once volume justifies the CAB audit cycle.
- (Inference) Lead-free makes **brass-heavy categories** (tapware, mixing valves, backflow devices, brass manifolds) more barrier-protected through 2026–2028. Plenty of stock and many certificates must be re-done, which favours suppliers who can show WMLF certificates. Plastic-bodied treatment units are much less exposed.
- (Inference) Under the ESV definitions, drinking-water UV units, surface pressure pumps and under-sink boiling/chilled units appear to fall in **Level 2**, not Level 3:
  - drinking-water UV units, because the only UV class listed is under pool/spa equipment;
  - surface pressure pumps, because only submersible pumps and pool pumps are listed;
  - under-sink boiling/chilled units, because most are vented, not portable and do not "store food".
  This should be confirmed with a RECS certifier before relying on it.
