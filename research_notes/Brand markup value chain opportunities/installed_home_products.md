## Q0. Cross-cutting gatekeepers: which certification level does each installed electrical product sit at, and can a private label ride the OEM's certificate?

### Takeaway
Most installed electrical home products are EESS **Level 3** (certificate of conformity + RCM + database registration): fans (incl. ceiling fans), wall switches, water heaters with elements, pool/spa equipment, air conditioners using flammable refrigerant (R290), and — from about Feb 2028 — EV chargers and home batteries. R32 split systems and LED downlights are **Level 2**. Registration fees are trivial (A$239.74/yr supplier + A$89.85–449.25 per equipment registration from 1 Jul 2026); the real cost is test reports/certificates and the Australian "Responsible Supplier" liability, which can only be held by an AU/NZ entity — i.e. this is exactly the layer a lean brand owner can occupy.

### Cited Findings
- EESS levels (from the regulator's own definitions document v4.2, July 2021, aligned to AS/NZS 4417.2:2020; levels read from the PDF table coordinates on 2026-10-10):
  - **Level 3:** "Air conditioner incorporating flammable refrigerant" (AS/NZS 60335.2.40); "Fan" ("primary function of moving air ... for comfort or extraction", incl. duct fans; AS/NZS 60335.2.80); "Water heater" (storage 4.5–680 L "incorporates a heating element", unvented; or instantaneous); "Swimming pool/spa equipment" (transportable spa pools, pool/spa pumps and equipment — **but excludes "a heat pump"**); "Wall switch" (≤20 A); "Room heater"; "Range hood"; "Light emitting semiconductor lamp (self-ballasted)"; "Power supply or charger"; "Submersible pump"; "Evaporative cooler"; "Refrigerating appliance".
  - **Level 2:** "Air conditioner incorporating non-flammable or low flammable refrigerant" (Class 1 or 2L — covers R32 splits; AS/NZS 60335.2.98 cited); "Recessed Luminaire—light emitting semiconductor type" (LED downlights; excludes "fans with integral luminaires", which fall under Fan); "Simple portable luminaire".
  - Obligations: Level 3 = meet standard + RCM + "valid Australian or New Zealand issued certificate of conformity" + registered in the national EESS database linked to the responsible supplier; Level 2 = RCM + registration + compliance folder producible within 10 days; Level 1 = RCM + documentary evidence + linked to a registered responsible supplier.
  - In-scope = >50 V AC and <1000 V AC, "designed or marketed as suitable for household, personal or similar use" — [EESS In-scope equipment definitions and risk levels v4.2 (PDF, read directly)](https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/EESS-Inscope-Equipment-Definitions-and-Risk-Levels-v4.2-.pdf)
- EESS fees from 1 Jul 2026: Responsible Supplier registration A$239.74/yr; Level 2/3 equipment registration A$89.85 (1 yr), A$179.70 (2 yrs), A$449.25 (5 yrs) — [EESS fees](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-fees/) (as recorded in sibling notes ../Marketplace import arbitrage Australia/, search summary there).
- The Responsible Supplier must be an AU/NZ entity with an ABN (or NZ IRD); overseas companies and "agents" cannot register — [EESS Responsible Supplier](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-responsible-supplier/) (search summary, via sibling notes).
- **AS/NZS 4417.2:2020 Amendment 1:2026** (published 12 Feb 2026) adds B.2.74 EV supply equipment (EVSE), B.2.75 battery energy storage system (BESS) equipment and B.2.78 portable power stations with a **24-month transition** (≈ Feb 2028); EVSE and BESS are described as new **Level 3** categories; the UL note links BESS to SA TS 5398:2025, AS/NZS 4777.2, AS/NZS 62477.1, IEC 62109-1/-2. A JJR Lab summary claiming a 12-month transition conflicts with the official text — [Standards NZ AS/NZS 4417.2:2020 A1](https://www.standards.govt.nz/shop/ASNZS-4417-22020-A1); [Energy Safe Victoria equipment classes](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/industry-guidance/electrical/appliances-equipment-and-manufacurers/equipment-classes); [UL compliance insights Feb 2026](https://au-nz.ul.com/news/compliance-insights-february-2026) (all search summary).

### Inferences
- Because a Level 3 certificate of conformity is issued to the Australian responsible supplier for a specific model, a private label generally needs its *own* certificate (or a re-issue/"family" listing based on the OEM's CB/IEC test reports), not just a copy of the OEM's. Cost is typically a certifier fee on top of existing CB reports (est. low-thousands A$ per model family; not verified in this research — see Gaps).
- A heat-pump water heater with a backup element plausibly falls under "Water heater" (Level 3) for its element, while the heat-pump part is AS/NZS 60335.2.40; I found no explicit EESS line for heat-pump water heaters (inference, needs certifier confirmation).

### Gaps
- Exact certifier fees for Level 3 certificates of conformity riding OEM CB reports were not found in this pass.
- The full AS/NZS 4417.2 Amd 1:2026 category list (paywalled) was not read.

## Q1. Hot-water heat pumps (HWHP) and pool heat pumps: ladder, barrier, clearability, annuity, incumbents, demand, score

### Takeaway
HWHP is the closest analogue to the whole-house-filter thesis and the best fit for a plumber-connected operator: an all-in-one R290 unit lists at ~US$600–830 (listing) and installs at A$3,000–6,000 (typical A$4,200 before rebates), a ~4× ladder, and the gatekeepers (AS/NZS 2712 certification → CER register for STCs → VEU/ESS product registers, 5-year warranty, WaterMark AS/NZS 3498, plumber + electrician) are paperwork-and-test barriers a brand owner can clear. The proof that a lean brand can own this layer is iStore (Solargain-owned, Chinese-made, 63% of installers' votes in 2026). Pool heat pumps are the same refrigeration product with almost no gatekeeper (no STC/VEU/WaterMark; heat pumps are excluded from the Level 3 pool-equipment class) and an even steeper supply-only ladder (~5×), but correspondingly crowded with importer brands.

### Inferences
- **Ladder (HWHP, est.):** FOB ~US$700 ≈ A$1,006 → landed ex GST ~A$1,250–1,400 (LCL ~1 m³ at A$244/W/M + share of A$900 shipment costs, 0% ChAFTA duty; est.) → trade/wholesale not visible → installed A$3,000–6,000 (typical A$4,200 before rebates). Installed ÷ FOB ≈ 3–6× (typical ~4.2×). The homeowner assigns the STCs (≈A$500–700) plus any VEU/ESS value to the installer as a point-of-sale discount and the installer redeems them, so the chain's real revenue is the pre-rebate price.
- **Capturable layer:** the brand/registrant layer (between factory and installer). A lean operator who holds the AS/NZS 2712 certificate, CER listing, VEU/ESS registration and a 5-yr Australian warranty can sell direct to plumbers at a trade price (est. A$1,800–2,400 ex GST, inference from the installed spread) or run consultation sales with partner plumbers and keep both the brand and installer margin.
- **Clearability:** moderate. Certification + TRNSYS modelling + register rounds (≥2/yr) mean 6–12 months to first STC-eligible sale (est.); the register shows that one OEM-certified model can be listed under several brand names (PHNIX ↔ iStore; Aquatech/Aquatherm/Ecotherm/Hydrotherm; Neopower/SolarBuild), so a private label can plausibly be added to an OEM's existing AS/NZS 2712 licensing schedule rather than re-testing from scratch — the mechanics/fees of adding a brand were not found.
- **Annuity:** anode replacement, air-filter cleaning, 5-year warranty service calls; modest.
- **Liability:** water damage/scalding (tempering valve), refrigerant (R290 flammable), plus regulator clawback of certificates for poor installs — real but insurable.
- **Pool HP:** ladder ~5.2× supply-only (A$4,129 retail ÷ ~A$790 FOB for a 10 kW unit, est.), barrier low (RCM only, probably EESS Level 1, inference), fits plumber/pool-builder channel + electrician for the circuit; but low barrier = many importer brands (Madimack, EvoHeat, etc.), so margin defence relies on service and warranty rather than certification.
- **Score (1–5, 5 = best for the operator):** HWHP — ladder 4, capturable layer 4, clearability 3, annuity 2, liability 3, incumbent risk 2–3 (137 brands already registered, incl. Chinese OEMs under their own names — PHNIX, Midea, TCL, Haier, Gree — plus Rheem/Rinnai/Dux/Reece-Thermann/iStore), channel fit 5 (plumber-installed). Pool HP — ladder 5, layer 4, clearability 5, annuity 2, liability 4, incumbent risk 3, channel fit 4.

## Q2. Split/ducted air conditioning: ladder, barrier, clearability, annuity, incumbents, demand, score

### Takeaway
A 2.5–3.5 kW R32 inverter split lists at ~US$138–188 (listing) and sells supply-only at ~A$799 (Hisense 2.5 kW) and A$1,200–1,800 installed (2.5 kW) or ~A$2,000–2,875 for a typical 5 kW install, so the ladder is ~5–8× FOB, but most of the spread is installer labour (A$600–1,200) and the brand layer is defended by GEMS registration, EESS Level 2 (R32) / Level 3 (R290), a 5-year warranty with national service, and ARC refrigerant licensing. The capturable layer for a lean operator is the installer/consultation layer (ducted and multi-head jobs at A$4,200–17,000+), not a new AC brand. Needs ARC-licensed installers (many plumber-and-gas firms hold them) plus an electrician.

### Inferences

## Q3. Home batteries (and solar): ladder, barrier, clearability, annuity, incumbents, demand, score

### Takeaway
Home batteries are the archetype of a list-protected layer (CEC approved products list required for the Cheaper Home Batteries rebate, accredited installers, DNSP rules, EESS Level 3 from ~Feb 2028), with a big ladder (rack LiFePO4 lists at ~US$130/kWh vs ~A$1,080/kWh installed after rebate for a 10 kWh system) and explosive demand (500,000 installs by Aug 2026). But the list is already crowded with Chinese brands, the rebate factor steps down every six months from May 2026, and 8,846 accredited installers compete — so the capturable layer for a newcomer is a thin retailer/installer margin, and it needs electricians, not plumbers.

### Cited Findings

**Top 3 — first steps**
1. **Hot-water heat pumps (brand/registrant + plumber install + consultation sale)**
   - Shortlist OEMs that already hold AU-registered models. The register shows PHNIX (58 models) plus newer entrants such as MUYANG, NEWNTIDE, AMITIME, GOODHEAT and KINGHOME; whether each is a factory or a trader must still be verified.
   - Ask each for its AS/NZS 2712 licensing schedule and WaterMark (AS/NZS 3498) certificate, and ask whether it will add your brand to an existing certified model, as it did for iStore, Evoheat, Oasis and ThermalArk. Get the cost and timing, and aim for an early-2027 register round, since round 60 closes 2 Nov 2026.
   - Register as an EESS Responsible Supplier. Confirm with a certifier whether element-boosted units need a Level 3 water-heater certificate of conformity.
   - Set up a 5-year Australian warranty and service arrangement (a VEU requirement), then apply to the VEU Register of Products and check ESS listing rules.
   - Recruit 3–5 partner plumbers (with electricians), and use integrated monoblock units to avoid ARC refrigerant handling.
   - Pilot consultation sales to existing whole-house-filter customers, bundled with filtration.
2. **Pool and spa heat pumps (fast start, same supply base)**
   - Sample 10–17 kW inverter R32/R290 pool units from listed OEMs. Get RCM evidence and confirm the EESS level with a certifier: heat pumps are excluded from the Level 3 pool-equipment class.
   - Price under Madimack (A$4,129 for 10 kW).
   - Sell through pool builders, pool shops and plumbers, and offer a 5-year warranty.
   - Use it to fund the HWHP certification timeline and to cross-sell "pool + hot water" electrification.
3. **Portable spas and plunge pools with a consumables annuity**
   - Shortlist OEMs whose listings claim SAA certificates, for example Guangzhou Joyee ("valid since 2022-09-16"). Check the certificate holder and model on the EESS database, then obtain your own Level 3 registration and certificate of conformity.
   - Benchmark Spa World and online importers on price; this was a gap in this pass.
   - Design a sanitiser, filter-cartridge, cover and servicing subscription that draws on the operator's water-treatment know-how.
   - Partner electricians for 15–32 A circuits, and issue state pool/spa barrier checklists covering ≥300 mm water depth, council approval and AS 1926.1 covers.
   - Ship 40HQ loads to keep per-unit freight down.
- **Alternate #4: plantation shutters and security screens.** Pick this if the operator wants a consultation category that needs no certification. It needs a measure-and-install crew, AS 5039 testing for screens, and NSW Class 2C licensing for security doors in NSW.
