# Who actually makes China's bumper plates, cast-iron plates, kettlebells and 40 kg dial dumbbells (factories vs resellers)

*Research date: 10 Oct 2026. Scope: rubber/urethane Olympic bumper plates first; cast-iron plates, kettlebells and 40 kg dial-adjustable dumbbells second. No supplier was contacted. Access notes: ImportYeti company/supplier pages returned HTTP 403 to both curl and WebFetch, so all ImportYeti facts below come from search-engine summaries of those pages (counts may be partial; several pages were marked ~680–740 days old). Google Patents robots.txt allows only /patent/ pages, so patents were read one page at a time and further rights-holders were mined from the cited-by / similar-document tables on those pages. Qichacha, Tianyancha and GSXT were not used: they were blocked in the earlier round, and no CAPTCHA was attempted. The shared WebSearch quota ran out mid-task (after ~35 searches by this researcher); the rest of the work used direct URL fetches only. Evidence grades follow the brief: A = registry production scope with industrial address and insured headcount, customs record as direct shipper to a Western brand, a certificate naming the manufacturing site, or a patent holding; B = own site with ICP, licence image and production footage, association membership, a trade-show booth as manufacturer, or being named on a Western brand's label; C = platform badges or self-description only.*

## Q1. Where is the real production, and which companies demonstrably own the presses, moulds and foundries?

### Takeaway
Customs data and patents point to a few real producers: Nantong (Jiangsu: Chongchuan, Tongzhou, Rudong) and Qingdao/Jimo (Shandong) for plates, and Zhejiang (Yongkang, Wuyi, Jinhua, Ningbo) for adjustable-dumbbell tooling. Many storefronts that call themselves manufacturers sit in office towers in Rizhao, Linyi, Qingdao and Dingzhou, or describe someone else's factory. Customs records name the direct shippers to US brands, but the shipment lines that would show which shipper makes rubber bumpers could not be read. I found no supplier for which a vulcanising-press line could be independently verified.

### Inferences
- **Identical SKUs and plate-maker counts.** The same bumper SKU on many storefronts fits a small number of plants supplying many sellers. Many of the storefronts are office-based traders: Rizhao International Building, Blue Innovation World (Modun/Leadman), "Eastern Worldview" in Linyi (Dx Grandway, Q3), and Dingzhou village or residential rooms. The plants are probably in the Qingdao/Jimo rubber belt, Xintai/Tai'an and Nantong. The number of actual rubber-plate moulders could not be counted.
- **Shippers that are probably factories.** Nantong shippers with town or industrial addresses (Chahe, Shuangdian, Tongzhou), and Qingdao DH in Jimo, are more likely producers. Shippers at city-centre office addresses (Gongnong Road, Renmin East Road, office plazas) are more likely export or trading companies, even though they ship directly to Western brands.
- **Cast iron.** Dingzhou is the domestic cast-iron small-weights cluster. The MIC-facing Dingzhou firms seen here are traders or small finishers, so the foundries behind them were not identified. Phuong Anh Casting appearing in a Western brand's supply chain suggests some casting has moved to Vietnam.

## Q2. Candidate factory profiles and evidence grades

### Takeaway
Two companies meet the "two independent A/B items" bar as factories and are relevant to plates:
- Nantong Ironman (南通铁人运动用品有限公司), on two A items and a B item;
- Nantong Tengtai, on an A item and two B items.

Neither one proves that it moulds rubber bumper plates in-house, and Tengtai says plates come through its trading team. Nantong Modern Sporting Industrial and Nantong Tianlong also clear the bar (A customs plus B trade-show booth), but their plate output is unverified. The best customs-only plate leads (one A item each) are Nantong Zhaoyang Sports Goods and Qingdao Winning Rubber Technology.

## Q4. The 40 kg dial-adjust dumbbell: who owns the design, who makes it, who resells it, and why do listings show the same unit?

### Takeaway

## Q5. Realism check: will a real factory take a first 15–21 t single-container order, at what price per kg, and on what terms?

### Inferences
- **Container orders from a new buyer.** Nothing seen suggests these suppliers serve only traders or 40HQ brand buyers. Plates are weight-limited, so a 40HQ carries no more weight than a 20GP under Australian road limits; a full 20GP is the normal plate order. A 15–21 t first order sits in the top published tier at the suppliers that publish tiers.
- **Realistic price band (inference).**
  - About US$1.00–1.30/kg FOB for standard black or colour recycled/virgin-blend bumpers with steel inserts, at ≥10 t.
  - US$1.30–1.70/kg for virgin rubber, stainless hubs, tighter tolerance or urethane.
  - Platform list prices do not show a systematic trader premium: traders Grandway, Dili and Shangshuo price at or below the "factories". So the real factory-direct saving can only be found through competing RFQs to verified moulders, compared spec-for-spec.
- **Required diligence (inference).**
  - Pay the 30% deposit only to the entity named on the export contract and B/L.
  - Hold the 70% balance until an independent pre-shipment inspection checks weight tolerance, hub bonding and odour/PAH test reports.
  - Ask the "factory" for a video of its own press line and its business licence 经营范围, to rule out a Tengtai-style consolidator.
