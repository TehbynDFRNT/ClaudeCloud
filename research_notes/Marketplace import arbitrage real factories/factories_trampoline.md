# Who actually makes 12–14 ft enclosure trampolines in China (factories vs traders), and will one take a 128-unit first order?

_Research date: 2026-10-10. All pages were fetched on that date unless noted. WebSearch ran out of quota part-way through; after that, only direct URLs were used, never search engines._

**Evidence grades used.** These follow the brief:
- **A** = official disclosure (registry or stock exchange); customs records showing the company as the direct shipper to Western brands; a certificate naming the site; or patent holder.
- **B** = own site with ICP, trade-show booth as manufacturer, association membership, or being named on a brand label. Third-party-verified platform fields are also graded B in these notes.
- **C** = platform badges or self-description only.

A company is called a **factory** only with two independent A/B items. ImportGenius and 52wmb both republish the same US CBP manifests, so they count as one item.

## Q1. Where are the real 12–14 ft trampoline factories, and which companies own production?

### Takeaway
**Where the verified factories are.** The trampoline makers that verifiably ship to Western brands sit mainly in **Qingdao (Shandong)** and **Hangzhou-Xiaoshan (Zhejiang)**, plus **Taishan (Guangdong)**:
- **Sportsoul / 青岛三柏硕健康科技股份有限公司** (SZSE 001300, Chengyang District, Qingdao). Its customers are Decathlon, Walmart, Amazon, ICON/iFIT, Plum Products, Dutch Toys Group, Avero, Sportspower and Escalade, plus its own Skywalker brand.
- **Qingdao Jinhuaxing Sports Products** (Huangdao). It ships to Propel, Vuly's US entity and Rural King.
- **Hangzhou Transasia Sporting Goods Manufactory** (Suoqian Town, Xiaoshan). It ships to Acon, JumpSport, Super Jumper and BERG.
- **Hangzhou Entire Sporting Goods / Hangzhou Hengjian Technology** (Xiaoshan EDZ). It ships to JumpSport.
- **Kogee Outdoor Group** (Taishan). It ships to BERG and North Trampoline.

**Where Lingsha fits.** The earlier round's "Lishui/Yongkang/Jinhua" picture holds only for **second-tier** makers. Lingsha (Jinyun County, Lishui) is a genuine factory that ships full containers to US Amazon sellers. The Yongkang/Jinhua names found are mostly small mini-trampoline workshops.

**What "owning production" means here.** Even the market leader buys its springs, jumping mats and nets from specialist suppliers. It also subcontracts part of its galvanising, sewing and welding. "Owning production" in this industry means making the frame in-house: tube-making from galvanised strip, cutting, welding and powder-coating, plus final assembly and packing.

### Inferences
- **Where Western brands' 12–14 ft trampolines come from.** They come overwhelmingly from a handful of container-scale OEMs:
  - Sportsoul and Jinhuaxing in Qingdao
  - Transasia and Entire/Hengjian in Xiaoshan
  - Kogee in Taishan

  Qingdao looks like the single biggest cluster. Sportsoul's port mix (Qingdao "Ching Tao") and Jinhuaxing's address support this.
- **What "owning production" means.** It means making frames: tube mill on pre-galvanised strip, bending, welding, powder coat, sewing, and assembly/packing. Springs, PP mats and PE nets are commodity inputs bought from specialist component makers such as 青岛鑫佰利 (springs), 纤科(珠海) (mats) and 山东凯程 (nets and mats). Galvanising and sewing are partly subcontracted even by the leader.

  A supplier that claims to make everything in-house is unusual. The real question is whether it makes the frame.
- **The same SKU on many storefronts.** This fits a structure in which a few frame makers sell catalogue designs to many traders, Amazon sellers and house brands. I could not prove that a specific trader's listing comes from a specific factory.

### Gaps
- **Chinese registries not reached.** gsxt.gov.cn, qcc and tianyancha were not reached; they sit behind CAPTCHA/login walls and were not attempted. So registered production scope, registered address and insured headcount (参保人数) are missing for every company except Sportsoul (prospectus).
- **Chinese names missing.** Not confirmed for Jinhuaxing, Transasia, Entire/Hengjian or Kogee.
- **Trade-show lists not checked.** No China Sport Show, Canton Fair or Spielwarenmesse exhibitor lists were checked; the search quota was exhausted.
- **1688 storefronts not found.** None were found for any 12–14 ft maker, and there is no 1688 domestic price for 14 ft sets.
- **Customs coverage is US only.** The data covers US imports only, and the "supplier" on a B/L can be a trading company. Australian import B/Ls are not public.
- **Unopened aggregator claims.** The Jinhuaxing claims (founded 1998, JUMPTASTIC trademark, Walmart/Kmart/Carrefour) come from search snippets of aggregators and were not opened.

## Q2. Lingsha, Nanjian and Set Sail: factory, factory's trading arm, or trader? Does Lingsha's TÜV report verify?

### Takeaway
**Lingsha (浙江凌鲨科技有限公司) is a factory.** It meets the bar with two independent A items:
- US customs shows it as exporter of record of full trampoline containers from a Jinyun industrial-park address.
- It holds 2023 utility patents on tube-bending, tube-cutting, laser-welding jigs and a trampoline-cloth tear tester.

MIC fields support this: the Production Capacity block (6 production lines, 6 QA/QC inspectors) is marked "verified by TÜV Rheinland", and the listed machines are "Steel pipe production line, Cutting machine, Pipe bender, Welding machine". I could not confirm that the machine list itself sits inside a verified block.

The caveats:
- It is young (established 2020-10-26).
- Its self-reported headcount and site size are inconsistent.
- Its customers are US cross-border e-commerce importers, not brands.
- Registry and insured-headcount data were not obtained.

**TÜV report.** MIC-ASR2531608 **does verify** on TÜV Rheinland China's portal: "Zhejiang Lingsha Technology Co., Ltd", audit type "Audited Supplier Verification", dated 2025-10-10, open-ended validity. The portal states **no scope, address, grade or file**. Per MIC, the scheme is an on-site authenticity and capability check across eight areas, not a product certificate or social audit.

**Nanjian is a real but tiny maker** (18 staff, 1 line) of mini trampolines and pilates beds. There is no evidence it makes 14 ft sets, so treat its 14 ft offer as likely bought-in.

**Set Sail is a trader:**
- Its address is an office (Jinpeng Mansion, Dingzhou).
- Its catalogue spans boxing, camping, table tennis, track and field and basketball stands; trampolines are not among its main products.
- No US customs record was found.

## Q3. Does the 14 ft enclosure design have an identifiable origin that explains why many storefronts show the same SKU?

### Takeaway
**The design is an unpatented commodity.** The pole-and-net enclosure concept goes back to **JumpSport's 1998–99 US patents** (US6053845A, US6261207B1), both now "Expired – Lifetime". So the round spring trampoline with pole-and-net enclosure is unpatented.

**Why listings look identical.** Many factories build to a converged bill of materials, and traders reuse factory catalogue photos; no single patent-holding originator explains it:
- 14 ft ≈ 80 springs, 4 W-legs, 8 enclosure poles, ~1.8 m net
- one ~172 × 50–55 × 23–26 cm carton of 59–62 kg

**Where proprietary designs exist.** Patented features sit at the brand/OEM top end, such as Sportsoul's "NO-GAP" enclosure and Crown Series. Lingsha's patents cover production equipment only. Model numbers are factory catalogue codes (e.g. Lingsha "FJ-C01" for its whole 6–16 ft range), not a shared convention.

## Q4. Realism check: will a real factory take ~128 units (one 20GP), at what price and on what terms?

### Takeaway
**Two tiers of factory:**
- **Brand OEMs do not sell on small-buyer terms.** Sportsoul publishes **MOQ 500 pieces per item**, "Negotiable" price and T/T payment. It sells FOB on credit terms to brand customers, and its top 5 customers are 71–89% of revenue. Jinhuaxing ships to Propel by the thousand containers. A 128-unit order is outside their published terms.
- **Second-tier factories that sell on MIC take it.** Lingsha does so explicitly:
  - MOQ 100 (50 per size), with 3–4 sizes mixed in a 20GP
  - **30% deposit, 70% against B/L copy** (or LC at sight)
  - **20–45 working days**, US$200 sample
  - **US$96 per 14 ft at 100–499 pcs**

  128 × US$96 = **US$12,288**, so the deposit is about **US$3,686**.

**Is the price realistic?** Sportsoul's audited average selling price for **8–14 ft** trampolines was **US$99.81 (2019), US$110.10 (2020), US$120.00 (2021) and US$114.46 (H1 2022)**. Its average unit cost was **US$74–89**, at 25.6–29.8% gross margin. So a US$96 14 ft from Lingsha is at or below the leader's blended mid-size price, plausible only for a lighter spec. The trader price (Set Sail US$119 for a near-identical carton) is about 24% higher.
