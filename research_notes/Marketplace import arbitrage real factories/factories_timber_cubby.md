# Real factories behind timber (fir/pine) kids' cubby houses and wooden playhouses in China, and what a ~200-unit Australian first order involves

_Researched 2026-10-10. Main evidence: US bills of lading (ImportGenius public supplier and importer pages: robots.txt allows them and they show the top 5 partners plus the 10 latest BoLs) and Made-in-China (MIC) supplier pages, both fetched directly. Blocked or unusable: ImportYeti (403), Google Patents XHR (503), FSC search API (robots.txt disallows /api), agriculture.gov.au (503 or connection reset), BICON (403) and web.archive.org (reset). Qichacha, Tianyancha and GSXT were not attempted (they blocked last round). The shared WebSearch quota ran out mid-task, so the later steps used direct URLs only. Customs figures are ImportGenius aggregates ("containers" and "kg" as the site counts them). Some BoLs show "South Korea" as origin, which is probably transhipment via Busan or a data artefact. Only Q1 covers other clusters; the other questions cover the named suppliers._

## Q1. Where is timber playhouse production actually done, and which companies own it?

### Takeaway
US customs data shows the timber playhouse and playset volume for the big Western brands comes from three places:
- **Zhuangzhai Town, Cao County (Heze, Shandong).** Heze Jinran Woodware and Heze Zhongran Woodware ship KidKraft's outdoor playhouses, kitchens and kids' furniture. They also ship to Walmart, Costco Canada and PriceSmart.
- **Northern Fujian.** Factories in Nanping prefecture (Shunchang, Jian'ou) and exporters in Xiamen and Fuzhou ship the cedar or fir swing sets, forts and gazebos for Backyard Discovery/Step2 (Leisure Time Products, Step2 Discovery Canada), Eastern Jungle Gym, Yardistry and KidKraft.
- **Lishui, Zhejiang.** Zhejiang Nengfu Tourist Products in Longquan ships KidKraft's "Modern Outdoor Playhouse". Yunhe and Taizhou/Huangyan exporters supply KidKraft's small wooden toys, dollhouses and kitchens.

Most of these high-volume firms do not show up when searching Made-in-China for playhouses. Of the residential-playhouse sellers MIC does list, only **Heze Zhongran** and **Xiamen Warmwood** show up in US customs as direct shippers to Western brands.

### Inferences
- **Two Zhuangzhai Town firms make KidKraft's playhouses.** KidKraft BoLs code kitchens and tables "-MSN-JRN" (JRN ≈ Jinran) and playhouses "-MSN--ZGR" / "MLNZGR", even when Jinran is the shipper (Prairieview 2019, Greystone 2025). Zhongran ships other KidKraft playhouses (Lantana Lane, Riverscape) under its own name, and its MIC profile mentions "two production bases".
  - Likely reading: ZGR is KidKraft's vendor code for Zhongran's outdoor plant, and Jinran and Zhongran are affiliated (same town, same customer, mixed shipments).
  - This is not registry-proven common ownership.
- **Scale versus the platform picture.** The Backyard Discovery/Step2 supply base (Nanping mills and exporters, thousands of containers) and Jinran/Nengfu are essentially invisible on MIC playhouse searches. Platform listings therefore over-represent traders and under-represent the real mills. A buyer who only searches Alibaba or MIC sees mostly the tier above the mills.
- **FSC CoC numbers as a lead.** The FSC CoC numbers on BoLs (SGSHK-COC-006688, -332374, -330306; BV-COC-209735) are concrete leads to look up by hand on search.fsc.org. The certificate scope would show whether the holder is a sawmill or manufacturer, or a trader. Not done here (see Gaps).
- **Fir cubbies for Australia.** For the user's ~140×140 cm fir cubby, the realistic factory-direct candidates found are Zhongran (Grade A, MOQ 300) and possibly Jinran. The Fujian Nanping mills concentrate on large cedar swing sets and gazebos for US brands, which is a different product and order scale.

### Gaps
- **Chinese names and registry.** Chinese registered names, unified social credit codes, 经营范围, 参保人数 and legal representatives were not obtained for any candidate. Web searches for "中然木业" and "金然木业" found nothing; GSXT, Qichacha and Tianyancha were not reachable last round. Common ownership of Jinran and Zhongran is therefore unproven.
- **Design patents** (木质儿童游戏屋 / 儿童木屋 外观设计): not checked. Google Patents XHR returned 503 and no alternative patent database was used.
- **FSC database:** search.fsc.org disallows /api in robots.txt and the UI is JavaScript-only, so none of the certificate numbers could be verified.
- **Trade fairs and associations:** exhibitor lists (Canton Fair Phase 2, China Toy Expo, Spielwarenmesse) and the 曹县木制品行业协会 / 专精特新 lists were not checked, because WebSearch was exhausted. Zhongran's fair photos are self-reported.
- **Other regions:** no wooden playhouse shipper from Linyi (Shandong), Guangxi or Guangdong appeared in the customs pages reviewed. That does not prove none exist.
- **AU and UK brands:** the source factories of Lifespan Kids, Kmart's Activo cubby, Ausway, Rovo, Plum and TP Toys were not identified. Australian and UK import BoLs are not public, and no product labels were found.
- **1688:** source-factory (源头工厂/深度验厂) cards for 儿童木屋 were not found. wiki.1688.com category pages are client-rendered, and article IDs could not be discovered without search.

## Q2. How should Heze Zhongran, Ningbo Dreamgo (and Xiamen Warmwood) be classified?

### Takeaway
- **Heze Zhongran Woodware: a factory, provisionally.** It is the direct exporter of a factory's own output, a Grade A customs shipper of KidKraft playhouses with an industrial-park address in Cao County. It still needs one more independent A/B item (registry production scope and insured headcount, or an FSC certificate naming the site) to meet the strict two-item rule.
- **Ningbo Dreamgo Toys: a trader or sourcing company** for playhouses:
  - office unit in Beilun;
  - 2 trade staff and 30 "supply chain partners";
  - no production-capacity section in its BV-verified profile;
  - no US shipper record under its name;
  - its "own factory in Yunhe" is unnamed.
- **Xiamen Warmwood: an exporter with some own production and many partner factories (hybrid).** It is Grade A as a direct shipper to Backyard Discovery, but has an office address and a 108-staff, 17,600 m² plant claim against huge volume.

## Q3. What does Australian biosecurity require for solid-timber playhouses from China, and who can provide it?

### Takeaway
- **Classification.** A solid Chinese-fir cubby is a manufactured wooden article. It is not on DAFF's "Highly Processed Wooden Article" list, which covers plywood, veneer and reconstituted wood.
- **Treatment options.** Expect to need one of:
  - an offshore treatment by an acceptable provider (heat, methyl bromide or other approved fumigant, irradiation, or permanent preservative), with a treatment certificate, export within 6 months of treatment, and a manufacturer's, supplier's or exporter's storage declaration; or
  - inspection and/or onshore treatment on arrival.
- **Methyl bromide from China.** DAFF's China provider list (summary) says certificates must be **GACC-endorsed, with the GACC logo**. Certificates from private Hong Kong providers for treatments done in China are not accepted.
- **Packaging.** It must be ISPM-15 marked and bark-free, or contain no solid wood.
- **Who arranges it.** The exporter of record arranges it, which a factory with its own export rights (Zhongran) can do.
- **Status of this evidence.** DAFF's own pages were unreachable (503), so these points rest on DAFF notices seen in search summaries plus industry guidance. Per-container costs were not found.

### Cited Findings
- **DAFF IAN 71-2017** (search summary; page 503):
  - From 1 Sep 2017 the time allowed between treatment and export for Wooden Manufactured Articles rose from 21 days to 6 months.
  - Consignments must carry additional storage certification: a manufacturer's, supplier's or exporter's declaration covering the period between treatment and export (max 6 months).

  — [DAFF IAN 71-2017](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/industry-advice/2017/71-2017)
- **DAFF IAN 34-2017** (search summary): reconstituted wood, plywood and veneer articles were added to the Highly Processed Wooden Article list. Items on that list need only commercial documentation. — [DAFF IAN 34-2017](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/industry-advice/2017/34-2017)
- **DAFF "QID108450 China" offshore methyl bromide treatment-provider list, dated 2 June 2023** (search summary; file not retrievable):
  - Certificates from China-based providers must be endorsed by GACC, shown by the GACC logo top-left; certificates without it are not accepted.
  - DAFF does not accept certificates from private Hong Kong providers for treatments performed in China.
  - AEI codes: CN0001MB (with GACC logo) and CN0002MB (unacceptable).

  — [DAFF China list](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/QID108450_China%20230602.pdf)
- **DAFF AEI guidance** (search summary): for methyl bromide treatment in an AFAS country, a registered AFAS provider must be used unless a phytosanitary certificate is also provided. — [DAFF AEI reporting](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/aei-reporting.pdf)
- **ATIF information sheet** (27 Apr 2026; industry association; written for timber imports):
  - Approved options: fumigation (methyl bromide, sulfuryl fluoride, ethylene oxide), heat treatment (kiln drying), gamma radiation, permanent timber preservative.
  - Offshore heat or preservative treatment must be done by recognised companies in the exporting country; it lists "AusTreat Offshore Treatment Providers".
  - Methyl bromide methodology V3 took effect 1 May 2025.
  - For FCL, a container packing declaration covers ISPM-15 packaging and container cleanliness.
  - BMSB season runs 1 Sep–30 Apr.
  - Regulated timber products carry Illegal Logging Prohibition Act due-diligence duties.
  - Its statement that a government phytosanitary certificate is "compulsory" (recording species and m³) describes timber consignments. The sheet is written for timber importers, so this may not apply to finished articles (inference).

  — [ATIF via ForestSource](https://forestsource.io/resources/document/biosecurity-customs-processes)
- **icecargo furniture guide** (28 Oct 2022):
  - Treatments: "fumigation, heat treatment, kiln drying, cold storage".
  - Documents: a fumigation or pest-control certificate covering timber goods and wooden packing, a new-plywood declaration, and a storage declaration.
  - A phytosanitary certificate (NPPO-issued) is needed for furniture made from plant products.
  - Non-compliant goods may be seized, re-exported or destroyed.

  — [icecargo](https://icecargo.com.au/import-furniture-australia/)
- **Earlier round (from earlier notes; not re-fetched today):**
  - China is a BMSB "emerging risk" country for 2026-27 (random onshore inspections; offshore BMSB treatment not mandatory for direct China shipments).
  - The FID biosecurity charge is AUD 71 per sea entry from 1 July 2026.

  — [DAFF 2026-27 BMSB presentation (summary)](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2026-27-bmsb-industry-presentation.pdf); [ACN 2026/23](https://www.abf.gov.au/help-and-support-subsite/CustomsNotices/2026-23.pdf)
- **What Chinese playset factories already put on export documents** (US examples):
  - "FSC 100% FSC CERTIFICATE NO. SGSHK-COC-006688 … SHIPPER DECLARED THAT THIS SHIPMENT CONTAINS SOLID WOOD IPPC HEAT TREATED PALLETS PACKAGING" (Fujian Shunchang Shengsheng, 2026-10-05).
  - "THIS SHIPMENT CONTAINS NO SOLID WOOD PACKING MATERIALS" (Fujian Three Dimensional and Heze Jinran).

  — [ImportGenius Leisure Time Products](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/leisure-time-products-inc); [ImportGenius Fujian Three Dimensional](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/fujian-three-dimensional); [ImportGenius Heze Jinran](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/heze-jinran-woodware-co-ltd)
- **Export rights:** Zhongran states "It has the right to import and export on its own" and uses Qingdao as its port. — [MIC Zhongran profile](https://chinakidstoy.en.made-in-china.com/company-Heze-Zhongran-Woodware-Co-Ltd.html)

### Inferences
- **The PO should require:**
  - a treatment certificate from a DAFF-acceptable provider. For methyl bromide that means GACC-endorsed, with the GACC logo; for heat treatment, a recognised provider. A factory's in-house kiln-drying record alone is unlikely to be accepted unless that provider is recognised.
  - a consignment-specific storage declaration on the manufacturer's or exporter's letterhead, signed and dated, with shipment within 6 months of treatment;
  - an FCL packing declaration with ISPM-15 or no-solid-wood packaging;
  - bark-free timber;
  - an FSC or legality statement (useful for any due-diligence check).
- **Who can supply it.** A factory that exports in its own name (Zhongran) can book the fumigation or heat treatment itself. When buying through a trader, the trader is the exporter and holds the certificates, so you depend on its paperwork.
- **Materials check.** Ask for a bill of materials. If roof and wall panels are plywood or reconstituted board and only frames are solid fir, the solid-wood parts still trigger treatment. A cubby that is mostly plywood or MDF (Kmart's 19 kg Activo is far lighter than Zhongran's 28.5 kg fir house) could face lighter paperwork; confirm with BICON or a broker.
- **Tariff heading.** US entries used HTS 9506.99 for a wooden fort ("FLAGSTAFF FORT FSC HTS: 950699"), and Warmwood quotes China HS 4421.99. The ICS wooden-furniture question (9401.61/9403.60) therefore probably does not apply. BICON timber conditions still apply to the material regardless of heading.
- **Costs.** No sourced per-container figure was found. The earlier round's A$500 per shipment remains an unverified estimate.

### Gaps
- **BICON wording:** the current text for manufactured wooden articles was not verified (BICON 403, DAFF 503, web archive unreachable). This includes exact heat-treatment parameters, accepted fumigants for articles, whether irradiation applies, certificate wording and the inspection fallback.
- **Costs:** none found for offshore fumigation or heat treatment in China (per container), onshore fumigation in Australia, or DAFF inspection.
- **Illegal logging rules:** whether wooden playhouses (HS 9503/9506/4421.99/9406) are "regulated timber products" under the Illegal Logging Prohibition Regulation was not verified.

### Inferences
- **Why the same SKU shows up across storefronts:**
  1. A factory such as Zhongran designs a catalogue model and photographs it.
  2. It sells the model at MOQ 300 to many importers. These include Chinese cross-border sellers' US, AU or EU entities and traders like Dreamgo, who may relist it at MOQ 1.
  3. Each relists it with the factory's photos under its own store brand, often adding a colour variant.

  The user's "fifth middleman" worry is realistic for platform listings. Customs records let you skip to the shipper of record.
- **Design ownership.** Brand-specific named models (KidKraft's) are probably brand-owned designs and not sold to others. Generic "Toys Valley"-type catalogue models are what multiple resellers carry.
- **Tracing an AU storefront SKU.** Compare carton dimensions and weights. For example, Ausway's carton is 127×100×15.5 cm and 33.5 kg; Kmart Activo's pack is 104×104×14.5 cm and 19 kg. Match these against factory listings (Zhongran's are 105×98×17.5 cm / 28.5 kg and 103×51×33 cm / 31.3 kg). None of these matched, so the AU designs likely come from other plants.
