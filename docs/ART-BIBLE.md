# DAVID & GOLIATH — art bible and engine contract

A hyperreal cosmic short (178 s, 24 fps, 1920×1080) retelling David and Goliath as a **white dwarf** stealing the
outer layers of a **red giant** until the stolen hydrogen detonates in a **classical nova**. The giant's
overwhelming mass becomes the fuel for the small star's transformation; the white dwarf survives the blast.
Everything is drawn in code: WebGL2 fragment shaders for light and matter, Canvas 2D for drawings and titles.

## The story in four movements (music: Vivaldi, *Winter* I, Allegro non molto)
| Act | Frames | Music | Feeling |
|---|---|---|---|
| I Attraction | 0–925 | bars 1–11, the trembling chords stack voice by voice | vastness, dread, scale |
| II-A Resistance | 925–1592 | bars 12–19, solo violin "horrid wind" (rubato) | matter ripped away, the first stream |
| II-B Compression | 1592–2146 | bars 20–26, tutti repeated notes, "running and stamping" | engulfing vortex, atoms pressing |
| II-C Frenzy | 2146–2583 | bars 27–31, solo 32nd-note runs: cuts on every beat, then half-beat | a frenzy of perspectives |
| II-D Ladder | 2583–3134 | bars 32–38, five strikes on the downbeats, tremolo | compression to the limit |
| III Ignition | 3134–3399 | **music cut**: electronic pressure wave, descending Doppler roar | white-hot eruption; the frame opens |
| IV Aftermath | 3399–4272 | bars 56–63, closing tutti + fermata | expansion, devastation, survival, order |

The exact shot list with purpose/action/framing is in `film-plan.json` (generated by `tools/build_plan.mjs`).

## Visual grammar
* **Palette**: abyssal black, deep crimson, molten gold, cold blue. Space is *black*, not navy. Stars are sparse and fine.
  Use the palette constants in `engine/glsl.js` (`C_CRIMSON, C_EMBER, C_GOLD, C_WHITE, C_ICE, C_DEEPBLUE`) and the
  temperature→colour logic of the shared libraries. Heat reads as crimson → ember → gold → white; the dwarf and magnetism read cold blue.
* **Light is physical and overwhelming**: emissive HDR radiance, not painted colour. A hot thing is *bright*
  (values of 5–500 are normal for plasma cores); post handles bloom, ACES tonemapping, halation and grain. Never
  pre-tonemap or clamp in a scene. Let bright cores clip to white with coloured falloff.
* **Scale**: the giant is colossal (fills frames, convection cells the size of suns); the dwarf is Earth-sized,
  usually a blinding point. Sell scale with parallax, atmospheric depth, tiny bright things against huge dim things,
  slow camera at large scale and fast camera at small scale.
* **Interstellar** (gravitational terror): slow, heavy camera; vast silhouettes; matter streaming past in the foreground;
  restrained lens artefacts (subtle horizontal blue streaks only on very bright sources).
* **Oppenheimer** (tactile atomic intensity): macro shots of particles and fire with real texture and depth; jitter,
  vibration, sparks, filaments; a sense of touching the matter.
* **Renaissance studies** (restrained): iron-gall ink and red chalk on aged paper; Leonardo's mirror script; the
  drawings visibly *are* the same geometry as the real imagery (the Roche figure-eight, the parabolic stream, vortices,
  rings). Humanity appears only through the impulse to understand.
* **Composition**: 2.39:1 scope (letterbox 0.128 of height per bar) until ignition, then the frame opens to full
  16:9. Compose for the scope band (rows 138–942 of 1080) before ignition. Letterbox crops, it never rescales: the
  camera frustum is always the full 16:9 frame.
* **Motion**: every shot moves with intent (slow push, track, orbit, drift). No dead stops unless authored. Fast
  cuts in the frenzy still need internal motion. Camera paths are smooth curves (`camFromKeys`) with eased keys.

## Physical facts we honour (illustrative, not a fit to one system)
* Binary geometry from `src/scenes/lib/binary.js`: q = M_giant/M_wd = 0.55, separation a = 1, giant at x = −0.645,
  white dwarf at x = +0.355, L1 at x = −0.206, giant's Roche lobe (volume radius 0.33a; 0.437a toward L1), disk
  outer radius ≈ 0.32a, circularisation radius ≈ 0.15a. Orbital plane = world XZ, Y up.
  `streamPath()` integrates the real ballistic stream from L1 (it bows toward +Z, passes the dwarf on its +Z side,
  then wraps). Disk gas orbits in the same sense as the stream wraps.
* The giant fills its Roche lobe → teardrop pointing at L1. Mass flows through L1 as a narrow stream, hits the outer
  disk at a hot spot, spirals inward; the inner disk is hottest (white/blue), the outer cooler (gold/crimson).
* Nova = thermonuclear runaway in the accreted hydrogen layer on the dwarf's surface (CNO cycle). It ejects a
  shell at thousands of km/s; the shell becomes clumpy (Rayleigh–Taylor fingers, knots). **The dwarf survives**;
  the cycle restarts (recurrent nova, like T Coronae Borealis / RS Ophiuchi which have red-giant companions).
* CNO cycle: ¹²C +p→ ¹³N →β⁺→ ¹³C +p→ ¹⁴N +p→ ¹⁵O →β⁺→ ¹⁵N +p→ ¹²C + ⁴He.

## Engine contract (read `src/engine/*.js`, `src/scenes/redgiant.js` as the reference scene)
A scene module (`src/scenes/<id>.js`) default-exports:
```js
export default {
  id: 'binary',
  scale: 1,                    // render scale of the HDR scene target (0.5–1). Use <1 for soft volumetrics.
  presets: { 'S04-scale': {...params}, 'F27.1': {...}, default: {...} },   // per-shot params keyed by shot id
  init(E) { /* compile programs once: this.prog = E.program(frag(...chunks, MAIN), 'name') */ },
  render(E, S, target) { /* E.draw(this.prog, {...camera.uniforms, ...}, target) — HDR linear radiance */ },
  overlay(E, S, ctx) { /* optional Canvas2D at full 1920×1080 output pixels; return 'add' | 'over' */ },
  post(E, S) { /* optional dynamic post overrides, e.g. { exposure: 1.3, streakStrength: 0.2 } */ },
};
```
* `S`: `{ f, t (film seconds), local (seconds since shot start), dur, u (0..1), params, shot, seed, W, H, fps }`.
  `local` can be outside [0, dur] during dissolves — handle it (clamp).
* `E`: `{ G, W, H, fps, program, draw, target(name, scale), camera(pos, target, fovDeg, roll), music, math, rng, overlayCtx }`.
  `E.target()` makes extra frame-shaped targets for multi-pass scenes; `E.draw` auto-scissors to the letterbox.
* Standard uniforms set for you: `uRes, uFull, uTime, uLocal, uDur, uU, uFrame, uSeed, uNoise` (3D noise volume).
  Camera uniforms come from `E.camera(...).uniforms` or `camFromKeys(params.cam, S.local)` (see `lib/util.js`).
* GLSL: `frag(...chunks)` prepends HEADER, COMMON, NOISE, COLOR. Optional chunks: `STARS` (engine/glsl.js),
  `GIANT` (lib/giant.glsl.js), `DWARF` (lib/dwarf.glsl.js). Use `frameUV()` + `cameraRay()` for rays,
  `n3/fbm3/ridged3/cells3` for noise (texture-based, cheap), `ign()` for ray jitter, `blackbody(T)`.
* Overlays: `E.overlayCtx` is cleared each frame; draw in output pixels; project 3D points with
  `cam.project(p, E.W, E.H)`. Scene shaders may also sample the overlay as `uOverlay` (pass
  `E.overlayTexture()`), e.g. ink multiplied into paper. Return `null` from `overlay()` when the shader consumes the
  overlay itself, so it is not composited a second time.
* Rotation sense: seen from +Y looking down (+X right, +Z toward the bottom of frame) the orbit and the accretion disk
  turn **counter-clockwise**; matter spirals inward counter-clockwise. Drawings of vortices follow the same sense.
* `E.music.at(bar, eighth)` → film seconds of a score position; `E.music.pulse(t)` → current eighth note
  `{bar, k, since, period}` for beat-synced accents (works in bars 1–38 and 56–63).
* Post params (defaults in `engine/post.js` `POST_DEFAULTS`): exposure, bloomStrength, bloomThreshold,
  streakStrength, halation, aberration, vignette, grain, saturation, contrast, tint, blur [x,y] (directional motion
  blur in px), zoomBlur, shake [dx, dy, rot]. Global effects (letterbox, strike flashes, shakes, fades) come from
  the plan — don't duplicate them.
* **Determinism**: a frame is a pure function of (plan, frame). No Math.random, Date, or accumulated state. Seeded
  noise via `E.rng` or GLSL hashes of stable ids. Precompute tables in `init()`.

## Performance budget (SwiftShader CPU WebGL — this matters)
* Target ≤ 3 s per 1920×1080 frame for heavy shots, ≤ 1.5 s typical. The film has ~4,300 frames.
* Prefer texture noise (`n3`, `cells3`: 1 fetch) over ALU hash noise. fbm with 3–5 octaves. Raymarch ≤ 48 steps;
  jitter start with `ign()`; early-out when transmittance < 0.02 or outside bounding spheres.
* Soft volumetrics can render at `scale: 0.5–0.75` (post upsamples). Sharp elements (stars, filaments, lines) want 1.
* Measure: `node tools/render.mjs bench <scene> --preset <shotId> --w 1920 --t 2` (other agents may be rendering at
  the same time, so absolute numbers are inflated; keep shaders lean).

## Working method
* Develop with the sandbox: `node tools/render.mjs sandbox <scene> --preset <shotId> --times 0,2,4 --dur <shotDur> --w 960`
  then inspect the JPEGs in `out/sandbox/<scene>/` with the Read tool. Check at least start/middle/end of each shot,
  and a few consecutive frames (`--times 1.0,1.0417,1.0833`) for flicker/swimming.
* Render real plan frames with `node tools/render.mjs shot <shotId> --n 3` (uses true timing and global effects).
* Only edit your own scene file(s) and your own new `src/scenes/lib/<yours>*.js` files. Never edit the engine,
  `film-plan.json`, `tools/build_plan.mjs` or another agent's files; if you need an engine change, report it.
* Judge your frames like a cinematographer: does it look *hyperreal* (detail at several scales, physical light,
  depth), on palette, on story beat? Fix the root problem rather than adding glow or grain over it.
