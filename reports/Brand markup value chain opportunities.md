# Licences, Not Products, Guard Australia's Fattest Markups

Australia's fattest product markups belong to brands that also own their channel, and the version of that layer a small water operator can actually capture is narrower: products where a mandatory certificate that can be endorsed in the operator's name meets a licensed installer who is personally liable for fitting anything uncertified. FY26 filings put pure trade distributors at 25–32% gross margin and 3–9% EBIT (Reece earns 8.6% in ANZ), brand owners selling through merchants at 35–50% and 10–19% (GWA 40.6% and 18.5%), and brand-plus-own-channel firms at 62–83% gross (Beacon 68.6%, Koala 65.2%), so the opportunity is to collapse layers rather than become another middleman; the operator's own filter model shows plumber wholesale earning A$52–248 a unit against about A$1,036 for an owner-sold installed job. Re-scored on the shared framework at one exchange rate, the strongest lines all sit inside the WaterMark-plus-plumber family the operator already clears: an own-brand tank-water UV and filter cabinet (index 82.5 of 100, about A$1,400 a job plus A$200–450 a year in lamps and cartridges), an RO and filter-tap attach sold on the same visit (86.5, but only as an attach), bore and problem-water packages including softening (77), boiling/chilled taps for homes and offices as a second phase (73.5), and plumbed eyewash and safety showers for the industrial and pool-plant sites a water business already serves (69). The thesis holds with one correction: brand markups survive only where the barrier binds at installation and names a liable party, which is why Kings sells a A$599 winch against Warn's A$2,999 yet stocks no bull bars, tow bars or canopies, and why Temu, Kmart's Anko and merchant house brands flatten every category guarded only by trust. The certificate is not the moat, because five unrelated brands already sell the reference filter on one WaterMark licence; the durable advantage is the install-and-consult channel, a designed consumable annuity and, eventually, a licence in the operator's own name. Every FOB price here is a listing rather than a quote, and the decisive WaterMark register facts could not be read by automated tools, so the next step is an afternoon of manual register searches and three certifier quotes, not a container.

## Filings put the fat margin in brand plus channel, not in the middle

Australian FY26 accounts sort into four margin bands, and the band that pays is not the one the word "middleman" suggests. Pure trade distributors of other firms' brands earn about **25–32% gross and 3–9% EBIT**: Reece earned A$534m EBIT on A$9,378m of sales, **8.6% in its 678-branch ANZ business** ([Reece FY26](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260824/pdf/073339wfnfjcqp.pdf)), on a gross margin of about 28.9% computed from its statutory cost of sales ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/asx/REH/financials/income-statement/)); Metcash's Hardware & Tools division made about 4.8% EBIT on A$3.7bn ([Metcash FY26](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260622/pdf/070vqhtcmk3bw7.pdf)) and Wesfarmers' Blackwoods-led Industrial & Safety division 4.3% EBT ([Wesfarmers FY26](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260827/pdf/0738z7fxb48k84.pdf)), in line with Ferguson's 30.7% and Pool Corp's 29.7% gross overseas ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ferg/financials/); [stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pool/financials/)). Brand owners that sell through those merchants keep **35–50% gross and 10–19% EBIT**, with GWA (Caroma, Methven, Dorf) at **40.6% gross and 18.5% normalised EBIT** ([GWA Annual Report 2026](https://cdn.gwagroup.com.au/v3/assets/bltad292a6aa65b6a42/blt645d58ced7d57b26/6ab6094458e06805af882e7b/GWA_Annual_Report_2026_spreads.pdf)), Waterco, owner of Davey, at 50.4% gross ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/asx/WAT/financials/income-statement/)), Breville at 36.0% ([Breville FY26](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260819/pdf/072x5fpvmsbz8q.pdf)) and Reliance Worldwide at 35.2%, though only 0.7% adjusted EBIT in Asia-Pacific ([RWC Annual Report 2026](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260818/pdf/072vm08477yl6w.pdf)). Firms that own the brand, the import and the counter reach **62–83% gross**: Beacon Lighting 68.6% with 43% of relevant sales through its own trade program ([Beacon FY26](https://bulletin.webull.com/qbd/announcement/20260827/499501693/4ea705569d36ea87ff3b7ced5280a2c8.pdf)), Nick Scali 65.6% ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/asx/NCK/financials/income-statement/)), Koala 65.2% ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/asx/KOA/financials/income-statement/)) and Lovisa 82.6% ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/asx/LOV/financials/income-statement/)). Retailers of other people's brands sit at the bottom, with JB Hi-Fi at 22.4% ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/asx/JBH/financials/income-statement/)) and Temple & Webster at 31.5% ([TPW Annual Report 2026](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260929/pdf/074n0zggpwv77d.pdf)).

The thick gross pool is not the thick profit pool, and the gap shows a small operator where to stand. A merchant's 29 points pays for branch stock, 30-day trade credit with retention of title ([Middy's terms](https://www.middys.com.au/getmedia/dcea8410-002b-4f27-b2fb-5033ffa51967/Terms-and-Conditions-of-Sale-Middendorp-Electric-Co-Pty-Ltd.pdf)), delivery to site, a warranty counter and showrooms that "act as an extension of your business" for the plumber's clients ([Tradelink](https://tradelink.com.au/about-us/)), which is why only 8.6 points survive at Reece. The brand owner's 40 points pays for national warranty, reps and merchant rebates, and 18.5 survive at GWA. The integrated firms keep 60-plus points of gross but spend most of it finding and serving customers: Beacon's operating costs run at 44% of sales, leaving about 14% EBIT, and Koala turns 65.2% gross into 7.3% EBIT. An operator that already has an install crew, a referral flow and a consult sale attacks exactly the cost line that consumes the integrated brands' margin, which is the structural reason the brand-plus-own-channel layer is the one to take; this is an inference from the margin pattern, not a disclosed finding.

Worked through for a plumbing product, a brand selling to Reece at 100 implies a trade price near 140, a plumber's 20–30% materials markup lifts it to about 168–182 ex GST ([AroFlo](https://aroflo.com/blog/how-price-plumbing-jobs)), and the householder pays roughly **3.1–3.3 times landed cost for the goods alone**. The reference filter sits at the top of that stack on supply and above it installed. US$290 FOB is A$417 at the RBA's 0.6956 ([RBA F11.1](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/f11.1-data.csv)) and models to **A$486 landed per sellable unit** on 64 units by LCL, after inspection, charges and a 3% defect allowance (A$507 if no ChAFTA certificate of origin is held). The same PWF-style cabinet sells supply-only at **A$1,299–1,499** under AQUAOZZY ([AQUAOZZY](https://www.aquaozzy.com.au/)), 2.4–2.8 times landed ex GST, and installed at A$2,190 + GST under Aquarius ([Aquarius Water Filters](https://aquariuswaterfilters.com.au/)), 4.1–5.6 times landed across the operator's A$2,200–3,000 range. Modelled against those costs, the channels settle the "middleman" question for this product: wholesaling to plumbers at a realistic A$600–800 ex GST earns **A$52–248 a unit**; online supply-only at parity with AquaSafe's A$975 stainless twin ([AquaSafe](https://aquasafe.com.au/products/aquasafe-twin-20-stainless-stand-and-cover)) about A$156; and an owner-sold installed job at A$2,999 about **A$1,036 after A$650 of plumber labour, A$200 of sundries and A$300 of marketing** ([TrueFlow](https://trueflowplumbing.net.au/whole-house-water-filters/)). Becoming the middle layer is the weakest option on offer; collapsing the layers is the strongest.

Two features of the merchant layer push a small operator toward its own channel. Merchants are taking the brand layer themselves: Reece amortises an acquired Metalflex brand name ([Reece FY26 presentation](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260824/pdf/07333d16gqvr31.pdf)) and sells Thermann as its exclusive heat-pump brand ([SolarQuotes](https://www.solarquotes.com.au/hot-water/heat-pump/reviews/thermann-review.html)), RSEA's house-brand Blue Rapta P2 masks sell at A$1.75 each against 3M's A$6.50 ([RSEA](https://www.rsea.com.au/ppe/respiratory-protection/disposable-respirators/blue-rapta-disp-mask-p2-cupped-p2-box-20-8620)), and 51% of Temple & Webster's revenue is private label or exclusive ([TPW Annual Report 2026](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260929/pdf/074n0zggpwv77d.pdf)). Getting ranged also costs more than getting certified: Metcash's auditor treats supplier rebate income as a key audit matter ([Metcash Annual Report](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260622/pdf/070vps5ftc4wf8.pdf)), GWA reports DIFOT above 90%, 30,000 plumber technical interactions and "an exclusive new range with a key merchant" as the price of shelf space ([GWA FY26 presentation](https://cdn.gwagroup.com.au/v3/assets/bltad292a6aa65b6a42/blt3cd706759491a200/6a823f810e035ced1bb86a48/3103265.pdf)), and Bunnings returns 5% to trade buyers through PowerPass Pro ([Bunnings Trade](https://trade.bunnings.com.au/powerpass-pro-rewards)). Two caveats apply: Reece and GWA do not show gross margin in their results documents, so both figures are computed from statutory accounts, and ARB, often cited as the archetype of an integrated 4WD brand, appears only in secondary summaries (A$702.0m of FY26 sales and a materials-basis gross margin of about 56–59%) ([StockWireX](https://stockwirex.com/asx-stock-news/consumer-discretionary/arb-corporation-fy26-results-august-2026/)), so it is a directional comparator rather than evidence.

## Certificates cost hundreds; liability and gatekeepers set the real toll

Official fees are rarely the barrier. The ABCB's WaterMark licence fee is **A$525 a year per licence**, passed through by the certifier alongside application, audit and endorsement fees that are quoted only on request ([IAPMO POL-100](https://iapmooceania.org/media/a5dfckvx/pol-100-wmk-governance-rules.pdf)); electrical registration costs A$239.74 a year per responsible supplier plus A$89.85–449.25 per model ([EESS fees](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/registration-fees/)); GEMS energy registration A$440–780 per model ([GEMS](https://reg.energyrating.gov.au/application/help/payment/)); a refrigerant trading authorisation A$280 a year ([ARC](https://www.arctick.org/refrigerant-trading-authorisation/authorisation-fees/)); and a Queensland plumber's licence A$248.75–602.99 ([QBCC](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/licences/apply-licence/licence-fees/licence-fees-occupational-plumbing-drainage)). The expensive outliers are the Clean Energy Council's battery and inverter list, at **A$5,500 + GST per application with a 20-week queue before engineering review starts** ([CEC](https://www.cleanenergycouncil.org.au/industry-programs/products-program/apply)), TGA Class IIa devices at A$1,244 to apply and A$1,305 a year before audits ([Pure Global](https://www.pureglobal.com/news/tga-medical-device-ivd-fees-annual-charges-2026)), and gas appliances, whose certification is quote-only and runs per model family ([AGA Rules](https://www.aga.asn.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Rules-Governing-GAS-10-June-2024.pdf)). What protects a price ladder is the stack those fees buy into: a certificate tied to a named licensee and model, an installer who is personally liable for fitting anything else, deemed-manufacturer liability for whoever puts a brand on the goods, and merchants that range only certified stock.

WaterMark is the strongest example because every layer of that stack is written into law. The scheme's Approved User, which can be "a manufacturer, assembler, distributor, retailer or importer", must mark each product with its own name, brand or trademark, the licence number and the specification, so a buyer-branded unit is lawful only if that brand is listed against that model ([WaterMark Manual](https://abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/Manual-for-WaterMark-certification-scheme.pdf)). There are two routes. Under Route A the factory's licensee asks its certifier to endorse the buyer's brand on its schedule, which certificates record in "Brand Name" and "Date Endorsed" columns ([EBOS certificate](https://www.eboshealthcare.com.au/globalassets/product-images/v/watermark-certificate-2020-2024-updated.pdf)) and which licence 080071 already does for AQUAPRIMUS, Water Filter Direct, AquaOzzy, Aquarius Water Filters and PureFlow ([WaterMark database index](https://cpd.nccb.gov.au/watermark-search?page=15206)). Under Route B the buyer holds its own licence, which needs the manufacturer's written authorisation, accepts existing test reports if current and traceable, takes "a few months to 12 months plus" ([IAPMO](https://iapmooceania.org/certification-services/watermark-product-certification)), and is the only version the buyer truly owns, because transferring a licence counts as a new application. Queensland enforces the installer end with a 100-penalty-unit offence for installing a non-WaterMark product and another for directing someone to do it ([Plumbing and Drainage Act 2018](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/pdf/current/act-2018-017)), and the supply end with non-conforming building product duties on anyone who "imports or supplies", carrying **maximum penalties of 1,000 penalty units, or A$172,700 at the July 2026 value** ([QBCC Act](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/pdf/current/act-1991-098); [penalty unit regulation](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/asmade/sl-2026-0046)). Lead-free rules add a timing trap the sources disagree on: the national schedule authorises installation of non-lead-free-certified stock until 1 May 2028 ([Schedule of Products 2026-2](https://watermark.abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2026/WaterMark%20Schedule%20of%20Products%202026-2_0.pdf)), but Victoria already treats 1 May 2026 as the installation cut-off for copper-alloy drinking-water products ([BPC Victoria](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/resource-hub/industry-updates/new-lead-free-plumbing-product-requirements)), so only lead-free-certified brass is worth buying.

Three conclusions follow for this operator. First, the barriers it can clear in months for low thousands of dollars (WaterMark by endorsement, the plumbing licences it holds, consumer-law cover, EESS Levels 1–2, ActivFire agency and GEMS) are exactly the ones that protect plumber-installed and alarm-type products, while the ones it cannot clear cheaply (gas, CEC, TGA above Class I, ADR, full-scale fire tests and merchant ranging) protect categories outside its channel. Second, riding a maker's certificate is quickest where a scheme lists brand names under one licensee and slowest where the certificate attaches to the Australian responsible supplier per model, and it creates a dependency: a WaterMark licence lasts 12 months, a lapse removes the listing, and suspension "may include product recall" ([WaterMark Manual](https://abcb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2022/Manual-for-WaterMark-certification-scheme.pdf)). Third, liability is the barrier small importers underestimate. The average escape-of-liquid home claim is **A$23,843** ([Budget Direct](https://www.budgetdirect.com.au/home-contents-insurance/research/escape-of-liquid-survey-statistics.html)), about the gross profit of 23 installed filter jobs, and a business that already insures plumbers and documents compliant installs carries that risk more cheaply than a parcel seller can, which is part of why the barrier protects it.

## Five challenger plays already work, and the first fits this operator

The documented Australian challengers fall into five repeatable plays that differ mainly in which channel the challenger owns. The first, **a certified brand with its own licensed installers and a consultation or finance-led sale**, is the one this operator is already halfway into, and its best example was bought by a multinational this year. Complete Home Filtration began "in a small garage", holds WaterMark certification and plumbing licences in every state, installs "thousands of systems every month" and claims more than 100,000 customers ([CHF](https://www.completehomefiltration.com.au/about-us)); it fills that crew through member pricing with the RAC, RACV, RAA, RACQ and NRMA ([CHF partnerships](https://www.completehomefiltration.com.au/about-us/affiliations)) and a builder program that writes the filter into working drawings for fit-off before handover ([CHF builders](https://www.completehomefiltration.com.au/build-with-us)). Business News reported on 4 March 2026 that Culligan had bought CHF ([Business News](https://businessnews.com.au/article/US-giant-Culligan-buys-Perth-water-filtration-firm)). One sibling note treated the deal as unconfirmed and another blurred it with Pure Water Systems; the corrected fact is that the buyer was Culligan and the target was CHF, corroborated by CHF's licence table, which lists its Queensland licence under ZIP Water Services QLD Pty Ltd, part of "the Culligan International Group" ([Zip Water](https://www.zipwater.com/)). Pure Water Systems runs the same model from the Gold Coast with Queensland and NSW contractor licences and a cartridge "made… exclusively for Pure Water Systems" ([PWS](https://purewatersystems.com.au/pages/about-us)), and Water Analytics Australia, a business name of Liqi Trading Pty Ltd since 2022 ([ABN Lookup](https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View?abn=34622587437)), sells at "$5.50/Day, Interest-Free" after a free water test, installs through "WAA-approved, independently licensed" plumbers and wholesales to resellers ([WAA](https://www.wateranalytics.com.au/filtrationsystem)). The play travels beyond water: Solargain's iStore sells heat pumps made in China to its specification and won 63% of installers' votes for their own homes in 2026 ([SolarQuotes](https://www.solarquotes.com.au/blog/best-heat-pump-hot-water-2026/)). SolarQuotes names Huawei as the maker ([SolarQuotes review](https://www.solarquotes.com.au/hot-water/heat-pump/reviews/istore-review.html)), but the Clean Energy Regulator's register lists iStore's 270-litre model code under the Chinese maker PHNIX as well ([CER register](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-systems/solar-water-heaters/register-solar-water-heaters)), which points to PHNIX. EVSE Australia, which started in a garage and finances chargers as a service ([EVSE](https://evse.com.au/about/)), and JET Charge ([JET Charge](https://jetcharge.com.au/about-us/)) run the same installer-led model in EV charging.

The second play is **a direct-to-consumer brand that adds trade accounts and then stockists**. ABI Interiors, founded in Queensland in 2016, opened trade accounts for plumbers, builders and designers and showrooms for "a tactile experience", then entered New Zealand in 2019 and the UK in 2021 ([ABI Interiors](https://www.abiinteriors.com.au/our-story/)); Meir, selling since 2012, now reaches buyers through "over 800 stockists globally" ([Meir](https://www.meir.com.au/pages/about-us)); and Koala, the clearest financial proof, listed in March 2026 after reaching **A$332.3m of FY26 revenue at 65.2% gross and 7.3% EBIT** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koala_%28company%29); [stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/asx/KOA/financials/income-statement/)). JasonL took the same route into business furniture, from an online launch in 2009 to a place on the NSW whole-of-government furniture contract ([JasonL](https://www.jasonl.com.au/pages/about-us)). None of these brands rested on a certificate, and all spent years building a channel, which is the cost this operator has already paid.

The third play **buys exclusivity or sells it**. Spa World, "the largest spa retailer in the Southern Hemisphere", holds exclusive rights to distribute Jacuzzi, Vortex and Fisher ([Spa World](https://www.spaworld.com.au/contact-us/about-us/)), and Crimsafe runs the licensor version, with "every Crimsafe security screen… fabricated and installed by our Australia-wide network of authorised Crimsafe Licensees" ([Crimsafe](https://crimsafe.com.au/supplier/bee-safe-security/)). Certificate-riding brands are the low-capital form of the same idea: MATelec and GSM Electrical sell smoke alarms on certificates whose registrant is Siterwell Electronics of Ningbo, with the Australian brand named as agent ([ActivFire afp-3314](https://activfire.csiro.au/html/certDetailsView/afp3314.htm); [afp-3735](https://activfire.csiro.au/html/certDetailsView/afp3735.htm)), Brilliant Lighting rides Anka Sci-Tech of Shenzhen ([afp-3738](https://activfire.csiro.au/pdfs/certificates/afp3738.pdf?nc=1)), five brands ride WaterMark licence 080071, and a China-made Peppy boiling-chilled tap sits in Tradelink at A$3,500 ([Tradelink](https://tradelink.com.au/4-in-1-filtered-boiling-filtered-chilled-hot-and-cold-tap-chrome-288822/)). The licensor version is this operator's long game: once it holds its own WaterMark licence, it can let partner plumbers install and service its brand under its warranty, as Crimsafe's licensees do.

The fourth play is **the channel owner taking the brand layer for itself**, and for a small operator it is mostly a threat. Kmart Group, whose Anko has been an umbrella house brand since 2019 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kmart_Australia)), earned **9.4% EBT on A$11.75bn** ([Wesfarmers FY26](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260827/pdf/0738z7fxb48k84.pdf)) against 6.6% EBIT at JB Hi-Fi, which resells other firms' brands; Reece's Thermann and Metalflex, Beacon's own ranges and Temple & Webster's direct-imported private label are the same move in plumbing, lighting and furniture. Wherever the merchant runs its own brand, a challenger competing for that merchant's shelf is competing with the landlord. This operator's version of the play is the first one, in which the install channel is the channel owner taking the brand.

The fifth play is **an exclusive third-party brand at a big box**, and its best-known example is widely misattributed. Ozito is not a Bunnings house brand, as it is often taken to be: Germany's Einhell took it over in 2013, its site footer reads "© 2026 Einhell Australia Pty Ltd", and its tools are "available through Bunnings Warehouse stores" ([Ozito](https://www.ozito.com.au/about-us/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einhell)). GWA's exclusive Caroma range for one merchant is the plumbing equivalent. The play needs national supply reliability and a buyer willing to grant exclusivity, so for this operator it is a later channel or an exit rather than an entry.

All five plays have a price at exit. Clarus paid about **US$198m for Rhino-Rack on trailing sales of about US$90m and adjusted EBITDA of US$16.7m**, roughly 11.9 times ([Clarus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/913277/000110465921077020/tm2118432d1_ex99-1.htm)); Reliance Worldwide paid A$160m for Holman at 7.0 times EBITDA ([RWC](https://www.rwc.com/sites/default/files/media-file-public/RWC%20to%20Acquire%20Holman%20Industries%20%20-%20Announcement.pdf)) and has agreed a takeover by Brookfield at about 12.1 times FY26 EBITDA ([RWC](https://www.rwc.com/sites/default/files/media-file-public/2026%2009%2016%20RWC%20Enters%20Into%20Scheme%20Implementaton%20Deed%20with%20Brookfield.pdf)); SCEE agreed up to A$53.5m for the fire-services firm Force Fire, whose FY25 EBIT was A$8.3m and whose recurring work was about 30% of revenue ([SCEE](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20250331/pdf/06h4w5kblbkdby.pdf)); and Culligan did not disclose its price for CHF. Strategic buyers pay for a brand plus an installed base, although the Rhino-Rack write-downs in the final section show what happens to that value when no barrier stands behind the brand.

## Re-scored on one basis, water-treatment adjacencies lead the field

The five sibling studies scored their categories on unweighted 35-point scales, at three different exchange rates (A$1.4376, A$1.52 and about A$1.54 per US dollar), and without the shared framework's barrier dimension or gates, so their totals cannot be compared directly. The tables below restate every ladder at the RBA rate of US$1 = A$1.4376, price each sale ex GST in the mode the operator would actually use, take landed cost as 1.17 times FOB for dense goods (calibrated on the filter's modelled A$486) and 1.3–1.5 times for bulky goods, and compute the framework's contribution ratio T as price less landed cost, installation labour, sundries, a warranty reserve and compliance amortisation, divided by price, before customer-acquisition cost. The ladder (a), contribution (b) and annuity (d) sub-scores follow mechanically from those inputs; barrier protection (c1) is assigned by the framework's definitions; clearability (c2), liability (e), incumbent risk (f) and channel fit (g) are the sibling researchers' judgements, adjusted where the framework's rules require it, which caps channel fit at 3 where a different trade, such as an electrician, leads the work and caps incumbent risk at 2 where the channel owner sells its own brand. The index is 20 × (0.10a + 0.20b + 0.10c1 + 0.15c2 + 0.10d + 0.10e + 0.10f + 0.15g), on a 20–100 scale. Four gates then apply: G1 caps commodity categories (barrier protection of 2 or less) at 60, G2 excludes the most severe liability classes (gas, lithium storage, medical devices, vehicle safety parts) unless a certified product and specialist insurance quotes are in hand, G3 excludes impractical barriers, and G4 flags installed or consult-led sales that contribute less than about A$500 a job. L is the end price ex GST divided by landed cost; contribution is per unit or job, ex GST and before marketing.

## Five picks reuse the plumber, the WaterMark and the consult

The five picks are ordered by what the operator should build rather than strictly by index: the RO attach outscores everything but cannot stand alone, so the tank-water cabinet leads. All five reuse the operator's three assets at once (WaterMark know-how, plumbers and the in-home or on-site consult), and all five follow the same path from a maker's certificate, where one exists, to a licence in the operator's own name.

**1. An own-brand tank-water UV and filter cabinet (82.5).** Tank water is a large, under-served installed base: in the ABS's 2013 survey, 26% of households used a rainwater tank and 23% of households outside the capitals drank tank water as their main source ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/ausstats/abs@.nsf/Lookup/4602.0.55.003main+features4Mar%202013)). Incumbent prices sit far above landed cost: Puretec's Hybrid E rainwater-plus-UV systems carry RRPs of **A$3,144–4,100** ([Puretec](https://www.puretec.com.au/HYBRID-E7)) and its pump-ready cabinets A$8,595–10,409 without a pump ([Puretec](https://www.puretec.com.au/HYBRID-P1)), while UV chambers list at US$34–143 ([Made-in-China](https://www.made-in-china.com/products-search/hot-china-products/UV_Water_Sterilizer.html)). At A$3,000–3,500 installed the job contributes about **A$1,400 before marketing**, and the annuity is unusually strong because the lamp is a safety item with a countdown alarm: Puretec's RL6 lamp sells for A$275.55 against A$125 for the same fit at Aquasafe ([Puretec](https://www.puretec.com.au/RL6); [Aquasafe](https://aquasafe.com.au/products/uv-11066-replacement-uv-lamp-rl6)), on lamps listed at US$3–7. AS 3497 explicitly covers ultraviolet treatment, so the full WaterMark-plus-plumber gate applies; the ballast is probably EESS Level 2, an inference to confirm with a certifier. Puretec defends the channel with a A$50 gift card per FilterWall installed and a 1,000-home mail-out for its first 50 certified installers ([Puretec](https://www.puretec.com.au/plumbers-program-terms-and-conditions)), which shows what plumber loyalty costs. The first step is a written RFQ to Purezzo for the PWF cabinet with an integrated 304 stainless UV chamber (55–110 W, lamp-life timer), conditional on a WaterMark licence covering the UV variant and the operator's brand and on the ballast's AS/NZS 60335 test report, with Ningbo Zuanbang, a US-customs-verified supplier to APEC ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/ningbo-zuanbang-technology-co-ltd)), and Shanghai Chaomei as alternates. Until that certificate exists, attaching a third-party-certified UV unit to whole-house sales in tank areas measures demand. The decisive unknown is that no Chinese UV maker was found holding an Australian AS 3497 licence.

**2. Under-sink RO and a 3-way filter tap with a cartridge plan, sold as an attach (86.5).** This is the highest-scoring line because the plumber is already on site. Puretec's RO270 lists at A$1,184 and sells for A$1,099 ([Puretec](https://www.puretec.com.au/RO270); [Plumbing Sales](https://plumbingsales.com.au/filters-water/osmosis-water-filters.html)), Pure Water Systems bundles RO with a mixer tap at A$1,365–1,415 ([PWS](https://purewatersystems.com.au/products/ecohero-5-stage-reverse-osmosis-under-sink-and-premium-mixer-tap-bundle)), and at about A$1,600 installed as an add-on the attach contributes about **A$840**; membranes listed at US$7.50–11 that retail at A$120–282 ([Made-in-China](https://www.made-in-china.com/products-search/hot-china-products/RO_Membrane.html); [Aquasafe](https://aquasafe.com.au/products/dow-filmtec-tfc-reverse-osmosis-membrane-75-gal-day)) add A$150–250 a year per customer. Plumbers fit RO on its own for A$500–1,200 ([TrueFlow](https://trueflowplumbing.net.au/water-filter-system-cost/)), which is why the line fails the A$500 gate standalone and works only inside a consult. The first step is to ask the certifier handling the 080071 endorsement whether an AS 3497 point-of-use system from a maker that already holds a licence can carry the operator's brand in the same application, and to ask Ningbo Hidrotek, a US-customs-verified supplier to Canature and BWS ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/ningbo-hidrotek-co-ltd)), Ningbo Eastpure and NanJing Tsung for licence numbers, while specifying proprietary quick-change cartridge heads, because standard 10-inch housings invite generic refills.

**3. Bore and problem-water packages, including softening (77; softeners alone 76).** Iron, manganese, tannin and pH problems are sold on expertise rather than certificates: each system is designed from a water test, which suits a water-industry operator and keeps incumbents fragmented. Puretec's tannin systems list at A$5,156–5,992, its neutraliser at A$2,095 and its iron systems by quote only ([Puretec](https://www.puretec.com.au/TAN40-E1); [Puretec](https://www.puretec.com.au/NTS2000)), and its WaterMark-certified SOL30-E3 softener at A$3,097 ([Puretec](https://www.puretec.com.au/SOL30-E3)), against automatic iron-removal filters listed at US$109–129 ([Made-in-China](https://www.made-in-china.com/products-search/hot-china-products/Iron_Removal_Filter.html)) and cabinet softeners at US$110–250 ([Made-in-China](https://www.made-in-china.com/products-search/hot-china-products/Water_Softener.html)); bore systems install at A$2,500–6,000+ and a comprehensive water test costs A$150–350 ([TrueFlow](https://trueflowplumbing.net.au/water-filter-system-cost/)). A package at about A$4,750 installed contributes roughly **A$2,100**, a softener at A$3,500 about A$1,700, and media, bore cleaners, cartridges and service form a moderate annuity. The weakness is clearability: who certifies a site-assembled media system under AS 3497 is not established. The first step is a paid water test, credited against purchase, across the target bore districts; the first 10–20 results fix two or three standard packages, and one certifier quote for a packaged system built on a control valve whose maker already holds WaterMark settles the route.

**4. Boiling, chilled and filtered taps for homes and offices, as a second phase (73.5).** This is the purest brand-dominated markup in the operator's barrier family. The ACCC described Zip (Culligan) and Billi as the two largest suppliers and required Billi's divestment when Culligan bought Waterlogic ([ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/culligans-proposed-acquisition-of-waterlogic-not-opposed-subject-to-divestiture)); Zip's boiling-chilled-sparkling G5 sells for **A$5,945 against a A$7,895 compare-at price** ([Cass Brothers](https://www.cassbrothers.com.au/products/zip-hydrotap-g5-boiling-chilled-sparkling-matte-black-h55783z03au)) and its commercial G5 units for A$5,479–9,358 ([Cass Brothers](https://www.cassbrothers.com.au/products/zip-commercial-hydrotap-g5-arc-plus-boiling-chilled-tap-160-175-matte-black-h5l704z03au); [Blue Leaf Bath](https://blueleafbath.com.au/products/zip-hydrotap-g5-bc20-classic-chrome)), while a China-made Peppy 4-in-1 is already ranged at Tradelink for **A$3,500** with a five-year replacement warranty ([Tradelink](https://tradelink.com.au/4-in-1-filtered-boiling-filtered-chilled-hot-and-cold-tap-chrome-288822/)). Built from listed parts, an under-sink chiller at US$100–115 and a 5-in-1 tap at US$48–68 ([Made-in-China](https://www.made-in-china.com/products-search/hot-china-products/Water_Chiller_Tap.html)), a unit lands near A$500, so even at Peppy's price it contributes about **A$2,200**, with Billi's filter set at A$149 and CO2 cylinder at A$181 as the annuity ([Appliances Online](https://www.appliancesonline.com.au/product/billi-994004-replacement-filter-set/); [Appliances Online](https://www.appliancesonline.com.au/product/billi-996911-replacement-co2-cylinder/)). It is a second phase because the barrier is heavier for the operator too: 98 °C water at a kitchen tap, an electrical certificate that may be Level 3, and Zip's practice of tying its five-year warranty to "Zip Certified Installation" with free-install promotions ([Winnings](https://www.winnings.com.au/promotion/purchase-a-selected-zip-hydrotap-and-receive-free-certified-installation-5year-warranty-jun-2026)). The landed figure is assembled from component listings because no complete unit was found as one listing. The first step is a certifier's view on the WaterMark specification and EESS level for boiler-chiller units, plus an insurer's quote that names scald risk, before any sample is ordered.

**5. Plumbed emergency eyewash and safety showers with tepid-water and TMV service (69).** This carries the WaterMark-plus-plumber logic into the business sites a water operator already visits: laboratories, schools, pool plant rooms, water and wastewater plants, mines and chemical stores. Enware lists stainless combination showers at **A$2,493–3,054** and its tepid-water system at A$3,759 ([Enware](https://www.enware.com.au/products/tepid-water-system-for-emergency-shower-and-eye-wash-in-ss-cabinet-with-lid)), RSEA sells Speakman's SE-607 at A$2,631 ([RSEA](https://www.rsea.com.au/workplace-safety/emergency-eyewash-and-showers/pba-safety-shower-aerated-eye-face-wash-hand-foot-operated-se607)) and Storemasta a combination unit at A$2,501 ([Storemasta](https://shop.storemasta.com.au/products/psrsh027)), while Wenzhou makers list combination stations at **US$105–430 with a two-unit minimum** ([Made-in-China](https://www.made-in-china.com/products-search/hot-china-products/Emergency_Shower.html)). Installed at about A$3,000 the unit contributes roughly **A$1,000**, although the A$850 of plumbing labour and parts behind that figure is an assumption, and TMV cartridges at A$147 seed a service annuity. The evidence gaps are real: AS 4775's duty cycles were not read, the WaterMark status of a potable-connected shower was not confirmed, and a text search of the WHS regulations found no eyewash duty. The first step is to buy AS 4775, ask the plumbing regulator about WaterMark and backflow requirements, sample two units, and quote installed-plus-inspection packages 20–30% under the incumbents' list to existing industrial and pool-plant clients.

Two runners-up are strong but conditional. Interconnected smoke alarms (72) have the best factory-level evidence in the study, because ActivFire certificates name the Chinese factory as registrant and the Australian brand as agent, and they have a legal driver: Queensland requires interconnected photoelectric alarms in all dwellings by 1 January 2027, with hardwired alarms replaced by hardwired ones ([QFD fact sheet](https://www.fire.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-06/Smoke-Alarm-Fact-Sheet.pdf)). The line needs an electrician, though, and the deadline is about 12 weeks away while a new 500-unit order would take roughly as long to land, so the durable business is the 2027–2032 ten-year replacement cycle, Victoria's annual rental checks ([VBA](https://www.vba.vic.gov.au/consumers/residential-tenancies-regulations-2021)) and landlord subscriptions near A$149 a year ([Coast Smoke Alarms](https://coastsmokealarms.com.au/landlords/)). Commercial warewashing with kitchen water treatment (68) is the business twin of the filter: Washtech's XP pass-through sells at A$6,781 + GST against hood-type machines listed at US$700–1,500, plumbers connect every unit, and chemicals and filters form an annuity, but whether the whole machine or only its backflow device needs WaterMark certification has to be settled with a certifier first.

Three service lines are worth bolting on without a brand. Backflow testing is a mandated 12-monthly annuity on the operator's home turf, at A$150–320 for the first device and A$90–145 for each additional one, carried out by plumbers with a backflow endorsement ([What's The Damage](https://whatsthedamage.com.au/backflow-testing-cost-australia/)). Portable fire-equipment servicing is licence-gated, with Queensland's class requiring two years' experience ([QBCC](https://qbcc.qld.gov.au/licences/apply-licence/available-licences/fire-protection/portable-install-maintain)), so it is better bought than built, as SCEE's Force Fire deal shows. First-aid restocking at A$50 + GST a kit ([St John Queensland](https://www.stjohnqld.com.au/first-aid-kit-restocking)) rides the same business-site visits.

## Kings, Temu and Kmart show exactly where the thesis breaks

Kings is the cleanest natural experiment in the study. 4WD Supacentre describes itself as a discounter with more than 31 stores and 750,000 customers that "cuts out the middleman" ([BHG](https://www.bhg.com.au/promo-codes/4wd-supacentre/)), and wherever it competes the brand layer is gone: its 12,000 lb winch sells for **A$599** and its 40 A DC-DC charger for A$179 ([Kings storefront](https://sc-prod.4wdsc.com/graphql)), against **A$2,999** for Warn's Zeon 12-S ([4x4 Down Under](https://4x4downunder.com.au/products/warn-zeon-12s-12v-12000lbs-electric-winch-95950)) and A$695–778 for REDARC's BCDC1250D ([Autopro](https://autopro.com.au/ol/Autopro-Category/Brands/Redarc/Redarc-BCDC-Classic-Under-Bonnet-50A-DC-Battery-Charger---BCDC1250D/p/EL30864)), while its 50-litre 12 V fridge sells for A$179–349, a range that starts below a small importer's modelled landed cost of about A$240 ([4WD Supacentre](https://www.4wdsupacentre.com.au/fridge-freezers.html)). Across those categories Kings retails at 1.5–4 times landed cost. Yet the same storefront returned no bull bars, tow bars, suspension, canopies, trays or camper trailers, the categories gated by per-model airbag evidence, approved-facility ADR 62/02 testing ([DITRDCA](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/guidance-note-what-is-an-approved-calculation-for-adr-62_0.pdf)) and engineering sign-off. A well-funded importer that has flattened every trust-only category still stays out of the barrier-gated ones, which is the strongest available evidence for the thesis and the strongest argument against entering trust-only categories.

Clarus's Rhino-Rack shows what that flattening does to a brand with no barrier behind it. Clarus bought Rhino-Rack for about US$198m in 2021; by 2025 the Adventure segment that houses it earned a gross margin of about 35%, down from about 40%, ran a **US$42.5m operating loss**, carried fully impaired goodwill, and took a US$26.1m impairment "specifically the Rhino-Rack and MAXTRAX trademarks", citing "a challenging wholesale market in Australia" ([Clarus 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/913277/000110465926024159/clar-20251231x10k.htm)); US customs records show its racks come from Winbo-Dongjian in China and King Rack in Taiwan ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/rhino-rack-usa)). ARB, by contrast, owns Thai factories, vehicle-specific engineering that now integrates with driver-assist systems, a fitting network and Ford-licensed accessory status ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARB_Corporation)), and pushed through two list-price rises in a year when its Australian aftermarket sales fell 3.3% ([Montgomery](https://rogermontgomery.com/arbs-fy26-a-look-under-the-hood/); [StockWireX](https://stockwirex.com/asx-stock-news/consumer-discretionary/arb-corporation-fy26-results-august-2026/)).

Kmart and Temu flatten the layer from the other two directions. Kmart internalised it: Anko became its umbrella house brand in 2019, Kmart Group earned A$1,109m EBT on A$11,751m of revenue ([Wesfarmers FY26](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260827/pdf/0738z7fxb48k84.pdf)), and it buys from factories it does not own ([Wesfarmers](https://www.wesfarmers.com.au/docs/default-source/sustainability/sustainability-2026/2026-wesfarmers-modern-slavery-statement.pdf?sfvrsn=f2acafbb_1)) but names in a published list ([Kmart](https://www.kmart.com.au/factory-list/)). Temu collapses brand, distribution and retail into a parcel stream, with **5 million Australian shoppers and close to A$3bn of sales in 2025** ([Roy Morgan](https://www.roymorgan.com/findings/10158-amazon-temu-and-shein-growth-story-continues-march-2026)) at prices 30–40% below Amazon's on comparable items by Jarden's analysis ([ChannelNews](https://www.channelnews.com.au/amazon-temu-and-shein-to-capture-a-third-of-australias-online-retail-market/)); Wesfarmers' chief executive partly blamed Temu and Shein for the closure of Catch ([ChannelNews](https://www.channelnews.com.au/wesfarmers-retailersbunnings-officeworks-face-pressure-as-temu-and-shein-surge-in-australia/)). Neither can install a plumbed-in product, hold a WaterMark licence for it or carry the plumber's liability, which is why the installed categories in this report are insulated while the parcel-sized Marketplace categories examined earlier were not.

The same forces already reach into water. Licence 080071 keeps uncertified filters out of plumbers' hands, but not identical certified ones under other labels, so the reference cabinet's supply-only price competes with AquaSafe's stainless twin at A$975 and Puretec's 10-year-warranty WH2-30 at A$826 ([Buildmat](https://www.buildmat.com.au/products/puretec-wh2-30-lt-whole-house-dual-water-filter-system)); shower filters listed at US$3–44 sell for A$84–152 to DIY buyers with no WaterMark claim in sight ([PWS](https://purewatersystems.com.au/products/inline-shower-filter)); and Chinese charger makers sell 22 kW OCPP units under their own names for A$950 against A$2,200 for ABB ([Gridly](https://www.gridly.com.au/ev-chargers/abb-terra-wallbox-22kw)). The test that separates survivors from casualties has three parts: the barrier must bind at the point of installation, it must name a licensee or installer who carries the liability, and clearing it must cost more per unit than a parcel seller's margin can absorb, or require a licensed trade that seller lacks. Where trust is the only barrier, where a scaled importer can spread a certificate's cost across national volume, or where the channel owner puts its own brand on the product, the markup goes.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "thinking deeper into the value chain" should mean for this operator. It does not mean finding a new branded category elsewhere; it means climbing the expertise and liability gradient inside the barrier family the operator already clears, from mains filters to tank-water UV, bore chemistry and boiling-chilled units, and carrying the same WaterMark-plus-plumber gate into the laboratories, pool plant rooms and commercial kitchens a water business already serves. The certificate itself is a commodity input that five unrelated brands share on one licence. The durable advantages are the channel that removes the merchant and installer markups, a consumable annuity designed in from the first unit through proprietary cartridge heads, lamp timers and service plans, and eventually a licence in the operator's own name that partner plumbers install under, Crimsafe-style. "Middlemanning" is the wrong word for the opportunity: the filings show the middle is the thinnest layer in the chain, and the money goes to whoever removes it.

The next dollars should buy facts rather than stock, because the decisive facts are cheap. An afternoon in the WaterMark Product Database answers who holds licence 080071, whether PWF-203BC itself is listed with lead-free certification, and whether any UV, RO or media-filter maker holds an AS 3497 licence that can carry another brand; three certifier quotes price Route A against Route B; and one broker quote that names escape-of-water and scald risk prices the liability the whole strategy rests on. Culligan's purchase of CHF suggests an installed base built this way has a strategic buyer at the end, and Clarus's write-downs on Rhino-Rack suggest it keeps that value only while a barrier stands behind the brand.
