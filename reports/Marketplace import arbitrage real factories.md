# Customs Records Unmask Traders, Not Fifth Middlemen

The challenge is right about our earlier supplier list and wrong about how long the chain is. Checked against US customs bills of lading, stock-exchange filings, patents and auditor portals, **7 of the 16 suppliers our earlier report named for the six products are traders, 3 are a consolidator or a factory's export arm, and 2 are real factories that probably buy the product in; only Lingsha (trampolines), Jiecang and Prorials (desks) and Heze Zhongran (cubbies) make what they offered**. Yet the same records show one or two layers between factory and importer, not five, and they name real makers for every product, mostly in clusters the earlier report missed: Qingdao for brand-grade trampolines; Qingdao, Wangdu and Xiamen rather than Nantong for bumper plates; Ningbo and Longyou for hammock stands; Cao County for cubbies; and Jinhua prefecture for aluminium camp furniture. Identical SKUs show a shared design rather than a long chain, because they come from expired patents and a common bill of materials, factories selling catalogue designs to many resellers, a few drive-kit makers behind most desks, and traders reusing factory photos. Buying from the factory saves 19% on the trampoline and 13–16% on the cubby, but it saves nothing visible on plates and hammocks, whose trader lists sit at or below factory lists, and it costs more on desks, where the real column maker charges more than the frame assemblers; the camp-kitchen price we used was a trader's low outlier. Re-costed on factory evidence, the trampoline holds at **A$97 a unit (31%)** on a 128-unit container its factory will accept, bumper plates reach **A$95–109 (31–36%)** if moulders quote US$1.10–1.20/kg, the cubby rises from A$73 to **A$92–96 (34–35%)** on Zhongran's 300-unit minimum, the hammock falls to **A$15–23** at factory-type prices of US$25–30, desks bought from Jiecang **lose A$40 a unit** on 100 desks, and the camp kitchen drops to **A$5–20**. The cheapest reliable check is about three hours of free desk work and three documents per supplier, then a US$210–300 audit day before the first container; because no Chinese registry record, written quote or Australian import manifest could be obtained, every maker named here except the listed companies is "probable", not proven.

## Seven of sixteen named suppliers turn out to be traders

Platform labels cannot show who owns production, because suppliers buy them. On one Made-in-China (MIC) results page for aluminium camp kitchens, **all 30 cards carried an "Audited Supplier" badge, including those of declared trading companies** ([MIC](https://www.made-in-china.com/products-search/hot-china-products/Camping_Kitchen_Table.html)). Alibaba's Gold Supplier tier is "not a factory or quality audit" ([Tianwen Wangluo](https://tianwenwangluo.com/en/is-alibaba-legit/)), and 1688's "real-factory guarantee" pays a buyer who proves the seller is not a factory at most 5% of the order, capped at ¥500 ([1688](https://www.1688.com/factory/b2b-1726331428.html)).

This round therefore graded suppliers only on evidence a reseller cannot easily fake. **A-grade** evidence is an official registry record showing production scope, an industrial address and a credible insured headcount; US customs records showing the company as direct shipper to established Western brands from an industrial address, with goods that fit one production technology; a certificate naming the factory site; a patent on the SKU held by the contracting company; or a listed company's audited filings. **B-grade** evidence is the weaker version of each, such as customs shipments to small importers or Amazon sellers, an auditor-verified capacity block confirmed on the auditor's own portal, an own website with an ICP licence (China's official website registration), a trade-show booth, or a patent on production equipment rather than the product. Badges, "Manufacturer" labels and self-descriptions are **C-grade** and never count. A **confirmed factory** needs two A items from different source families, or one A plus two B from three families; a **probable factory** has one A, or two to three B; and a supplier showing two trader red flags, such as an office-tower address plus unrelated product lines, is **treated as a trader** whatever its badges say. Customs records answer the "fifth middleman" question directly, because they show who handed the container to the ship. Across the six products the chain runs from the factory through one middle layer, which is the factory's own export company, a trading house or the brand's own China office, to the importer, and big brands use that layer as much as small buyers do. Rogue Fitness's largest partner by containers is **Rich York International, a Hong Kong trader that has shipped Rogue 716 containers and now ships lamps, folding trolleys and cat trees to other buyers** ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/rich-york-international-limited)). A 2025 bill from the Ningbo trader Ningbo Prime to Sunnydaze's importing company reads "HAMMOCK HAMMOCK STAND PO#IAG- DANLONG HAMMOCKS", naming the factory behind the trader ([ImportGenius](https://static.importgenius.com/suppliers/ningbo-prime-import-export-co-ltd)). Costway's US importer buys through Ningbo Joys, an export agent in its own group that has shipped it 23,549 containers ([ImportGenius](https://static.importgenius.com/importers/goplus-corp)); Outsunny's owner Aosom ships from its own export companies ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/aosom-llc)); and KidKraft's top "supplier" until 2019 was its own KidKraft Trading (Fuzhou) ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/kidkraft-inc)). The challenge is right that a small buyer browsing a platform usually meets that middle layer rather than the maker: the Fujian mills behind Backyard Discovery's thousands of containers, and Heze Jinran, which has shipped KidKraft 806 containers, do not appear in MIC's playhouse directory at all, while traders do ([MIC](https://www.made-in-china.com/factory/playhouse.html)).

Identical SKUs show a shared design, not a long chain, and none of the four mechanisms behind them needs five middlemen. The first is expired design protection plus a common bill of materials. JumpSport's 1998 patent on the pole-and-net trampoline enclosure has expired ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6053845A/en)); a Chinese factory's 14 ft specification (80 springs, 4 legs, a 1.8 m net) matches the parts list in Lifespan Kids' 14 ft BounceZone manual ([Lifespan](https://share.lifespanonline.com.au/manuals/Kids%20Manual/LSK_Manual_BounceZone%20Round%20Trampoline_Digital.pdf)); three unrelated MIC sellers ship 14 ft sets in cartons of about 172×55×23 cm weighing 59–62 kg ([Lingsha](https://lsfunjump.en.made-in-china.com/product/uphRrGWYIgci/China-Funjump-14FT-Outdoor-Indoor-Fitness-Kid-Child-Mini-Bungee-Jumping-Trampoline-with-Safety-Enclosure.html)); and the space-saving hammock stand goes back to a 1991 US patent that has also expired ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5113538A/en)). The second is factory catalogues sold to everyone: Heze Zhongran lists **408 playhouse SKUs under its own "Toys Valley" brand at a 300-unit minimum** while making KidKraft's exclusive models in the same plant ([MIC](https://chinakidstoy.en.made-in-china.com/product-group/beQToaxjIlpE/Playhouse-1.html)). The third is a few component makers behind many brands: Jiecang's Malaysian plant ships the same "adjustable table parts" to UPLIFT, HAT, Element Ergo and BestQi ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/jstar-motion-sdn-bhd)), and six rubber moulders supply the bumper plates of Rogue, Core Health, Bells of Steel, Fringe, Titan and Rage. The fourth is relisting: a Ningbo trader's MIC hammock listing copies the Amazon Basics title and its "30 Pounds" weight word for word ([MIC](https://mugeneralunion.en.made-in-china.com/product/cngpdloJqmVY/China-Basics-Double-Hammock-with-9-Foot-Space-Saving-Steel-Stand-and-Carrying-Case-Multi-Color-400-Lb-Capacity.html)), and a one-year-old Baoding seller on 1688, China's domestic wholesale site, copied a Ningbo seller's camp-kitchen title ([1688](https://www.1688.com/market/-BBA7CDE2B3F8B7BFD7C0.html)). A matching photo or carton therefore says nothing about how many hands a unit passed through; the bill of lading, which names the shipper of record, and the export declaration, which names the producing company (生产销售单位), do.

### Trampolines: Qingdao's brand factories will not sell 128 units, but Lingsha will

The 12–14 ft trampolines sold under Western brands come from a handful of container-scale factories that make products for brands to sell under their own names (OEMs, or ODMs when the factory also designs them), and most are in Qingdao rather than the Lishui–Yongkang–Jinhua belt the earlier report named. The definitive one is **Sportsoul (青岛三柏硕健康科技, Shenzhen-listed 001300), a confirmed factory**: its IPO prospectus describes tube-making, welding, powder-coating and assembly at its Chengyang plant, names Decathlon, Walmart, Amazon, ICON, Plum Products and Escalade as customers, and reports **1.136 million trampolines sold in 2021** ([Sportsoul prospectus](https://disc.static.szse.cn/disc/disk03/finalpage/2022-09-23/af15bcac-c370-4195-8868-dc6032ae30a8.PDF)), while US customs show it shipping trampolines directly to Skywalker and North Trampoline as recently as 8 October 2026 ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/sportsoul-co-ltd)). Five more are probable factories on one A-grade customs record each: **Qingdao Jinhuaxing Sports Products**, with 1,041 containers to Propel Trampolines plus Vuly's US arm and Rural King ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/qingdao-jinhuaxing-sports-products)); its namesake **Qingdao Jinhuaxing Metal**, which shipped US Kmart, Spin Master and Vuly ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/qingdao-jinhuaxing-metal-co-ltd)); **Hangzhou Transasia** in Xiaoshan, which ships Acon, JumpSport and Super Jumper ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/hangzhou-transasia-sporting-goods)); **Kogee Outdoor** in Taishan, which ships BERG and North Trampoline ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/kogee-outdoor-group)); and **Dongguan Sheng Hui**, with 670 containers to Springfree and 27 to Costco Wholesale Canada ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/dongguan-sheng-hui-fitness-equipmen)). "Owning production" here means making the frame, because even Sportsoul buys its springs, mats and nets from specialist suppliers and subcontracts 5.9–7.7% of its purchases, including galvanising and sewing ([Sportsoul prospectus](https://disc.static.szse.cn/disc/disk03/finalpage/2022-09-23/af15bcac-c370-4195-8868-dc6032ae30a8.PDF)). None of these brand factories sells on small-buyer terms. Sportsoul's own listing asks for **500 pieces per item** on bank transfer (T/T) ([MIC](https://www.made-in-china.com/showroom/sportsoul/product-detailMZOfbHIYEaWh/China-12-FT-13-FT-14-FT-Square-Trampoline-with-Enclosure.html)), about 109 m³ of one SKU or more than one and a half 40ft containers, and its top five customers took 78–89% of revenue, buying FOB (free on board) on credit.

The factory that will take a 128-unit first order is **Zhejiang Lingsha Technology (Funjump)** in Jinyun County, Lishui, and its evidence is real but sits one grade below the brand factories. US customs show it as exporter of record of full containers of "SPORTS TRAMPOLINE", 355 cartons of 49 kg each, from the Lijin Hardware Technology Industrial Park, but its consignees are Amazon-type importers rather than brands (B) ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/zhejiang-lingsha-technology-co-ltd)). It holds 2023 utility patents on its own tube-bending, tube-cutting and laser-welding equipment rather than on the trampoline itself (B) ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN219724210U/zh)). Its MIC production block, with 6 lines, a steel-pipe line, pipe benders and welders, is marked "verified by TÜV Rheinland", and TÜV's portal confirms report MIC-ASR2531608 with an audit dated 10 October 2025 (B) ([MIC](https://lsfunjump.en.made-in-china.com/company-Zhejiang-Lingsha-Technology-Co-Ltd.html); [TÜV](https://www.verified.chn.tuv.com/en/)). Three B items from three families make it a **probable factory**; a registry check of its business scope and insured headcount would make it confirmed, and should also settle its self-reported headcount, which reads 142, 152 and "300+" on different pages. Its listed terms fit a small importer: **a minimum of 100 units with 50 per size and 3–4 sizes allowed in one 20ft container, a 30% deposit (about US$3,690 on 128 units at US$96) with 70% due against a copy of the bill of lading or by sight letter of credit, 20–45 working days, and a US$200 sample** ([MIC](https://lsfunjump.en.made-in-china.com/product/uphRrGWYIgci/China-Funjump-14FT-Outdoor-Indoor-Fitness-Kid-Child-Mini-Bungee-Jumping-Trampoline-with-Safety-Enclosure.html)). The price is a genuine factory price rather than a trader's. Sportsoul's audited 8–14 ft average selling price was **US$99.81–120.00 in 2019–2021, against an average unit cost of US$74–89** and a gross margin of 25.6–29.8%, converted at Federal Reserve average exchange rates ([Sportsoul prospectus](https://disc.static.szse.cn/disc/disk03/finalpage/2022-09-23/af15bcac-c370-4195-8868-dc6032ae30a8.PDF); [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h10/hist/dat00_ch.htm)), so Lingsha's US$96 sits between an efficient maker's cost and its price and leaves little room to negotiate. It also buys a lighter build than Australia's brands: Lingsha's 14 ft ships at 62 kg with tube walls from 1.0 to 1.5 mm, while Everfit's 14 ft weighs 74.3 kg ([Amazon AU](https://www.amazon.com.au/dp/B08FWVCRG1)). The trader alternative, Beijing Set Sail, lists a near-identical 62 kg carton at **US$119, 24% more, with a 300-unit minimum, three times the factory's** ([MIC](https://setsailsports.en.made-in-china.com/product/vOtTxfGMgZYq/China-Outdoor-14FT-Trampoline-with-Safety-Enclosure-Net.html)). Still unproven are Lingsha's registry record, the full TÜV report, its AS 4989 and EN 71-14 safety-standard claims (C-grade until a test report naming the factory is seen) and whether US$96 holds for a 128-unit order.

### Bumper plates: Shandong, Hebei and Xiamen moulders, not Nantong consolidators

US bills of lading put the rubber-moulding presses behind Western plate brands in Shandong, Hebei and Fujian, not in Nantong. **Deluxe (Xiamen) Sports Equipment** in Tong'an Industrial Park has shipped Rogue 339 containers of bumper plates and ships "BUMPER PLATE" to Core Health & Fitness in 2025–26 ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/deluxe-xiamen-sports-equipment-co)); **Heng Li Heng** of Wangdu, Hebei, shipped Rogue 169 containers and Muscle Driver USA 303 ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/heng-li-heng-rubber-and-plastic)); **Qingdao DH Sport Equipment** in Jimo ships Bells of Steel, Fringe Sport and TKO ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/qingdao-dh-sport-equipment-co-ltd)); **Qingdao Hiperlion** has shipped Titan 56 containers, with loads still arriving in July–October 2026 ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/qingdao-hiperlion-fitness-technolog)); and **Shandong Zhuoao** (Linshu) and **Ri Zhao Bull King** (Wulian) ship plates to Rage, Gibson Athletic and Bells of Steel ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/shandong-zhuoao-sports-equipment-co); [ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/ri-zhao-bull-king-sports-technology)). Each is a **probable factory on one A-grade customs item**; none yet has a second family of evidence, such as a registry scope listing manufacturing (制造) or footage of a press line. Nantong's exporters ship steel racks, benches, bars and mixed accessory loads instead, which changes the status of both Nantong firms the earlier report relied on. **Tengtai** makes racks and coated dumbbells, but its trading team sources plates from what it calls "more than 300 suppliers", so its US$1.00/kg is a consolidator's list price. **Ironman** is a confirmed factory, but its visible bills are machines and benches, so its US$1.29–1.49/kg plate listing is probably bought in ([MIC](https://ironmaster-group.en.made-in-china.com/product/FZyxsYlOlpWn/China-Colorful-Bumper-Plates-for-Strength-Training.html)).

The moulders do take a container of the size a small Australian importer would order. Qingdao DH shipped Rage Fitness single loads of **17.2–20.2 t**, Heng Li Heng shipped Carolina Fitness Equipment **17.5–19.8 t (1,340–1,650 plates) a container**, and Zhuoao shipped Gibson Athletic 17.1 t ([ImportGenius Rage](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/rage-fitness-supply); [ImportGenius Carolina](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/carolina-fitness-equipment)), against the 211 sets (21.1 t) that fill a 20ft box to Queensland's 21.8 t weight limit ([ICE Cargo](https://icecargo.com.au/transport-options/)). Published minimums run 0.1–1 t, terms are typically a 30% T/T deposit with the balance due against a copy of the bill of lading, and lead times run from 15 days to 3 months ([MIC Grandway](https://dexuhongtu.en.made-in-china.com/product/SauUjfewHPrR/China-Colorful-Rubber-Bumper-Plates-Customized-Logo-Weight-Plate.html); [MIC Tengtai](https://tt-sports.en.made-in-china.com/company-Nantong-Tengtai-Sporting-Fitness-Co-Ltd-.html)). Customs records show no values and none of the moulders publishes a price list, while platform lists show **no consistent trader markup**: the consolidator Tengtai lists US$1.00/kg, the trader Grandway US$1.40, US$1.30 and US$1.20 at 1–5, 5–10 and 10-plus tonnes, and the factory Ironman US$1.29–1.49 ([MIC Tengtai bumper](https://tt-sports.en.made-in-china.com/product/mQlUwAIbaHVq/China-Gym-Fitness-Bumper-Plate-Rubber-Competition-Weight-Bumper-Plate.html)). ### Hammock stands: Ningbo and Longyou makers hide behind Ningbo traders

Most hammock-stand containers bound for Western brands leave Ningbo under trading-house names. **Ningbo Prime**, with 4,481 shipments including 869 containers to Patio Garden Direct and 352 to Algoma, also ships embroidery thread, quilts, mops and squat racks ([ImportGenius](https://static.importgenius.com/suppliers/ningbo-prime-imp-exp-co-ltd)), and **Zhejiang Hengming**, Vivere's main source, also ships bird feeders, cat litter and sheds ([ImportGenius](https://static.importgenius.com/suppliers/zhejiang-hengming-imp-exp-co-ltd)). Behind them sit a few direct shippers that ship only hammocks and stands. The best evidenced is **Ningbo Danlong Furniture, a probable factory**: it holds US design patent D957174 for a hammock stand (A) ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD957174S1/en)), ships "HAMMOCK WITH STAND" itself to Lidl US and to Techfaith, a Georgia importer, in lots of 780–1,010 cartons (B, because its address could not be seen) ([ImportGenius](https://static.importgenius.com/suppliers/ningbo-danlong-furniture-co-ltd)), and is the factory named on Ningbo Prime's purchase order to Sunnydaze. Three more have customs patterns of A strength but, with no visible shipper address, count as one B item each until confirmed: **Ningbo Deja Leisure** shipped Best Choice Products 75 containers of stands up to June 2024 ([ImportGenius](https://static.importgenius.com/suppliers/ningbo-deja-leisure-products-co-lt)), and **Longyou Huarun Tourism Products** and **Zhejiang Excellent Outdoor**, both in Longyou, Quzhou, ship The Hammock Source, Sunnydaze and Algoma's orders for the US retailer Menards ([ImportGenius](https://static.importgenius.com/suppliers/longyou-huarun-tourism-products-co); [ImportGenius](https://static.importgenius.com/suppliers/zhejiang-excellent-outdoor-products)). The two suppliers the earlier report used are further from production: **Ningbo Lounger is a trader** whose own customs history is mainly paper cups, plastic gloves and cutlery with a few hammock lots, and **Ningbo Xusheng** is the export arm of a Linhai swing-chair plant whose "double hammock" listings reuse swing-chair specifications ([MIC](https://xushengcompany.en.made-in-china.com/product/tZsAeqGordRV/China-Outdoor-Patio-Double-Hammock-with-Space-Saving-Steel-Stand.html)).

The design is in the public domain, so the sameness across storefronts reflects shared tooling and copied listings. The real factories mostly ship brand-sized lots of **530–2,860 cartons per bill**, although Jarder Home sent an Amazon seller lots of 118 and 190 cartons in 2026 ([ImportGenius](https://static.importgenius.com/importers/suncreat-inc-c-o-fba)), so **200–460 sets is feasible but at the bottom of what these factories serve**. Expect a demand for a 300–500-set run, a referral to the factory's export company, or a less-than-container (LCL) lot at a higher price, on a 10–30% deposit with the balance against a copy of the bill of lading and 15–35 days' production ([MIC](https://xushengcompany.en.made-in-china.com/product/tZsAeqGordRV/China-Outdoor-Patio-Double-Hammock-with-Space-Saving-Steel-Stand.html)). Listed prices show **no trader markup to avoid**: Lounger asks US$20–21 at 200 sets, Xusheng US$20–30 at 100 sets and US$27–35 at 560, and stand-only listings run US$15.80–29.90 ([MIC Lounger](https://lounger2.en.made-in-china.com/product/zatrBMfGjmRi/China-Easy-Assemble-Folding-Double-Portable-Hammock-with-Stand.html)). The reason to find the factory is weld quality. Australian sets such as Gardeon's (32 mm tube, about 12 kg) belong to a lighter class than US brand stands, which ship at 15–25 kg a carton ([Gardeon](https://www.gardeon.com.au/products/gardeon-hammock-bed-with-steel-frame-stand)), and the US Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled about **32,500 knock-down stands because "the weld attaching the stand to the base can break"** ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2022/The-Hammock-Source-Recalls-Key-West-Knock-Down-Hammock-Stands-Due-to-Fall-Hazard-Sold-Exclusively-at-Lowes)). No factory price, Chinese registry name or small-order policy was found for Danlong, Deja or the Longyou firms.

### Standing desks: three listed drive-kit makers sit behind most frames

A dual-motor desk passes through three production layers, and only the first is rare: actuator makers supply the columns, motors and control boxes, frame fabricators cut, weld and coat the steel and fit the bought-in kits, and board makers supply the tops. Three of the actuator makers are **confirmed factories through their stock-exchange filings, patents and customs records**. **Jiecang** (Xinchang; Shanghai-listed, 603583) made 4.77 million linear-drive sets in 2025 with 5,043 staff across plants in Ningbo, Xinchang, Malaysia, the US and Europe ([Jiecang 2025 annual report](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN202604271821616543_1.pdf)); **Kaidi** (Changzhou; 605288) made 7.98 million sets, mostly recliner drives, and sells "STEEL FRAME / CONTROL BOX / HANDSET" sets directly to the office-furniture maker Teknion ([Kaidi 2025 annual report](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN202604291821754028_1.pdf); [ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/changzhou-kaidi-electrical-inc)); and **Loctek** (Ningbo; 300729) can make at least 2.5 million lift sets a year, mainly for its own FlexiSpot brand ([Loctek 2026 interim report](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN202608280006758408_1.pdf)). Western brands buy from this first layer directly. Jiecang's Malaysian plant shipped UPLIFT 616 containers of "adjustable table parts" ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/the-human-solution-uplift-desk)), its export subsidiary has shipped HAT Contract, AMQ, Bush and Secretlab ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/xinchang-jiecang-import-and-export)), and an UPLIFT control box carries Jiecang's JCB35 model prefix ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/405964185266)). Jiecang also led the drafting of China's industry standard for electric lifting desks ([Jiecang](https://www.jiecang.cn/)), one reason so many frames look alike. The other desk suppliers sit lower down. **Prorials** in Cixi is a real fabricator and a probable factory on two B items, which began in monitor and TV stands and shows no motor or controller line ([Prorials](https://www.prorials.com/company-profile.html)); **Lumi Legend** is the export arm of the Ningbo LUMI Group and ships VIVO from an office tower ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/c-knapp-sales-inc-dba-vivo)); **Ningbo Aoke** builds frames for Autonomous, Bush and Bestar, fitting Autonomous's with electronics from TiMOTION, a Taiwanese actuator maker ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/ningbo-aoke-office-equipment-co-lt); [BTOD](https://www.btod.com/blog/autonomous-pro-vs-jarvis-desk)); and **Ningbo Yunqi**, which ships "lifting column, hand controller, desk top" sets to small US brands from an office suite, is the trader layer the challenge describes ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/ningbo-yunqi-innovation-intelligent)).

Here the real maker is open to small buyers but dearer. Jiecang's MIC storefront is the listed company itself, with a **five-piece minimum and a complete JC35TS-R13R dual-motor desk at US$179 for 10–99 units, US$159 for 100–499 and US$129 for 500 or more** ([MIC](https://jiecang.en.made-in-china.com/product/ewtfMmyoEWhc/China-Jiecang-Dual-Motor-Luxury-Height-Adjustable-Executive-Standing-Desk.html)). Its prospectus says it "usually requires new customers to prepay the full amount when signing the order" (in translation), and in 2018 even UPLIFT, Fully, HAT and AMQ paid 100% before their goods were released ([Jiecang prospectus](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN201808191180596187_1.pdf)). The other two kit makers fit worse: **Kaidi** sells only directly to manufacturers and brands and publishes no small-order terms, while **Loctek** owns FlexiSpot's Australian subsidiary and writes online-channel restrictions into its contracts with the brands it makes desks for ([Loctek prospectus](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN201711201053206901_1.pdf)). The assemblers are cheaper and take 100 units. Prorials lists its dual-motor frame at **US$76 with a 30% deposit and 70% before delivery** in about 35 days ([MIC](https://chinapurui.en.made-in-china.com/product/JRbUSIKobqVf/China-Fast-Lift-Dual-Motor-Electric-Height-Adjustable-Standing-Office-Desk-Frame.html)), and Lumi lists a complete desk at US$99–129 ([MIC](https://lumi2007.en.made-in-china.com/product/MolxpvyJXUDj/China-Modern-Home-Office-Furniture-Wholesale-3-Stage-Dual-Motors-Electric-Standing-Table-Gaming-Desk.html)), so a frame plus a 140×70 cm top costs roughly **US$90–130 from an assembler against US$159 from Jiecang**. Brand buyers paid less still: 2018 customs bills implied US$78–95 a set for Fully and HAT ([Jiemian](https://m.jiemian.com/article/2484732.html)). Jiecang's one advantage is paperwork, as the only candidate claiming Australian SAA electrical approvals in a regulated filing ([Jiecang 2025 annual report](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN202604271821616543_1.pdf)). Still unproven are which column maker supplies Prorials' and Lumi's kits, what small-order terms Kaidi and TiMOTION offer, and whether Jiecang's SAA certificates cover the JC35TS kit.

### Cubbies: a KidKraft supplier sells its own catalogue at a 300-unit minimum

The timber playhouses sold by Western brands come from three areas: Zhuangzhai Town in Cao County, Shandong, a national wood-panel base ([Dazhong](https://m.dzplus.dzng.com/share/general/0/NEWS3693564RBASVTRFVSINI)); Nanping prefecture in northern Fujian; and Longquan in Lishui. **Heze Jinran Woodware** shipped KidKraft 806 containers and Walmart 73, and KidKraft codes the playhouses Jinran ships "ZGR", which suggests a link to Zhongran that no registry yet proves ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/heze-jinran-woodware-co-ltd)). **Heze Zhongran Woodware**, in the same town, ships KidKraft's Lantana Lane and Riverscape playhouses under its own name, plus orders for Costco Wholesale Canada and PriceSmart ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/heze-zhongran-woodware-co-ltd)). In Fujian, **Shunchang Shengsheng** and the exporter **Wanmulin** ship thousands of containers of cedar swing sets, with FSC chain-of-custody numbers on the bills, to Backyard Discovery's parent company ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/leisure-time-products-inc)). Of all these, only Zhongran and Xiamen Warmwood, a part-maker, part-trader exporter, appear on MIC. **Zhongran is a probable factory**: its customs record is A-grade, but its second item is still missing, because its SGS-verified capacity block (10 lines, CNC routers, panel saws, paint lines) could not be checked on SGS's CAPTCHA-protected portal ([MIC](https://chinakidstoy.en.made-in-china.com/company-Heze-Zhongran-Woodware-Co-Ltd.html)). **Ningbo Dreamgo**, whose price the earlier ranking used, is a trader: an office unit with two trade staff and 30 partner factories that quotes a playhouse for delivery in five days from a single set ([MIC](https://dreamgotoys.en.made-in-china.com/company-Ningbo-Dreamgo-Toys-Co-Ltd-.html)).

Zhongran will take a small importer's container, but at its own minimum. Nearly all of its 408 playhouse listings carry a **300-set minimum, a 1–3-month lead time, and payment by T/T, letter of credit or documents against payment (D/P)** ([MIC](https://chinakidstoy.en.made-in-china.com/product-group/beQToaxjIlpE/Playhouse-1.html)), and its customs record shows it already serves unknown buyers: Sun Won Corp, a small Texas importer, received 201 and 110 cartons of "OUTDOOR PLAYHOUSE" in April 2026 ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/sun-won-corp)). The factory is also cheaper than the trader. Its enclosed fir playhouse with a flower box (108×106×142 cm, 28.5 kg) lists at **US$87–90, or US$3.05–3.16 per kilogram, against US$103.88, or US$4.33 per kilogram, for Dreamgo's playhouse at 50–499 units** ([MIC](https://chinakidstoy.en.made-in-china.com/product/mxyUzplJvdrK/China-Chinese-Fir-Outdoor-Backyard-Wooden-Playhouse-with-Wooden-Flower-Box.html); [MIC](https://dreamgotoys.en.made-in-china.com/product/mZhAywdJlOGV/China-Wooden-Outdoor-Playhouse-with-Pretend-Cooktop-Sink-BBQ-Grill-Sandbox-Under-Canopy.html)). The catch is volume: each carton is 0.18 m³, so 300 fill 54 m³ and the first order becomes a 40ft container, or about 155 units in a 20ft box if Zhongran waives its minimum. Buying direct also puts biosecurity in the factory's hands. Solid fir is not on the list of "highly processed" wooden articles exempt from treatment, so each consignment needs offshore treatment by an acceptable provider, export within six months of treatment and a storage declaration ([DAFF](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/industry-advice/2017/71-2017)); methyl-bromide fumigation certificates from China must carry the endorsement of China's customs authority, the GACC ([DAFF](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/QID108450_China%20230602.pdf)), which a factory exporting in its own name can arrange. Zhongran's registry record, the scope of its FSC certificate and its willingness to sell 200 units all remain unverified.

### Camp kitchens: no verified cupboard-kitchen maker, and the price we used was a trader's

Every camp-kitchen supplier the earlier rounds named is a trader. **Welfull Group**, whose US$25–29.50 price set the earlier margin, works from Hangzhou office towers, has made more than 4,000 US shipments of apparel, wall art, holiday knitwear and pet goods to 526 customers with no camping goods among the visible records, and presents a Bureau Veritas audit of "Partner Production Capacity" and 2,000 supply-chain partners ([Panjiva](https://panjiva.com/Welfull-Group-Co-Ltd/4255217); [MIC](https://welfull-outdoors.en.made-in-china.com/company-Welfull-Group-Co-Ltd-.html)). **Ningbo Eto**, the 300-unit fallback, has one US shipment in its history, 63 cartons of lanterns in 2018, and cites "over 60 suppliers" ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/ningbo-eto-outdoor-supplies-co-ltd)); **Yongkang Dashing "Factory"** has five employees and 36 supply-chain partners ([MIC](https://dashingleisure.en.made-in-china.com/company-Yongkang-Dashing-Leisure-Products-Factory.html)). The real camp-furniture makers sit mostly in the industrial zones of Jinhua prefecture, with others in Changzhou and Henan, and ship big-box brands: **Goleader** in Jinhua, with 1,308 containers to GCI Outdoor plus Dick's, Tractor Supply and Camping World; **Dongyang Sopop**, with 1,379 to Coleman; **Lanxi Trueyach** and **Changzhou Qiaoyu**; and **Henan Hengfeng Top Leisure** of Pingyu County, Henan, the only one shown shipping an aluminium camp table, Camp Chef's 48-inch model ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/goleader-zhejiang-industries-co); [ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/camp-chef)). Each is a **probable factory on one customs item, and none is shown making the windscreen-and-cupboard kitchen**. The product needs no extrusion press, only tube cutting, riveting, coating and sewing, so it suits small workshops: **Yongkang Lexiang** holds a folding-table patent and an SGS-checked 1688 profile listing 26 staff, and accepts custom orders of 300 and private-label orders of 50, but its profile says it does **not accept foreign-trade orders**, although a badge on the same page says it does ([1688](https://www.1688.com/factory/b2b-1726331428.html); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN219047694U/zh)).

So a 300–500-unit first order is realistic only through an intermediary that can handle export, and the price evidence says the earlier input was too low. Chinese domestic sellers list comparable cupboard kitchens at **¥250–358 (about US$37–54)**, above Welfull's export offer ([1688](https://www.1688.com/market/-BBA7CDE2B3F8B7BFD7C0.html)), and the trader tiers that look like real prices cluster higher, with General Union at US$41 for 100–1,999 units ([MIC](https://mugeneralunion.en.made-in-china.com/product/qmoretMWaSRn/China-Outdoor-Portable-Aluminum-Kitchen-Station-Folding-Camping-Table-with-Cupboard-Cabinet.html)) and Dashing at US$34.71–45.56 ([MIC](https://dashingleisure.en.made-in-china.com/product/DriYToZJYaks/China-Outdoor-Aluminum-Lightweight-Collapsible-Portable-Kitchen-Table-for-Camping-Picnic.html)). A full-spec 146 cm, three-cupboard unit should therefore be planned at **US$30–45 FOB**, with Welfull's figure a best case to test against a sample. Eto's 146 cm and 120 cm kitchens ship in identical 80×57×12 cm cartons ([MIC](https://eto-outdoors.en.made-in-china.com/product/KmupNtEHOCcT/China-Kitchen-Table-with-Windshield-and-Storage-Organizer-Portable-Folding-Adjustable-Cooking-Table-for-BBQ-Picnic-Camp-Cupboard.html)), and 1688 sellers copy "Amazon best-seller" titles word for word, which together point to a few shared source ranges. The Australian benchmark is not even the same product: Super Retail Group's Wanderer Dual Cupboard Kitchen has a powder-coated steel frame and a steel windshield ([BCF](https://www.bcf.com.au/p/wanderer-premium-series-dual-cupboard-kitchen/520333.html)). No cupboard-kitchen factory, design origin or firm price was established.

## Factory prices lift the cubby and sink the desk and camp kitchen

Each product was re-run on its own earlier cost model and the shared base, changing only the inputs the factory evidence changes. The shared base converts at **US$1 = A$1.4376 (AUD/USD 0.6956)** ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/csv/f11.1-data.csv)); charges door-delivered freight of **A$5,908 per 20ft container (A$211 × 28 m³), A$10,064 per 40ft high-cube (A$148 × 68 m³), and A$244 per weight-or-measure tonne plus A$900 for LCL**; applies 0% duty with a ChAFTA certificate of origin ([DFAT](https://www.dfat.gov.au/trade/agreements/in-force/chafta/doing-business-with-china/chafta-certificates-of-origin)); adds an A$400 inspection per factory order, a 3% defect allowance and the earlier product-specific test, compliance, biosecurity and storage lines; and counts net revenue as the sale price ÷ 1.1 to strip out GST. Gross profit (GP) is net revenue minus landed cost, and GP% is a share of net revenue. Freight rates are model assumptions rather than quotes, testing costs are estimates, and all factory prices are list prices. The bumper-plate baseline moves by A$0.27 because the earlier file used A$5,964 per 20ft rather than the shared A$5,908.

**The trampoline and the plates hold, and the cubby improves.** The trampoline's A$97 stands, but it now rests on a named factory's published terms rather than a listing of unknown origin. Its real exposure is the specification: if Australian buyers expect Everfit's heavier build and Lingsha prices it at a brand-grade US$110–120, GP falls to A$62–76, and the trader route at US$119 would leave A$63; breakeven stays at A$242 a unit, well under Kmart's A$299 for a smaller 12 ft springless model ([Kmart](https://www.kmart.com.au/product/12-foot-springless-trampoline-42836155/)). Bumper plates gain most if a moulder matches the trader's 10-tonne price tier, since every US$0.10/kg is worth A$14.80 a set; losing the Tengtai price costs nothing in the base case, because the earlier report's A$124 upside rested on a consolidator's list price that no moulder has offered. The cubby is the only product where the real factory is both identifiable and cheaper than the trader the earlier ranking used. Switching from Dreamgo to Zhongran adds A$18–23 a unit, taking GP to A$92–96 (34–35%), at the cost of a 300-unit, 40ft order worth about A$54,000 landed and 54 m³ of stock to store. Its breakeven falls to A$198, so it still earns A$46 a unit (20%) if it has to be cut to A$249 to answer Kmart's own A$169 cubby ([Kmart](https://www.kmart.com.au/product/activo-lets-go-play-wooden-cubby-house-with-kitchen-43271481/)).

**The hammock, desk and camp kitchen weaken.** The hammock's A$29 survives only because the trader's price is low; no factory has quoted, and at a factory-type US$25–30 GP falls to A$15–23 on a 200-unit LCL, or A$25–32 on a 457-unit 20ft container. The desk is the clearest case of the real maker being the wrong supplier. At A$299, buying direct from Jiecang loses A$40 a desk on 100 units and A$3 on 274; it breaks even only near US$157 at 274 units, and earns A$52 (19%) on a 571-desk 40ft container at its US$129 tier, which means about A$125,000 of stock. Even if Jiecang's SAA paperwork cut compliance costs to the earlier low case of A$1,740, a 100-desk order would still lose A$24. The assembler route keeps the earlier A$47 (17%) at US$100 on 100 desks, A$62 (23%) at US$90, and A$84–99 on 274 desks in a 20ft box. The camp kitchen falls hardest, from A$28 (31.5%) at Welfull's price to A$5–20 at US$35–45, and needs a quote of **US$33.40 or less** to clear 25%. Buying domestically from a Yongkang workshop at ¥250 would land near US$39–41 once a 1688 sourcing agent's usual 5–10% commission is added ([Sino-Shipping](https://www.sino-shipping.com/sv/inkop-upphandling-kina/hitta-leverantorer/basta-1688-agenter/)), earning A$11–14, and the 300-unit LCL fallback at US$35 earns A$15.

## Three hours of free checks and one audit day settle factory status

The cheapest reliable method, tested hands-on this round, takes about two to three hours of desk work per supplier at no cost, plus one to three days waiting for documents, and a US$210–300 audit day before the first container closes the remaining gap. Order matters: run the free steps that are hardest to fake first, and pay only for the last.

| Step | Time and cost | What proves factory status | What misleads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customs footprint on ImportGenius and Panjiva public pages, and ImportYeti in a browser | 20–30 min, free | Direct shipments to named brands over two years or more, from an industrial address, with goods from one production technology, and with name and address both matching your contract partner | Shipment volume alone: Welfull has more than 4,000 shipments and is still a trader. Name collisions: "Ningbo Xusheng Group" is an auto-parts exporter. Shipment counts differ between data sites |
| Platform "verified" blocks, checked on the auditor's portal | 15 min, free | A production block marked "verified by" an auditor, with the report found on TÜV's portal and an audit dated within about 18 months | Validity always reads 9999-12-31, and the portal shows no address or scope |
| Patents, searched in a browser on Google Patents or CNIPA, China's patent office | 20–30 min, free | A design or utility patent on your SKU, held by your contract partner and filed before the SKU was first listed | Since September 2024, patents on 1688 profiles are uploaded by the supplier; traders also hold design patents |
| Registry: GSXT, China's official company register, after real-name sign-up, or the free tiers of the private databases Qichacha and Tianyancha | 20–30 min, free | Business scope listing production (生产, 制造, 加工); registration at county level in the cluster; a credible insured headcount; sister import–export companies | A scope listing only sales and import–export means a trader; production wording alone can be registered without a plant |
| Three documents from the supplier | 1–3 days, plus about 30 min of review, free | A business licence whose QR code resolves on GSXT and whose name matches the contract and bank account; a redacted export declaration for the same model naming the factory as producer; a VAT invoice in the factory's own name, checked on the national invoice-check site | A trader that will not name its producer is useful only as a price benchmark |
| Live video walk of your SKU's production line | 45–60 min, free | The factory gate with a map pin, the licence on the wall, the specific line running, a mould or jig, and a request carried out on the spot | Pre-recorded footage and generic workshop photos |
| Audit at the factory address, then pre-shipment inspection | One man-day at US$210–300; inspection US$200–350 per man-day | An auditor sighting the licence, social-insurance payment list, equipment register and utility bills | Auditing the office instead of the plant |

These steps rest on sources that were reachable or quoted this round. ImportGenius and Panjiva public pages returned shipper addresses, top consignees and recent bills without a login, and ImportGenius sells fuller US data for US$229 ([ImportGenius](https://www.importgenius.com/suppliers/nantong-tengtai-sporting)). TÜV Rheinland's portal confirmed audit dates for Lingsha, Jiecang and Xusheng ([TÜV](https://www.verified.chn.tuv.com/en/)). GSXT blocked cloud IPs and asked for real-name registration ([GSXT](https://www.gsxt.gov.cn/)), so registry checks must be done by hand from Australia. 1688's own guidance says patent badges have been self-uploaded since September 2024 and advises checking that the licence address matches the production site ([1688](https://wiki.1688.com/zh/WKfizlowuxxnuo)); the licence name, unified social credit code, legal representative and bank-account name must match character for character ([NewBuyingAgent](https://www.newbuyingagent.com/resources/how-to-read-a-chinese-business-license-and-verify-a-supplier)); the national VAT-invoice platform is publicly reachable ([State Taxation Administration](https://inv-veri.chinatax.gov.cn/)); and audits cost US$210–290 per man-day, with inspections at US$200–350 ([Supplyia](https://www.supplyia.com/inspection-company-in-china/)). Two caveats apply: the export-declaration field names (境内发货人, 生产销售单位) come from background knowledge and should be confirmed with a customs broker, and customs data cover US imports only, so a genuine factory that sells only to Europe or Australia will look invisible.

The contract controls cost nothing, and they would have caught every misclassification above. Pay the 30% deposit only to the company whose licence matches the contract and that will appear as shipper on the bill of lading; if an export arm invoices, as Tengtai's T&T or Jiecang's 新昌县捷昌进出口 does, get a written statement naming the producing factory and inspect there; and make the 70% balance due only after an approved inspection, as Harris Sliwoski advises ([Harris Sliwoski](https://harris-sliwoski.com/chinalawblog/china-manufacturing-payment-terms/)). A five-supplier shortlist costs about 10–15 hours of desk time and one US$250–300 audit.

## Conclusion

The challenge changes the question from "who is the factory?" to "which tier of factory will serve you?" Every product has an identifiable maker, but the tiers behave differently. The brand-factory tier that supplies Vuly, Springfree, Rogue and UPLIFT, namely Jinhuaxing, Sheng Hui, Sportsoul, Kaidi and Goleader, is closed to a 128-unit buyer by minimum orders, credit terms or channel conflict; the second tier of factories with their own export rights, such as Lingsha, Zhongran and the Shandong and Hebei plate moulders, is open; and small domestic workshops such as Lexiang are open on quantity but closed on export paperwork. Buying from the factory is therefore a decision about specification and accountability more than about price. It saved 19% on the trampoline and 13–16% on the cubby, saved nothing visible on plates and hammocks, and cost money on desks, but in every case it buys a named party responsible for tube gauge, weld quality, test reports and the biosecurity certificate, and the right to inspect where the goods are made.

The ranking at the top survives for different reasons than before, and the next dollars should buy proof rather than stock. A GSXT check on Lingsha and Zhongran would lift both from probable to confirmed for almost nothing, three like-for-like quotes from the moulders would settle the bumper-plate margin, and a Lingsha sample with an AS 4989 lab quote would show whether its 62 kg build can sell against Everfit's 74 kg. One rule would have caught every misclassification in our earlier list: pay a deposit only to the company that will appear as shipper on the bill of lading and as producer on the export declaration.
